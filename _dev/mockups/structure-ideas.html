The number that matters here. At a $0 salary the S-corp looks +$21,233 better. At the $101,000 benchmark it is +$4,309 better. At the aggressive $71,000 it is +$9,561. The current design shows the first of those three with a green "higher take-home" badge — the one figure that is not a real option. Every direction below is really a different answer to how do we stop doing that.

Direction B

Verdict first, working hidden

Most people want one sentence: is this worth doing or not. Lead with the answer in plain language, put every number behind a disclosure.

Affects every tab

Should you incorporate?

Probably not yet — the gain is real but thin.

At your profit of $202,800, a Professional Corp paying the benchmark $101,000 salary would leave you about $4,309 a year better off.

Against that: roughly $1,200 a year in payroll administration, a separate corporate return, annual filings, and the $800 CA minimum franchise tax you owe whether or not you profit. The margin is thin enough that it turns on how much your accountant charges.

What would change this answer

The gap widens as profit rises, because more of it can move from salary to distribution. At roughly $260,000 of profit the same benchmark salary produces about double this gain. It narrows if you take a defensibly higher salary.

Solves: the reader never has to assemble the answer themselves, and there is only one claim on screen. Also the only direction here that names the admin cost as part of the verdict.
Costs: it takes a position. The tool becomes advisory rather than a calculator, which is a different liability posture — worth a "not personalised advice" line right in the box.
Direction C

The salary slider is the whole answer

The honest insight is that there is no single S-corp number — the answer is a function of the salary you can defend. So make the salary the interaction, and let the comparison be a readout of it.

Direction D

Decision ladder, analysis gated

Closest to what you suggested — nothing S-corp appears until you have cleared the questions that make it a real option. The gate is qualification, not a button for its own sake.

Affects every tab

As an LMFT you can form a Marriage and Family Therapy Corporation. An LLC is not available to you.

3

Needed before any figure below is real. The benchmark is $101,000 — 50% of your net profit.

Solves: a $0 salary can never produce a number at all, and the CA eligibility point gets told at the moment it is relevant rather than buried further down.
Costs: friction on every visit unless the cleared steps collapse once satisfied. Also the most work to build of the four.
