Three ways to build a Mohel Simulator

Same idea as the Therapy Practice Simulator, same guided single-page shape, pointed at a very different job. All three run the identical calculation engine — move a slider in one and the math is the same math in the others. What differs is entirely the skin, the metaphor, and how the character is drawn.

How to judge them. Open each and ask two things: does the aesthetic make the numbers more legible or less, and does the joke hold up on the second visit? Direction A is the loudest and wears out fastest. Direction C is the slowest to read and the only one that could plausibly ship to a real audience of mohelim.
A

Bris Tycoon

Straight arcade. Reuses the pixel type, coin gold and XP bar already built in tycoon.html. Level titles run Apprentice → Legendary. Loudest of the three, and the one that most obviously reads as a game rather than a tool.

Play the month

The pages above are the planner. These are the game — a real, playable 28 days. Calls come in, each locked to its eighth day. You accept or you pass. One bris per morning, so two on the same date is a choice, not a double-booking. Shabbat mornings you can only reach families you can walk to. Jaundice postpones dates you'd already committed to. And sometimes the father takes you aside in the doorway to say he can't pay, with everyone already seated in the next room.

All three run the same engine and the same seed, so they open on an identical month — the only difference is how it looks and what it calls things.

What the game actually asks of you

MechanicWhy it's there
The eighth day is fixedYou never schedule anything. Every decision is which room you're standing in when a date you didn't pick arrives.
One morning, one brisTwo families whose sons were born the same day is the core conflict. Taking one means calling the other.
Shabbat, no drivingRoughly one date in seven. Walkable families only. Turns your address into a strategy.
JaundicePostpones a bris you'd already blocked out — onto a date that may now be taken. Punishes a tightly packed month.
"He can't pay"The one real moral choice, and the one place the game stops being about money. Doing it free is the highest-standing move available.
Standing, not starsReputation drives how many families call you at all. Declining costs it. Word-of-mouth is the only marketing channel a mohel has.

What's deliberately not here yet

  • Real malpractice quotes. The default is a placeholder. Physician malpractice varies enormously by state and specialty and I couldn't find a defensible mohel-specific figure.
  • Metro-level Jewish birth counts. Market share is computed against a user-entered number rather than a lookup table, because reliable metro-level Jewish birth data isn't published.
  • Income tax. Take-home stops after self-employment tax, and says so. Porting the therapy simulator's full bracket engine is a later step, not a mock-up step.
  • Commissioned character art. All three portraits are hand-built SVG so they cost nothing and stay editable. If one direction wins, that's the slot where real illustration goes.
