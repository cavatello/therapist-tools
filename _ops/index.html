The only source that can say what a county actually paid rather than what it advertised, and nobody has done it for this audience. Spread is the story: 2.8× between the top and bottom county for comparable work.

The pre-licensed row, and its limit

Exactly one county publishes an explicitly pre-licensed clinical title: San Bernardino, “Clinical Therapist Pre-License”, 175 people, $71,510–$91,270.

Its licensed equivalent — 524 people across San Bernardino and Riverside — runs $73,528–$104,682.

About $13,400 of licensure premium at the top of the range. One county, and the page will say so rather than generalize.

Shipped — every link live

  • MBH-SLRP explained: the $180,000 California pays associate therapists
    32 direct-care hours a week, one lump payment, breach means repaying the whole award within a year, and cycle 1 was 3.7× oversubscribed — $331m requested against $90.1m available.
  • Medi-Cal safety-net employers in California: the health centers, named
    57 county behavioral health plans and 218 health center organizations, every link fetched before publishing. 23 of the state’s own links no longer point where they say.
  • Loan forgiveness for California therapists: which employers qualify
    Four programs, three different tests. PSLF ignores your license entirely, so a county associate is likely accruing qualifying payments right now.
  • Getting hired as a California associate: why half your applications never reply
    “Nobody will hire me” is a billing rule, not an hour count. Medi-Cal names associates as billable staff; commercial payers do not.
  • Out-of-state to California licensure: Path A, Path B, and your hours
    Path A against Path B across all three licenses, and the correction that continuing education cannot fix a transcript.
  • Therapists by state: what the license is called, and how many there are
    Per-capita workforce comparison with the license-title breakdown for every state in it.
  • Therapists by county in California: all 165,000, from the register
    All 58 counties, built from the state’s monthly licensee register.
