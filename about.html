Tools, reference and support for California MFTs.

Therapist Support is a free collection of practical things for California-licensed therapists — MFTs, LCSWs, LPCCs and psychologists. Calculators, rate research and plain-English notes on the parts of the job nobody trained you for. Built by a therapist. No account, nothing to buy.

The support part

Because most of this is learned from each other

Nobody teaches the business of a practice in graduate school. What most therapists know about rates, panels and paperwork they learned from a colleague in a hallway. This is an attempt to write some of that down.

Share what you know

Send a rate, a correction, or a thing that caught you out. It goes into the next update — credited or anonymous, your call.

Say when it is wrong

Rules change and figures go stale. Every message is read by a person, and corrections get made and dated.

Ask for what is missing

Most of what is here started as someone asking whether a thing existed. If you need a tool that is not here, say so.

How these pages are checked

Every page carries a badge saying how deeply it has been verified. The point of publishing it is that this site is not uniform, and pretending otherwise would let the thinnest pages borrow credibility from the strongest ones.

Verified to source

Every figure was re-checked against the statute, schedule or filing it cites. These pages cite three or more primary sources — the Board, the IRS, CMS, the Legislature — rather than somebody’s summary of them.

Figures checked, narrative not re-read

The numbers are current. The argument around them has not been reviewed since it was written.

Published sources only

Built from what an institution publishes about itself. Nothing on the page is independently verified, and where the institution publishes nothing the page says so rather than filling the gap.

Known gap

Something on the page could not be established. It is named, in full, under “What I could not establish”.

The badge is derived, not asserted: it is computed from what a page actually cites and whether it carries a gaps list, so it cannot drift away from the page it describes.
