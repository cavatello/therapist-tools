Settled, and worth knowing before you shop

The Board accepts an electronic signature. It does not accept a typed name.

Two of these products are sold largely on supervisor e-signature, so the first question is whether the Board will take one. It will, and it says so in three separate places — which is more than most things about supervision documentation can claim.

Where it says soWhat it says
FAQs for Supervisors, marked new February 2026, question 37“Signed documents may be original, scanned, or have an electronic signature.” The same document adds that supervisees are not permitted to sign the weekly logs for the supervisor.
In-State Experience Verification forms — 37A-301 (MFT), 37A-201 (LCSW), 37A-675 (LPCC)Printed immediately above the supervisor’s line: “ORIGINAL OR ELECTRONIC SIGNATURE REQUIRED”. The LCSW form adds “scanned”.
The license applications themselvesThe LMFT application asks for a wet signature or an electronic one, and says an electronic signature is accepted if completed through a platform “such as Adobe Sign or DocuSign which ensures security and authenticity”.
The Weekly Log forms — 37A-525, 37A-638No signature instruction at all. A bare “Supervisor Signature” line, unchanged since 2022. For these two forms the general FAQ answer is the only published guidance there is.

The line the Board draws is between a signature and a typed name. CAMFT reported in 2022 that the Board would keep accepting electronic signatures on supervision documents but would not accept a form with the supervisor’s name simply typed in — which is the distinction every product below is either on the right side of or silent about.

The part nobody writes up

You are choosing a custodian, not an app.

A tracker holds the only continuous record of 3,000 hours accumulated over at least 104 weeks. If it stops existing in year three, the reconstruction falls to you and to supervisors who may have moved on. That makes “will this company still be here” a more important question than any feature in the table above, and it is the question none of them answers.

What was and was not found, 10 August 2026

4 of 5

One of the five publishes a legal entity, a street address and a telephone number. Four publish an email address and nothing else, and three of those four return a 404 on their own /about page.

Three of the five domains were registered in 2026 — January, March and April. One dates from 2024 and one from 2006.

What could not be established at all: whether any of the four is a registered business anywhere, who runs them, or whether the cryptographic claims two of them make are implemented as described. Those are printed as unestablished rather than guessed at.

What to do about it, whichever you pick

  • Export on a schedule. Monthly, to a PDF you keep yourself. Every one of these can produce a file; the point is that the file lives somewhere the vendor does not control.
  • Get the verification signed as you go, not at the end. A supervisor who has already signed cannot become unreachable.
  • Keep the weekly logs even after they are totalled. The Board can ask, and the total is not the evidence — the logs are.
  • Check the export actually reproduces the form. A tracker that fills a board PDF is only useful if the PDF it fills is the current revision of the right form.
  • Do not let the tracker be the only place your hours exist. That is the whole of the advice, and it costs nothing.

A common and expensive assumption

Your practice software is not doing this.

Both of the practice-management systems most California associates work in have a supervision feature, and neither of them tracks hours toward licensure. They do countersignature of clinical notes, which is a different thing that happens to involve the same two people.

What it doesWhat it does not do
SimplePractice — designate a supervisor and supervisee, review and sign documentation, bill under supervision.No log of hours by Board category, no running total against the 3,000, no 104-week test, no cap on the non-clinical categories, no board form.
TherapyNotes — note review and co-signature, and incident-to billing.The same absence. Neither product publishes an hours-tracking feature at all.

Which is why a separate tracker exists as a category. If you are choosing between the two systems themselves, that comparison is a different page.

No winner, on purpose

What would actually decide it.

1If continuity is what worries you

Weight the vendor that publishes an entity, an address and a telephone number, and whose domain predates the category by eighteen years. That is Track Your Hours, and it is also the one that does not advertise supervisor e-signature at all — so the trade is explicit.

2If the signature workflow is what worries you

Two products capture a supervisor signature and say so in detail, and one of those names UETA and the federal ESIGN Act. Read the distinction in the table carefully: one product sends a form and leaves the signature field blank, which is a delivery feature described in signature language.

3If price is what worries you

The spread is $75 a year across the whole category, and one product has a genuinely free tier. Over a two-year associateship the most expensive option costs about $150 more than the cheapest. That is not nothing, and it is much less than one unbilled hour spent reconstructing a lost month.

4What should not decide it

The number of states a product claims to support. Every one of these is a California decision made by a California registrant, and “all fifty states” on a landing page is a statement about ambition rather than about the form your supervisor has to sign.

Nothing on this page is a recommendation and none of these links is an affiliate link.
