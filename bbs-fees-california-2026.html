California · Board of Behavioral Sciences

BBS fees halved in July. The route now costs $575 to $875.

Every Board fee halved on 1 July 2026. What the whole route costs still depends on one thing nobody writes down — how many years you spend as an associate. Here is the arithmetic, and the reversion date that ends it.

What changed

If you have looked up the cost of getting licensed in California in the past few weeks, there is a fair chance you were quoted a number that is no longer true. The Board of Behavioral Sciences reduced almost every fee it charges, effective 1 July 2026. The reduction is temporary and runs to 30 June 2030.[1]

Now the part every version of this article gets wrong, including an earlier version of this one. There is no single number, because the fees you pay once are not the whole bill. Five of them you pay exactly once:

Paid onceAmount
Registration application$75
California Law & Ethics exam$75
Licence application$125
LMFT clinical exam$125
Initial licence issuance$100
Subtotal$500

Your registration then runs for one year from the last day of the month it was issued — that first year is already inside the $75 you just paid. Every year after it is another $75, and you may renew five times before the registration cancels at six years.[3] So the answer is a line, not a point:

Time as an associateRenewalsTotal Board fees
Under a year0$500
1–2 years — the 104-week statutory minimum1$575
2–3 years2$650
3–4 years3$725
4–5 years4$800
5–6 years — the statutory ceiling5$875

The 3,000 hours cannot be completed in under 104 weeks, so $575 is the floor for anyone who finishes as fast as the law allows. $875 is the ceiling, and it is what you pay if you use the full six years. Most people land between. Every one of those totals is exactly half of what the same route cost before July.

$650two renewals — the middle of the range, and the most common answer

Two things the headline number leaves out

First, a $20 Mental Health Practitioner Education Fund fee is not part of the reduction. It attaches to licence renewal-related applications, which means it touches nothing on the road from registration to your first licence — but it does make a real LMFT biennial renewal $120, not $100.[1]

Second, this is temporary. On 1 July 2030 the old schedule returns. If you are budgeting a licensure timeline that runs past that date, the back half costs double the front half.

And the law and ethics exam fee can recur: associates must take it annually until they pass in order to renew a registration, so the $75 is not necessarily a one-off.[2]

The Board contradicts itself, and that is worth knowing

If you go looking for these numbers you will find two different answers on bbs.ca.gov. The fee reduction FAQ carries the reduced schedule. The Board's own Manage License/Registration page still displays the pre-reduction table, with $150 annual renewals and $220 active renewals.

We cite the FAQ, because it is the more specific and more recent document and it states its own effective dates. But if you land on the renewal page first and see the old figures, that is why. Do not assume you have been quoted wrongly.

What is not included at all

Board fees are a small part of what pre-licensure actually costs. Not in any of the numbers above:

  • Supervision, if you pay for it. Group private practice often bills it back at roughly $450 a month, which is about $5,400 a year out of your take-home — several times the entire Board fee for the year.
  • Professional liability insurance.
  • Continuing education. Associates need 3 hours of California law and ethics each one-year renewal period.
  • Fingerprinting and duplicate documents, which the FAQ excludes from the reduction explicitly. If you are applying from outside California you also pay the Board a $49 hard-card fingerprint fee, which was not reduced.
  • The clinical exam for LCSWs and LPCCs, which is administered by ASWB and NBCC respectively and priced by them, not by the Board.

One more thing worth knowing before you budget: the clinical exam fee is not billed to you later. It is collected with the licence application, as a single $250 payment, so that is one bill and not two.

The fee cut is real and it is welcome. It is also, for most associates, a smaller line than one month of supervision.
