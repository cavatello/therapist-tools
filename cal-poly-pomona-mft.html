What this programme actually is

To answer the question directly, because our records left it open: an LMFT-qualifying option at Cal Poly Pomona does exist, and it is not a subplan, concentration or option hidden inside a larger degree — it is the entire degree. The M.S. in Psychology is the MFT program. The department states the program is "designed to prepare you for the Marriage and Family Therapy (MFT) license in the State of California" and "meets all the requirements set forth by the BBS." The current catalog entry is titled, literally, "Psychology, M.S.: 60 units," and every one of its 60 units is clinical MFT preparation; there is no research track, no I/O option, no non-clinical exit. The BBS education-resources table lists California State Polytechnic University, Pomona for LMFT and nothing else — no LCSW, no LPCC, no LEP. That single-licence listing is not a gap in the program's record; it is an accurate description of a program that does one thing. If you want LPCC eligibility, this is not the place to get it, and there is no published bridge.

The thing that most distinguishes it is scale, or the lack of it. The program admits "a maximum of 16 students each year" — against roughly 200 applicants by the program's own FAQ, roughly 300 by the department's — which makes it one of the smallest and most selective MFT-qualifying cohorts at any California public university, at a resident price of about $10,300 a year. It is full-time only and non-negotiable about it: "Our program is designed to be full-time for 2 years and classes are only offered once a year." There is no part-time route, no evening cohort, no online option. Miss a course and you wait a year.

The clinical spine has an unusual and quite deliberate design: four separate "Diagnosis and Treatment of…" courses, each pairing a population with its associated abuse or violence mandate — PSY 6605 Diagnosis and Treatment of the Family/Family Violence, PSY 6606 Diagnosis and Treatment of Couples and Spousal Abuse, PSY 6607 Diagnosis and Treatment of Children and Child Abuse, plus PSY 6610 Substance Use Problems. Most California programs teach a family-therapy survey and then bolt on standalone partner-abuse and child-abuse-reporting modules to satisfy BBS. Pomona instead teaches the mandated content inside the clinical course for the population it concerns. Add PSY 5565 Advanced Testing and Professional Issues with a separate one-unit laboratory (PSY 5565L) — assessment taught with a hands-on lab component is rare in an MFT curriculum — and the shape of the program is clear: diagnosis-forward, assessment-literate, population-by-population. The culminating experience is a comprehensive exam, and preparing for it is itself coursework: PSY 6870 is a one-unit prep course taken twice, then PSY 6970 is the exam.

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

PSY 66063 units

Diagnosis and Treatment of Couples and Spousal Abuse

Why it is worth noticingThe course title is the argument. Pomona does not teach couples therapy in one course and intimate-partner-violence assessment in another; it fuses them, so the BBS-mandated spousal-abuse content arrives inside the clinical course on couples rather than as a compliance add-on. Alongside PSY 6605 (family/family violence) and PSY 6607 (children/child abuse), it is the most identifiable design decision in this curriculum. No catalog description text could be retrieved — see gaps.

PSY 66073 units

Diagnosis and Treatment of Children and Child Abuse

Why it is worth noticingSame pattern applied to child work: child therapy and the child-abuse assessment and reporting requirement are one three-unit course. Practically, that means a full term on children rather than the handful of weeks many 60-unit programs allocate. No catalog description text could be retrieved.

PSY 5565 / 5565L3 + 1 units

Advanced Testing and Professional Issues, with Laboratory

Why it is worth noticingA required assessment course with its own separate one-unit laboratory. Formal psychological testing is usually thin in MFT-track curricula — often a single survey course, sometimes none — so a lecture-plus-lab structure is a genuine differentiator and reflects the program's home in a psychology department rather than a counselor-education one. No catalog description text could be retrieved.

PSY 55753 units

Multicultural and Gender Issues in Therapy

The department describes multicultural competency as integrated throughout the coursework, "including dedicated course PSY 5575 (Cross-Cultural & Gender Issues in Therapy & Recovery Model)," and cites a "strong commitment to diversity, equity, and inclusion with diverse faculty representation." Note the department page and the catalog use slightly different titles for the same course.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe department's own phrasing attaches the Recovery Model to this course, which is a specific commitment — recovery-model practice is the framing used across California's public behavioral health system, and Pomona's practicum sites sit in exactly that system across the Pomona Valley and Inland Empire.

PSY 6620 / 66214 + 4 units

Supervised Practice and Advanced Supervised Practice

Why it is worth noticingThe eight units that carry the second year, when students are at their sites 20 hours a week. Together with PSY 5581/5583/5584 (Practicum I, III, IV, 2 units each), 14 of the 60 units are supervised practice — a bit under a quarter of the degree. How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You find your own site

You are responsible for securing a placement. Most programmes are like this and most students manage it, but a student who cannot find a site cannot graduate - and that risk is highest if you are rural, online, or need Spanish-language or specialty hours.

StartsAn introductory practicum in the spring semester of year one; the substantive placement runs through all of year two
Hours requiredYear two is a half-time placement — "students are at their practicum sites half-time (or 20 hours per week)". No total client-contact hour requirement is published.

Placement is student-driven from a vetted list, and the department is explicit about the division of labour: "Our program provides a list of approved practicum sites. Students are then responsible for contacting these sites and going through their application process." That means the search and the interviews are yours, in the spring of year one, while you are carrying a full course load and an introductory practicum. The department notes that "most practicum sites on our approved list afford students the opportunity to gain clinical experience working with diverse and underserved communities," which in this catchment means the Pomona Valley and Inland Empire county and community-agency system. In year two the commitment is 20 hours a week for two semesters, carried in PSY 6620 Supervised Practice and PSY 6621 Advanced Supervised Practice (4 units each) on top of PSY 5581/5583/5584. We found no evidence of an in-house training clinic — no clinic is named on the department's program pages, and a third-party program profile likewise reports that whether one exists is not publicly specified — so plan on the assumption that all client contact happens at outside agencies. The program also does not publish a minimum number of direct client hours, a relational (couple/family) sub-minimum, or supervision ratios; those are set by the site and by BBS trainee rules rather than by a published program standard. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 53 of the 65 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No catalog course descriptions could be retrieved. Cal Poly Pomona's Acalog catalog serves program pages inconsistently from this environment and blocks its course-list and course-detail pages entirely, so the signature courses above carry verified codes, titles and unit values from the current catalog program page but no description text. Titles are accurate; do not infer content beyond them.
  • The catalog's required list runs PSY 5581 Practicum I, PSY 5583 Practicum III and PSY 5584 Practicum IV, with no Practicum II. The listed courses sum to exactly 60 units, which suggests the list is complete rather than truncated, but we could not confirm whether a PSY 5582 exists as an unrequired or renumbered course.
  • No in-house training clinic could be identified, and no page states that one does not exist either. Worth asking directly, since it determines whether your first client contact is program-supervised or agency-supervised.
  • No published direct-client-hour minimum, relational/couple/family hour sub-minimum, or supervision ratio. The program publishes only the 20-hours-per-week year-two commitment.
  • The program does not publish graduation rates, licensure exam pass rates or employment outcomes — a real contrast with Sonoma State, which publishes all three.
  • The two Cal Poly Pomona FAQ pages disagree on both the applicant pool (200 vs 300) and the application deadline (February 15 vs February 1). Both are official university pages.
  • No credible first-person student discussion was found; Reddit search was unavailable from this environment.
  • No freely-licensed campus photograph could be verified — Wikimedia Commons was unreachable from this environment.
