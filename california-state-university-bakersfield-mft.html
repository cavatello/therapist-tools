What this programme actually is

OrientationExperiential and person-of-the-therapist centred — self-awareness, family-of-origin work, live clients and video review — over a broadly integrative theoretical base (behavioral, cognitive, humanistic/experiential, psychodynamic)

The number that reframes everything about this program: it is three years, not two. CSUB's M.S. in Counseling Psychology is "the three-year, 66-semester unit Master of Science in Counseling Psychology Program," and the extra year is not padding — it buys a clean separation between an in-house clinic year and a community-agency year. Year one is coursework only; you cannot touch a client until you have passed all six first-year courses (CPSY 6110, 6120, 6130, 6140, 6310, 6360) and been formally advanced to Graduate Candidacy. Year two is the University Counselor Training Clinic, three afternoons a week on campus. Year three is a community traineeship at 20 hours a week, weekdays, business hours. Almost no other California MFT-track program stages it that way; most compress clinic and field placement into overlapping semesters or skip the in-house stage entirely.

The second distinctive commitment is experiential, and the catalog is unusually blunt about it: "Enrollment in the program implies student consent to engage in individual and group experiential learning activities in various courses." Nine named courses are designated clinical process classes that "are experiential in nature and require the use of live clients and video recording of sessions," and the catalog warns the timetable will show more contact hours than the units imply because of the time needed "to move people from room to room and to change digital recordings." CPSY 6410, Theories of Family Counseling, requires that "students will relate their own family dynamics to the literature" — a family-of-origin assignment written into the course description. The department's own program page says faculty "use experiential learning to teach play therapy & psychodrama." There is also a required 1-unit course, CPSY 5772, on compassion fatigue and vicarious trauma, and another required 1-unit orientation course that must be taken in your first semester.

It is the southern Central Valley's program, and that shapes both the clinic and the price. The University Counselor Training Clinic in Education Building Room 200 charges $25 for a first session and $15 after that, and its student managers say plainly that "we don't turn anybody away for lack of payment"; it has run at roughly 80 to 100 clients at a time, offering individual, couple, family and group counseling to Bakersfield residents (not CSUB students, to avoid dual relationships), with every session videotaped and supervised by an LMFT. Practicum sections are capped at six students. The trade-off is scheduling: classes meet between 1 p.m. and 10 p.m., Monday through Thursday, and the program says outright that anyone employed full-time "will likely need to modify their work schedule, especially during years two and three." It is full-time, in-person, cohort-based, fall entry only, and there is no part-time or online route.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

CPSY 64103 units

Theories of Family Counseling

"Focus on major systemic and nonsystemic orientations in relationship, couples, and family counseling. Attention to the psychodynamics of relationships and transcultural family systems, communications theory, and the role of family of origin in individual and family functioning. Students will relate their own family dynamics to the literature."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThat last sentence is the program in miniature — a required family-of-origin self-application assignment written into the catalog description, not left to an instructor's discretion. It is also the gateway course: CPSY 6280 (Couple and Sexuality) and CPSY 6460 (Techniques of Group and Family) both list it as a prerequisite.

CPSY 62803 units

Couple and Sexuality Counseling

"Emphasis on the dynamics of couple interaction through the adult lifespan, including such issues as gender, ethnicity, health discordance, desire discrepancy, monogamy, and fantasies. Special attention given to the relationship between issues of sexuality and intimate interpersonal relationships, as well as therapist dynamics in the therapeutic triad."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingMost CA programs satisfy the BBS human-sexuality requirement with a survey course. This one names desire discrepancy, monogamy and fantasies as content, and then turns the lens on the clinician — "therapist dynamics in the therapeutic triad" is about your own reactivity in the room with a couple. Offered spring of odd years only, so the sequence is fixed.

CPSY 64303 units

Theories and Techniques of Developmental Counseling with Children and Adolescents

"Focus on the major approaches to therapeutic interventions with children and adolescents... Focus on the development of clinical skills necessary for effective preventive, therapeutic and consultative interventions with and related to children and adolescents. Laboratory experiences will be provided through observation, demonstration, and/or practice."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingOne of the nine designated clinical process courses that use live clients and video recording. This is where the program's advertised play-therapy training lives — CSUB's own program page says faculty "use experiential learning to teach play therapy & psychodrama," though the catalog description itself does not name either modality.

CPSY 57721 units

Self-Care for Mental Health Professionals

"Designed to prepare individuals who are entering the counseling profession with information as it pertains to compassion fatigue, vicarious trauma, burnout, and self-care... with an emphasis on trauma work... exploring the various theories and practical approaches to the treatment of compassion fatigue and vicarious trauma."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA standalone required course on therapist burnout, taken at the start of the program rather than tacked onto an ethics syllabus. Almost no California MFT curriculum gives self-care its own course number and unit. Paired with the 1-unit CPSY 5771 Orientation to Counseling, which "must be taken the first semester following matriculation."

CPSY 68102 units

Practicum I

"A supervised clinical practicum within the scope and practice of a Marriage and Family Therapist or Professional Clinical Counselor. Application of counseling procedures, with a focus on assessment, diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment of familial and relationship dysfunctions... Limited to 6 students per section."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe six-student section cap is the concrete supervision-ratio fact prospective students almost never get. Prerequisites are equally telling: advancement to Graduate Candidacy, a background check, professional liability insurance, and successful completion of the entire first year — this is a second-year course, and there is no way to start earlier.

CPSY 64603 units

Techniques of Group and Family Counseling

"Focus on the development of clinical skills necessary for effective group, family, and relationship counseling. Importance is given to process dynamics, leadership responsibilities and expertise, and ethical and cultural considerations. Involvement in experiential activities designed to relate observation, demonstration and practice to theoretical explanations."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is where the degree ends. The Master Comprehensive Examination — the program's culminating activity, which every student must pass to graduate — is "completed at the conclusion of" this course. Embedding the comps inside a live clinical-skills class rather than a standalone seminar is a deliberate and unusual choice.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

The school does not say

Nothing on this school’s published pages states who secures your placement. That is not the same as a bad answer - but it is the first question to ask admissions, and the specificity of the reply tells you a great deal. Thirty of the seventy-eight are in this position.

The school runs its own training clinic.  University Counselor Training Clinic Owning a clinic and holding a seat in it are different things, so this sits beside the model above rather than replacing it.

StartsSecond year — CPSY 6810 Practicum I, which requires advancement to Graduate Candidacy plus "successful completion of first year coursework." No client contact at all in year one.
Hours requiredNo hour totals published. CSUB states the time commitment structurally instead: three weekday afternoons a week in the training clinic across both semesters of year two (the applying page says "three afternoons (1:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m.) per week"; the 2024 program description says 1:00 to 5:00 p.m.), and "at least 20 hours per week during regular business hours (Monday through Friday 8 a.m. to 5 p.m.)" for the community traineeship across both semesters of year three. No minimum client-contact hour figure and no relational (couple/family) sub-minimum is given for either stage. CPSY 6980 Clinical Extension (1–3 units, repeatable, does not count toward the degree) exists specifically for students "requiring more than two semesters to complete the required hours for Practica or for Traineeships," which implies internal hour targets that are not published.
In-house clinicUniversity Counselor Training Clinic (UCTC), Education Building Room 200, CSUB campus

The two clinical years are run on completely different models. Year two is entirely in-house at the University Counselor Training Clinic — nobody hunts for a placement, everyone starts in the same room, sections of CPSY 6810/6820 are capped at six students, and the entry gate is advancement to Graduate Candidacy plus a background check and personal professional liability insurance. The clinic is a real community service: $25 for the first session and $15 thereafter, sliding down or waived for anyone who cannot pay, offering individual, couple, family and group counseling to Bakersfield-area residents but explicitly not to CSUB students, who are directed to the campus counseling center to avoid dual relationships. It has run at roughly 80 to 100 active clients with each trainee carrying about two to three, open Monday through Thursday afternoons and evenings during the school year. Every session is videotaped and supervised by a licensed Marriage and Family Therapist, and recordings are used for feedback — a practice consistent with the catalog's designation of nine "clinical process classes" that "require the use of live clients and video recording of sessions." The clinic deliberately screens out acute crisis, serious psychiatric disability, domestic violence, suicidality, substance abuse and court-mandated clients, so the year-two caseload is a supported one. Year three flips outward: CPSY 6910 and 6920 are "field placement courses in service providing agencies" chosen "to facilitate the professional goals of the trainee," requiring liability insurance and "placement in an approved setting," at a minimum of 20 hours a week during standard business hours — which is why the program warns that full-time employment is effectively incompatible with years two and three. Site supervisors rate trainee knowledge and skill, and those ratings feed the program's Good Standing determination alongside GPA. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

PrerequisitesBachelor's degree from an accredited university; a 3.2 GPA over the most recent 60 semester units (applicants below 3.2 must submit a justification statement); at least six undergraduate psychology courses in total, of which three are named prerequisites — introductory statistics, abnormal psychology, and a developmental psychology course (child, human growth and development, or lifespan). 84%passed first time78 of 93 candidates
80%statewide, same window14,096 of 17,698 candidates

Counting every attempt rather than only first attempts, the figure is 76% across 127 sittings here, against 66% statewide. First attempts are the fairer comparison — counting resits measures how many tries people needed rather than how many passed, and it penalises a school whose graduates keep going.

On the separate California Law and Ethics exam, 88% of this school’s 100 first-time candidates passed, against 80% statewide.

Recorded by the Board as California State University, Bakersfield. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A programme that enrols more career-changers and more students of colour will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the programme changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No cohort size published anywhere — not on the program page, the applying page, the catalog or the program description PDF. Only the six-student practicum section cap and a 2019 newspaper reference to "over a dozen student clinicians" give any sense of scale.
  • No client-contact hour minimums for either Practicum I/II or Traineeship I/II. The existence of CPSY 6980 Clinical Extension for students who need more than two semesters "to complete the required hours" proves internal targets exist, but they are not published.
  • No relational (couple/family) hour sub-minimum published.
  • No published term-by-term plan of study. The year-one block is inferable from the advancement-to-candidacy rule and the practicum/traineeship years from course prerequisites, but CSUB publishes no course-sequence grid or plan-of-study PDF.
  • The 66-unit total does not reconcile against the department's public required-courses list, which names 21 courses summing to 60 units. The most likely balance is CPSY 5771 (1), 5772 (1), 6970 (1) and 5350 (3), but the 2024 program description states that CPSY 5350 "does not count toward requirements for the MS in Counseling Psychology" while the 2026–27 catalog has dropped that sentence. The composition of the final 6 units is genuinely unclear from published sources.
  • The department's own applying page and its 2024 program-description PDF disagree on the year-two clinic window — "1:00 p.m. to 9:00 p.m." versus "1:00 p.m. to 5:00 p.m." for three afternoons a week.
  • CPSY 6360 has two different published titles: "Techniques of Individual Counseling" in the 2026–27 catalog and "Advanced Counseling Skills" on the department's required-courses page and in its program description.
  • The department's clinic and program-handbook pages (university-counselor-training-clinic.shtml, program-handbook.shtml) return 404; the live clinic page is at a different URL and no student handbook PDF was locatable.
  • No published licensure-exam pass rate, employment outcomes, or time-to-degree data. Not CACREP- or COAMFTE-accredited, so no annual accreditation report exists.
  • No independent student reviews located; Reddit blocked automated retrieval, and Student Doctor Network and The GradCafe had no substantive CSUB threads.
  • No high-resolution, clearly-attributed freely-licensed photograph of the Dorothy Donahoe Hall or Education Building exists on Wikimedia Commons. The two Walter W. Stiern Library images there are marked public domain but carry no author or source, and the larger of them is only 1050x596; the campus photo used here is a monument shot from Wiki Loves Monuments 2016.
