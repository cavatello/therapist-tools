What this programme actually is

OrientationSystemic/relational, with an explicit social-justice frame and sustained self-of-the-therapist work

CSUDH is one of the very few California MFT programs that is its own academic department rather than an option bolted onto a psychology or counseling department — there is a Department of Marital and Family Therapy, it admits only MFT students, and it "permits enrollment in courses solely to students admitted to the M.S. in Marital and Family Therapy program." The consequence shows up in the course list: every one of the 64 units carries an MFT prefix, and there is no shared psychopathology or statistics seminar with another cohort. The stated centre of gravity is systemic theory plus social justice plus "self-of-the-therapist work," and the mission statement is unusually blunt about the second of those: the program prepares students "to critically and ethically engage concerns of intersectionality, equity, and social justice to improve systems of oppression." Courses run primarily in the evening for students who work days, though the handbook warns that "students must expand their availability in year two to accommodate traineeship." The program has grown fast: entering cohorts were 15 (2022), 25 (2023), 35 (2024) and 37 (2025).

The COAMFTE claim on the program page — "The MFT curriculum aligns with the educational and clinical COAMFTE Standards" — is worth reading precisely, because it is not an accreditation claim and CSUDH does not present it as one. The program does not appear anywhere in COAMFTE's own directory of accredited programs, which lists 127 programs worldwide and 25 in California; CSU Northridge is the only CSU on that list. What CSUDH actually holds is WSCUC accreditation (institutional, campus-wide, not MFT-specific) and Board of Behavioral Sciences approval under B&P §4980.36. So "alignment" is self-declared and has never been audited by COAMFTE, and it does not buy you the portability or AAMFT-track advantages that accreditation does.

That said, the claim is not empty, and the evidence for it is public. CSUDH has voluntarily adopted the artefacts that COAMFTE Standards Version 12.5 requires accredited programs to disclose: its five program goals are written to "reflect the COAMFTE's five Developmental Competency Components outlined in Standards Version 12.5"; it publishes a Graduate Achievement Data table in COAMFTE's own GAD I/II/III format (advertised program length 3 years, maximum 5; the 2022 cohort of 15 graduated 93% on time, reported 85.71% job placement and 100% licensure at any level); and it publishes separate annual demographic reports for students, faculty and clinical supervisors. Its handbook says clinical-training policy is "derived from the Board of Behavioral Science (BBS) regulations, aligned with the Commission on Accreditation for Family Therapy Education (COAMFTE) standards." The practical upside for an applicant is that you can see this program's graduation, employment and licensure numbers at all — most BBS-only California programs publish none of that.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

MFT 5152 units

Pre-Practicum I: Therapy Skills

Seminar and demonstration of supervised basic clinical skills — clinical interviewing, assessment and intervention — with emphasis on the therapeutic alliance and self-of-the-therapist. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingCSUDH gates entry to fieldwork behind two full semesters of skills lab (515 then 525) before you meet a client, which is a semester longer than most California programs run. The pairing is the program's real signature.

MFT 5252 units

Pre-Practicum II Advanced Therapy Skills

Instructional seminar demonstrating supervised advanced therapy skills covering assessment, diagnosis, treatment planning, case management and safety planning. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the gate, not just a course: the handbook says the program evaluates skills here to "determine Practicum and clinical training readiness in the field," and you must score 4 or better on a Clinical Readiness Assessment to be allowed into fieldwork at all. A named, scored competency threshold before client contact is rare in CA programs.

MFT 5762 units

Human Communication, Theory, and Skill

"Instruction in the purposes, theory, and skills of communication applied to intrapersonal and interpersonal experience."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA free-standing course on communication theory as such — not couples communication, not a technique module inside a theories course — is close to unique among California MFT curricula. It is on the books at CSUDH but does not appear in the handbook's published three-year roadmap, so confirm with the department whether it is currently offered.

MFT 5883 units

Treatment of Trauma with Individuals, Couples, and Families

"Beginning and advanced techniques in assessment, diagnosis, treatment, and crisis intervention of child and elder abuse, domestic violence, rape, disaster, crime, and other trauma."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingMost CA programs satisfy the BBS trauma and abuse-reporting content inside a law-and-ethics or crisis unit. CSUDH gives it a full 3-unit third-year course and frames it relationally — trauma treated in couples and families, not only in the individual.

MFT 5963 units

Practicum: Marital Family Therapy Traineeship

"A multi-semester course that encompasses fieldwork training, on-site supervision, and classroom participation" spanning a minimum of 12 months.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe 12-month floor matters: you cannot compress CSUDH's fieldwork into two heavy semesters, and the roadmap runs the course across semesters 3 through 6 plus an optional summer. Plan on your second and third years being structurally busier than your first.

MFT 5892 units

Advanced Therapeutic Approaches and Application

"An inclusive survey of major theoretical approaches, integrative psychotherapies, evidenced-based practice" with demonstrated case conceptualization.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is half of the culminating experience: the handbook names "the final signature assignment in the Advanced Therapeutic Approaches and Applications course" alongside the comprehensive exam as the two things you must pass to graduate. It runs in the final semester next to MFT 599.

The whole degree, term by term

What you would actually take, in the order you would take it. Sequences change; check the current catalog before you count on any single term.

Strict lockstep cohort on a full-time three-year plan; the handbook states that "failure to take classes in sequence may delay admission into Practica and or graduation," and part-time study is permitted only with the program director's and chair's approval. Every student signs a Plan of Study. Note that the roadmap lists MFT 596 in four semesters plus an optional summer, but the degree requires only three semesters of practica — nine of those units, not twelve, count toward the 64. MFT 400 and MFT 590 appear on the roadmap as electives that explicitly "do not count as part of the required 64 units." How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You apply, with the school’s help

There is an approved-site list and real support, and the seat is still yours to win. Most programmes are here and most students manage it. Ask for last year’s numbers rather than the policy: how many students placed on the first round, and how many had to extend.

The MFT program provides its students with a directory of sites with approved sites accessible in Tevera to eligible students after completing the Fieldwork Orientation(s) ... Students are free to select sites from this repository and contact the designated person listed to determine if the site is currently accepting students and the procedures to apply for the internship. ... Students should select three (3) agencies of interest at a minimum and engage in the interview process before choosing one agency. the school’s own page →
StartsSemester 3 — the fall of year two. Entry requires all 21 units of first-year coursework passed at B or better, attendance at a Fieldwork Orientation at least one term earlier, an application filed through Tevera, written authorization from the Director of Clinical Training, and a score of 4 or better on the Clinical Readiness Assessment scored out of MFT 525.
Hours required300 face-to-face client contact hours to graduate, of which at least 100 must be relational — client contact with couples or families. Minimum 10–15 hours per week at the site, more if the agency demands it, across a minimum of three semesters of practica (12 months minimum). The program says students can bank 300 to 1,300 of the 3,000 hours the BBS requires for licensure.

There is no in-house training clinic — CSUDH does not run one, and all client contact happens at community agencies across greater Los Angeles and parts of Orange County. Placement is a supervised search rather than an assignment: the program maintains a directory of contracted and previously-contracted agencies inside Tevera, you must shortlist at least three, interview at them, and choose one, with the Director of Clinical Training approving the match; you may also petition to add a new site, which takes roughly 6–8 weeks to approve and about two months more to contract. You may not do your traineeship at your own employer, and you must be actively seeing clients by week three or you lose your practicum enrollment. Supervision is doubled up: your off-site agency supervisor is your primary supervisor and must provide one hour of face-to-face individual or two hours of face-to-face group supervision for every five hours of direct client contact, using audio, video, case notes or live observation. Separately, the MFT 596 course instructor — an AAMFT Approved Supervisor or Candidate — runs classroom case discussion with a systemic and relational emphasis and consults with your site supervisor at least twice a semester, but explicitly "does not serve as the student's supervisor for the agency's clinical cases." The department does not require background checks or Live Scan, but many agencies do. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

How its graduates did on the Board’s exam

71%passed first time122 of 173 candidates
80%statewide, same window14,096 of 17,698 candidates

Counting every attempt rather than only first attempts, the figure is 54% across 310 sittings here, against 66% statewide. First attempts are the fairer comparison — counting resits measures how many tries people needed rather than how many passed, and it penalises a school whose graduates keep going.

On the separate California Law and Ethics exam, 74% of this school’s 206 first-time candidates passed, against 80% statewide.

Recorded by the Board as California State University, Dominguez Hills. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A programme that enrols more career-changers and more students of colour will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the programme changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No application deadline is published on the program site, the Program Details page or the catalog — only "apply via Cal State Apply."
  • Cohort sizes are published but acceptance rate and applicant volume are not.
  • The handbook says entry to fieldwork requires "all first-year courses totaling 21 units," but the published Year 1 roadmap sums to 22 units (11 per semester). One of the two figures is off by a unit; the department has not reconciled them in print.
  • MFT 576 Human Communication, Theory, and Skill is an active catalog course but appears nowhere in the handbook's three-year roadmap; whether it is currently offered is unclear.
  • GAD II and GAD III (job placement and licensure) exist only for the 2022 cohort; 2023, 2024 and 2025 are all marked "In Progress."
  • No named list of partner traineeship agencies is public — the site directory lives behind Tevera and is visible only to enrolled students who have completed Fieldwork Orientation.
  • No credible student commentary found. Reddit and GradCafe returned nothing about this program through available search, and the program publishes no student testimonials.
  • The GRE requirement in the catalog conflicts with the general CSU trend and could not be cross-checked against a separate admissions page.
