What this programme actually is

OrientationFamily-systems core with a strong trauma emphasis, taught through named single-model electives (CBT, EFT, Gestalt) rather than a single house theory

The rename is real but shallower than it sounds. Through the 2016-17 catalog this was an "M.S. in Counseling, Option in Marriage and Family Therapy"; the current title, "Counseling Psychology (formerly Marriage and Family Therapy)," dates from the 2020/2021 university catalog, and the course titles followed later — COUN 609 was still called "MFT Practicum" in the 2020-2021 catalog and is "Counseling Psychology Practicum" now. Nothing about licensure eligibility was lost. CSULB's own FAQ still opens with "The Counseling Psychology degree prepares students to enter the field of Marriage and Family Therapy," and then says the program "prepares students to apply for licensing with the Board of Behavioral Sciences for Licensed Marriage & Family Therapist (LMFT) and Licensed Professional Clinical Counselor (LPCC)." CSULB does not publish a stated rationale for the change, but the dual LMFT/LPCC preparation is the visible difference between the old option name and the new degree name.

Two things genuinely distinguish the curriculum. First, family systems survived the rename as named, required, sequenced courses — COUN 508A (Family Systems Applied to Aging and Lifespan Development) followed by COUN 508B (Family Systems Methods), the second being pure application and practice. Second, the elective bank is unusually model-specific: rather than 'advanced techniques,' CSULB lets you spend a whole semester inside one named modality — COUN 682 Gestalt Therapy (run as an experiential group), COUN 683 Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, COUN 684 Emotionally Focused Therapy, COUN 680 Family Trauma Treatment Seminar, COUN 559 Play and Art Therapy. Trauma runs through the required core too: COUN 601 Trauma and Grief Counseling is required for Counseling Psychology students specifically, and the department's clinical training partners include the Long Beach Trauma Recovery Center alongside its own Community Clinic.

The economics are the reason people apply and the reason most don't get in. CSULB estimates "$25,380 - $30,600 for California residents" for the whole 65-unit degree as of Fall 2026, for a program that qualifies you for two licenses. Its own FAQ then says admission is "highly competitive (acceptance rate is approximately 5% of the total number of applicants)." A CSULB News piece in October 2025 filled in why: applications rose from about 300 a year before the pandemic to more than 1,000, and the department doubled the cohort from 25 to 50 to absorb some of it. Classes are built around working students — most core courses meet 4:00pm or 7:00pm Monday through Thursday with some weekend sessions — but the fieldwork is not: three semesters at roughly 15 hours a week, and the program notes that "few of the non-profit settings have paid positions."

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

COUN 6094 units

Counseling Psychology Practicum

Counseling under intensive supervision on-campus in the Community Clinic and off-campus at an approved agency. Students will be required to assess, diagnose, develop appropriate interventions, and conduct counseling services for individuals, couples, or families. 150 fieldwork hours required.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingRead the rest of this catalog entry before you enrol: a B or better is required to continue in the program, students who fail get exactly one second attempt, and "if a student does not pass COUN 609 with a B grade or better after two semesters/attempts, they will be dismissed from the MS in Counseling Psychology program." Very few CA programs put an explicit dismissal trigger on a single clinical course.

COUN 508B3 units

Family Systems Methods

The application and practice of family systems therapeutic methods. Prerequisite: COUN 508A or consent of instructor. Counseling Psychology Majors Only.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe second half of a two-course systems sequence, and the tell that the MFT identity survived the rename — the degree title changed but the family-systems requirement is still stacked and still sequenced, with a theory course feeding a methods course.

COUN 6013 units

Trauma and Grief Counseling

Overview of developmentally informed models of trauma response, identifying and assessing problematic responses to trauma and loss, and guidelines for working with traumatized children, adolescents and families in various settings. Current treatment approaches of forms of post-traumatic distress and traumatic bereavement.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe catalog notes this is a required course for Counseling Psychology students and only recommended for the department's other counseling programs — a deliberate choice. Combined with the Long Beach Trauma Recovery Center as a training site, trauma is the closest thing this program has to a specialisation.

COUN 6823 units

Gestalt Therapy

This seminar is designed to be an experiential learning process of Gestalt Therapy. Students will learn Gestalt history, theory, and clinical application in a contemporary context to incorporate techniques into their own clinical practice.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingAn experiential semester in a single humanistic model is rare in California programs, which usually reduce Gestalt to a chapter in the survey theories course. Sitting alongside COUN 683 (CBT) and COUN 684 (Emotionally Focused Therapy), it means your elective choices actually determine which modality you leave able to practise.

COUN 6803 units

Family Trauma Treatment Seminar

Hands-on training in evidence-based treatment for families contending with trauma or loss. Readings, lectures, and in-class demonstrations and practice will focus on family assessment, goalsetting, narrative sharing, and family training on resilience-enhancing skills.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the course where the two distinguishing threads meet — family systems and trauma — and it is specific about method: narrative sharing and resilience-skills training with the whole family in the room, not individual trauma work with a family history taken.

COUN 6851 units

Community Mental Health Counseling

Advanced seminar on community mental health counseling with an emphasis on helping counselors gain skills required in contemporary community mental health settings, including recovery-oriented treatment for severe mental illness, case management, client advocacy, and local community service resources.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required one-unit seminar on case management, client advocacy and severe mental illness is a very honest piece of curriculum design: it acknowledges that most graduates' first job is county or nonprofit community mental health, not private practice, and gives them the paperwork-and-systems skills that job actually requires.

The whole degree, term by term

What you would actually take, in the order you would take it. Sequences change; check the current catalog before you count on any single term.

CSULB publishes the required course list but not a term-by-term grid, so this is presented as a single required-coursework block. Students take 4–5 courses each Fall and Spring on either a 2-year or 2.5-year track; admission is Fall-only and the program is full-time (minimum three courses per semester). 59 units of core plus 3 units of research methods plus a 6-unit culminating option = 65 units minimum. How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

Mixed - some help, your responsibility

The programme maintains a site list or relationships and may place some students, but securing the placement is ultimately yours. Ask for last year's numbers, not the policy.

StartsThe first clinical semester is COUN 609, which requires COUN 510, 511, 512, 513, 515, 522 and 608 as prerequisites — so realistically the second or third semester, once the core skills block is done
Hours required500 hours total: 150 hours in COUN 609 (combined on-site and off-site) plus 350 off-site fieldwork hours across COUN 643D and COUN 644D. The program's practicum page describes roughly 15 hours a week for three consecutive semesters; the catalog specifies 150 fieldwork hours for each of 643D and 644D while the practicum page describes 175 each.
In-house clinicCommunity Clinic for Counseling and Educational Services (on campus, College of Education); the department also names the Long Beach Trauma Recovery Center as a clinical training site

Every trainee begins clinical training on-site at the Community Clinic, which provides counselling, developmental and psychoeducational assessment, and academic intervention to school-age children, adults, and youth and adults with disabilities aged 16–22, at moderate cost, under direct supervision by College of Education faculty. At the same time students carry an off-site placement in a nonprofit setting, supervised by a California-licensed professional who has held the licence more than two years and is employed at that site. Practicum applications are due March 1 for the following fall and October 1 for spring, and fieldwork applications on the same calendar. The program flags that "few of the non-profit settings have paid positions." source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • CSULB does not publish a stated reason for renaming the program from Marriage and Family Therapy to Counseling Psychology. What is documented is the timing (the 2020/2021 university catalog, with course titles such as COUN 609 renamed later) and that the current degree prepares for both LMFT and LPCC. Any account of 'why' beyond that would be speculation.
  • No term-by-term plan of study is published for the Counseling Psychology M.S. — the required course list exists but the sequence does not, so the curriculum section here is a single block rather than a semester grid. (CSULB publishes such a grid for its School Counseling program but not this one.)
  • The CSULB 2026-2027 catalog lists COUN course titles without descriptions; all quoted descriptions here come from the 2024-2025 catalog at the same course IDs.
  • COUN 684 Emotionally Focused Therapy appears on the program's elective list but no catalog course description for it could be located in any CSULB catalog year searched, so only its title is reported.
  • The fieldwork hour split is inconsistent across CSULB's own pages: the practicum page says 175 hours each for COUN 643D and 644D, the catalog entries for both say 150. The 500-hour total is consistent.
  • No licensure exam pass rate, graduation rate or formal job placement rate is published (the program is not COAMFTE-accredited). The '95% of graduates find work' figure in CSULB News carries no cohort or methodology.
  • No independent student reviews of this specific program could be verified. Reddit was unreachable through both search and direct fetch during this research; GradReports carries CSULB psychology master's reviews, but they are for the research/experimental M.A. in the College of Liberal Arts, not this College of Education program, so they are not cited here.
  • CSU tuition has no per-unit rate for graduates — it is a flat per-semester charge by unit band ($5,231/semester full-time, $3,437 part-time for CA residents in 2026-27). The program's own $25,380–$30,600 estimate is the more useful figure and reflects the 2-year vs 2.5-year track difference.
