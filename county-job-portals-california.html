Why this page is not just a list of links

Every one of the addresses below loads a real, working, professional-looking job portal. None of them is the county.

That is the failure mode worth publishing: a person applying for a county behavioral health post does not get a 404 and go looking. They get a page that looks exactly right, and they apply to the wrong employer — or to nobody, because three of these are empty shells.

Marin, San Diego, Santa Barbara and Santa Cruz resolve to a different public employer of the same name. Alameda, Monterey and Trinity resolve to an unbranded tenant with no listings, and all three counties recruit somewhere else entirely.

The one worth saying out loud

Alameda County is not on NeoGov. It recruits through JobAps, and its human resources department links there as the only application route. The NeoGov address that carries its name has no listings and no class specifications at all.

Alameda is the fourth-largest county workforce in the state and runs one of the largest behavioral health plans, so the pay data and the employer directory both have a great deal to say about it. Getting to the application form is the step that breaks.

Four systems, 58 counties

48 of the 58 share one system, which is worth knowing before you make 58 accounts.

California counties do not run a common hiring platform, but they are close to it. One profile and one saved search covers most of the state; the rest have to be visited individually.

One account on the largest system reaches roughly four counties in five. It does not reach the other ten, and nothing warns you of that — a saved search simply never returns their postings.

This is the practical reason a portal directory beats a job board. A posting for a behavioral health clinician is gone in six weeks. The address it was posted at has been the same for years, and will be there the next time you look — which is also why a job archive was decided against on this site.

How each one was checked

Two passes, which disagreed — and that is the reason for both.

1Predictable addresses, kept only where the page named the county.

Seven address patterns per county against the largest shared system. Anything whose own page title did not name the county was rejected rather than assumed. That produced 19 counties — and caught four addresses answering for a city or a court.

2The remaining counties, from their own human resources pages.

Resolved by starting at each county’s own site and following where it sends applicants, recording the exact wording that proves the destination is the county. This pass corrected three more that the first had accepted as plausible — unbranded tenants that answer, look normal, and hold nothing.

3Then every address was fetched.

A California government site answering 403 to a script is working perfectly in a browser, so only a refused connection, a timeout, a DNS failure or an explicit 404 counts as broken. All 58 answered. Ignoring that distinction reported 22 false deaths the last time this site checked a set of government links.

What this page deliberately does not tell you

Whether any county is hiring, and what any listing pays. Both change weekly and neither can be kept true.

What the work actually pays comes from payroll rather than postings, on the county pay page. Why a county can employ someone who is not yet licensed is a Medi-Cal billing rule. What a county job is worth against loan repayment is the forgiveness page, and why a trainee cannot take just any placement is the practicum page.
