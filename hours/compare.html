What B actually has that A lost

It isn't the blue. Swapping the palette would change nothing that matters.

B's discipline is subtraction with a reason. Its core screen is four lines and one sentence — no chart, no percentage, no streak, no tabs, no "welcome back Dana". The answer to am I okay? is the largest object on the page and everything else is deliberately smaller than it. B even says this about itself: "If a competitor screenshots this they'll think it's unfinished — which is the same reaction Basecamp got."

A has been growing by accretion for eleven rounds. Nineteen sections, 36,700px — nearly four times B's height. Every addition was individually justified and the sum is a page that answers every question and asks the reader to work for it. That is exactly the failure mode B was written to avoid.

The honest read: A is a better argument, B is a better product surface. A's density is defensible on a marketing page where a reader is deciding; it is indefensible inside an app opened weekly by a tired person. That was the original settled position — B as the chassis, A as the evidence layer, split by emotional state — and the drift since then has been A quietly colonising both.

Recommendation: keep A's warmth and its research, and impose B's subtraction on the home page — cut to eight screens, one idea each, and let the feature pages carry the detail. Build the app itself in B, unchanged.
