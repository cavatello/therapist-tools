Licensure · California

Every gate between a master’s degree and an LMFT license.

23 guides plus a directory of 78 California programs, priced and dated — 66 of them with a page of their own. The four requirements that close at different speeds, what the Board charges now that fees have halved, and which one is actually holding you up.

Licensure toolsThe route, and what it costsWhere the degree comes from

Becoming an LMFT in California is five requirements with no discretion in any of them: a qualifying degree, practicum before you graduate, 3,000 supervised hours over at least 104 weeks, supervision that meets the Board's form, and two exams. The 3,000 is rarely the requirement that decides your date. A caseload of adult individuals will close it and still leave you ineligible, because 500 of your 1,750 direct hours have to be couples, families or children. The calculators here project all four requirements from the week you actually work and name the one you are waiting on.

Money runs alongside all of it. Board fees for the whole route fell from $1,750 to $875 on 1 July 2026 and revert in 2030. An associate job offer is really two questions wearing one coat — what it pays now, and how fast it closes your hours — and offer letters only answer the first. What the site cannot settle: whether the Board will accept a particular supervisor's paperwork, or whether a specific degree qualifies. It gives you the code section to check, not the ruling.
