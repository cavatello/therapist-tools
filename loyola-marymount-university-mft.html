What this programme actually is

OrientationClinical art therapy integrated with systemic marital and family therapy

This is not an MFT program with an art therapy elective attached — art therapy is the spine, and the two credentials are welded together at every level. The degree is the M.A. in Marital and Family Therapy with Specialized Training in Art Therapy, 57–60 semester units, accredited by the Commission on Accreditation of Allied Health Education Programs on the recommendation of the Accreditation Council for Art Therapy Education (CAAHEP/ACATE) — the specialised accreditation that makes the graduate eligible for the Art Therapy Credentials Board's ATR registration, on top of the California LMFT educational requirements. There is no version of the degree without the art therapy. It shows up in the studio courses (MFTH 601 Art Therapy Studio, MFTH 690 Art Therapy Studio: Professional Ethics and Identity), in assessment (MFTH 640 Psychological Tests & Art Therapy Assessment), in the DEI requirement (MFTH 692 Art-Based Exploration of DEI in Clinical Treatment), and — most tellingly — in all three practicum courses, each of whose catalog description reads 'supervised application of marital and family theory and psychotherapeutic techniques, including the application of art therapy, in field placement.' The gate is at the front door too: admission requires 18 semester units of studio art coursework that 'demonstrates proficiency and disciplined commitment in art making,' a portfolio of up to ten images, and a roughly 300-word written analysis of the psychological content of one of your own artworks. No other California MFT program asks for that.

The lineage is the oldest of its kind on the West Coast and among the oldest anywhere. Helen Landgarten, a painter, began developing the approach in 1964 working with geriatric and hospital patients, ran a trial program at Cedars-Sinai in 1967, and started a certificate program at Immaculate Heart College in 1972; the master's launched in 1973 and 'The first class graduated in 1975.' When IHC closed for financial reasons in 1980 the whole program moved to LMU as a separate department. It expanded to 60 units in 1988, opened the Helen B. Landgarten Art Therapy Clinic in 2007, and added the Art Therapy Research Institute in 2012. That fifty-year continuity is the reason the department has its own clinic, its own research institute, its own Mexico-based international courses (MFTH 620/622/629) and a summer arts workshop that has 'impacted upwards of 500 young people and their families' — infrastructure a newer program simply cannot assemble.

The cost is the honest counterweight. LMU charges $1,814 per semester hour for Marriage and Family Therapy in the 2026–2027 bulletin, so 57–60 units is roughly $103,000–$109,000 in tuition alone, before fees, art materials or living costs in Los Angeles — the top of the range for a California MFT degree. And the ATR is not conferred at graduation: the ATCB describes the ATR's '1000/100 requirements,' meaning post-degree supervised direct client contact and supervision hours accrued after you finish, running alongside (not instead of) the BBS's 3,000 hours for the LMFT. You are buying a two-credential pathway, and you pay for both in years as well as dollars.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

MFTH 6012 units

Art Therapy Studio: Theories and Practice

A hands-on tutorial of various art techniques and directives, so students recognise art's clinical properties and develop creative problem-solving skills.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required first-semester course in which the student is the one making art, not the client. This is the structural bet of the whole program — that you cannot use art clinically unless you have kept making it yourself — and it is why the studio-art prerequisite exists.

MFTH 6402 units

Psychological Tests & Art Therapy Assessment

Advanced knowledge and training in clinical assessment and psychological testing, covering intelligence tests and objective, subjective, and projective psychological evaluations.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingMost CA MFT programs teach one assessment course covering the DSM and standard instruments. Here the same course is required to carry formal art therapy assessment alongside conventional testing — an ACATE content area that has no equivalent in a standard MFT curriculum.

MFTH 6902 units

Art Therapy Studio: Professional Ethics and Identity

Students examine their professional identity in relation to social justice through art directives addressing licensure, values, and legal and ethical responsibilities.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe capstone ethics course is taught through art-making rather than case law recitation, and it is where the dual-identity problem — am I an MFT or an art therapist, and what does each licence let me do — is deliberately worked through rather than left to the student.

MFTH 6922 units

Art-Based Exploration of DEI in Clinical Treatment

A required art-based course on diversity, equity and inclusion in clinical treatment, taken in the same term as the first practicum. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingTwo things are notable: the DEI requirement is a separate required course rather than a thread, and it is delivered through art-making. It is scheduled in the same semester students start seeing clients, which is a deliberate pairing rather than a coincidence.

MFTH 6802 units

Marriage and Family Therapy Observation, Telehealth and Contemporary Practice

Explores contemporary approaches to psychotherapy and clinical art therapy with a diverse range of client populations, including instruction on best practices in art therapy telehealth.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingArt therapy over video is a genuinely unsolved clinical problem — materials, containment, and what happens to the artwork afterwards. A required course that names 'art therapy telehealth' specifically is a program that has had to think about it.

MFTH 6221 units

Multiculturalism and Art Therapy II in International Practice (Field Work)

Field work in international environments facilitated virtually and in study abroad experiences, with guided supervision.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingAn optional summer sequence (MFTH 620/622/629) built around the department's long-running Mexico program — supervised international field work as a credit-bearing option, which almost no California MFT program offers.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

Hours requiredA minimum of 350 accumulated hours providing art therapy services to individuals, groups, and/or families, across three practicum courses; 16 hours per week during MFTH 617 and 20 hours per week during each of MFTH 618 and MFTH 619
In-house clinicHelen B. Landgarten Art Therapy Clinic (opened 2007)

Practicum runs across three consecutive terms and 11 units — MFTH 617 (3 units), MFTH 618 Capstone II (4 units) and MFTH 619 Clinical Studies Capstone III (4 units) — and it begins unusually early, in the second semester of the two-year track, meaning students are seeing clients less than a year in. The weekly load steps up deliberately: sixteen hours a week in the first practicum, twenty hours a week in each of the next two, against a floor of 350 accumulated hours 'providing art therapy services to individuals, groups, and/or families.' Note that the required hours are counted as art therapy service hours, not generic clinical hours — that framing is what feeds the ATR pathway as well as the BBS one. Supervision is doubled: the program states that both clinical supervision and art therapy supervision are provided, which is the practical mechanism by which one placement generates hours toward two different credentials. The department runs its own site, the Helen B. Landgarten Art Therapy Clinic, whose mission is 'to serve the community by offering art therapy interventions to underserved children and families who have experienced trauma or are facing serious obstacles in life'; it contracts with outreach programs, schools and other organisations across Los Angeles County, and trainees deliver services there alongside licensed art therapists. A minimum grade of B in each practicum course is required to advance and to graduate. What the program does not publish is who initiates the placement — whether the department assigns students to clinic and contracted sites or whether students apply out — so the who_places field is left null rather than guessed. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • The program does not publish who initiates practicum placement — whether the department places students into the Landgarten Clinic and its contracted sites or whether students apply out to sites themselves. 'who_places' is left null rather than inferred.
  • No published couple/family sub-minimum within the 350 practicum hours. The requirement is stated as art therapy services to 'individuals, groups, and/or families' without a relational floor, unlike California programs that break out a specific relational-hours count. The BBS relational-hours requirement would still apply at the licensure level.
  • Cohort size is not published anywhere on the program site; the 18–21 figure is inferred from employment-outcome tables, not stated as an admissions number.
  • The full ATR education and experience standards could not be fetched — atcb.org returned 404s on its credential-requirements pages. The '1000/100' figure comes from ATCB's ATR Bridge Plan page, which references 'the ATR's 1000/100 requirements' in passing; the authoritative breakdown of what portion of those hours can be earned pre- versus post-degree was not obtainable.
  • The bulletin lists MFTH 621 as 1–3 units and MFTH 630 as 2–3 units, which is why the degree total is a range (57–60) rather than a fixed number.
  • No credible student discussion (Reddit, GradCafe, Student Doctor Network) could be gathered — the research session's web-search budget was exhausted and direct Reddit API access was blocked. 'voices' is empty rather than absent-because-nothing-exists.
