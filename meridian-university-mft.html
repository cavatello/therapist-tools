What this programme actually is

OrientationDepth/imaginal and transformative-learning, with somatic and expressive-arts elective tracks

Meridian is a small, mostly-doctoral, WSCUC-accredited graduate school whose entire curriculum is built on a house pedagogy it calls Transformative Learning and, inside that, a method it calls the Imaginal Process. This is not branding laid over a conventional syllabus: the first required course, ANC 101, introduces the Imaginal Process directly, in which "human capacities are cultivated through diversifying, deepening, embodying, and personalizing experience," and the same sensibility runs through the licensure courses. The BBS substance-abuse requirement is met by PSY 530 Ecstatic States of Culture, which frames addiction through "myth, current psychological models, and our own experience" and "the human need for ecstatic experience." The law-and-ethics content area is met partly by PSY 525 Career Development, which opens on Joseph Campbell's "Follow your bliss" and doubles as the course where students design their fieldwork plan. Psychopathology is taught to cover the DSM-5 "without succumbing to the diagnostic illusions of the medical model." If that language reads as intellectually serious to you, this is a distinctive place; if it reads as a stretch, that is the thing to resolve before enrolling.

The headline size is misleading and worth doing the arithmetic on. The 103 credits are QUARTER credits, and the catalog is explicit about the split: 92 credits (920 hours) of coursework plus 11 credits of supervised fieldwork. At the standard two-thirds conversion that is roughly 61 semester units of coursework and about 69 semester units all-in — an ordinary-sized California MFT degree, not an outsized one. Courses are 4 quarter credits each and run seven weeks; four quarters a year adds up to about 28 weeks of active instruction, "just about half the year that courses are actively running." The 23 courses the catalog names as covering the BBS MFT and LPCC content areas multiply out to exactly the 92 stated coursework credits. Coursework is primarily asynchronous online, with synchronous meetings encouraged but not required; students outside California but inside the US must come to a residential Transformative Learning Lab in California each academic year.

The consumer numbers deserve equal weight. Meridian's own 2024 filing with the California Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education reports 21 students beginning the M.A. in Counseling Psychology, 8 degrees awarded, 6 on-time graduates and an on-time completion rate of 28.57 percent; all 6 graduates available for employment were placed, but reported salaries clustered low (two under $30,000). Total charges for the program were reported as $62,192; the 2026–27 catalog rate is $676 per quarter credit, which puts 103 credits at about $69,600. Institution-wide, 365 students were enrolled, 279 of them doctoral — the master's programme is a minority of a school whose centre of gravity is its PsyD/PhD. Also note that a heavily promotional third-party site, meridianuniversityreviews.org, aggregates 75+ overwhelmingly positive testimonials and links out to Meridian's own website while disclosing no operator; treat it as marketing, not as independent review.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

ANC 1014 (quarter) units

Introduction to Transformative Learning

"This course introduces students to Imaginal Process, a distinct approach to transformative learning practiced at Meridian. In this approach, human capacities are cultivated through diversifying, deepening, embodying, and personalizing experience... Listening deeply to each other's stories is at the heart of this process."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the school's own method, taught as a required first course rather than described in a brochure. Nowhere else in California is 'Imaginal Process' a named pedagogy you enrol in; if you do not want it, essentially every later course is shaped by it too.

PSY 5304 (quarter) units

Ecstatic States of Culture

"This course utilizes myth, current psychological models, and our own experience to develop an integrated view of addiction that considers biology, psychology, cultural considerations, and human yearning. Issues regarding the use and abuse of drugs and alcohol will be explored within the contexts of culture and the human need for ecstatic experience."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingEvery California MFT programme has to teach substance use and co-occurring disorders. Almost none teaches it as a course about ecstasy and human yearning. This single course tells you more about Meridian's temperament than any mission statement — it is the clearest test of whether the depth frame reads to you as illuminating or as evasive of a very concrete clinical competency.

PSY 5014 (quarter) units

Psychopathology

"Each of us suffers in a unique way. Yet it is interesting and useful to recognize distinct varieties of human suffering without succumbing to the diagnostic illusions of the medical model... A genuinely psychological approach draws our attention to culture, myth, story, and metaphor in the meaning we make about the symptoms we observe. The limits, ambiguities, and cruelties of professionalized responses to human suffering will be among our considerations."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe DSM course at most programmes is pure compliance. Here it is explicitly framed as a critique of the medical model, which matters practically: you will still need fluency in DSM-5 diagnosis for insurance panels and agency work, and you should ask how much drilling of that fluency this course actually contains.

PSY 5254 (quarter) units

Career Development

"Joseph Campbell's famous phrase, 'Follow your bliss,' is a directive that for most people is easier said than done." The course covers vocation, calling and "the potential relationship between forms of psychopathology and Western culture's lack of support for the notion of personal destiny," and is also where students "become thoroughly familiar with Meridian's fieldwork process" and formulate a fieldwork plan.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the course where the mythopoetic register and the practical machinery collide — it is both the Campbell course and the course where you plan the traineeship that will determine whether you finish on time. The catalog also lists it under the BBS 'California Laws and Professional Ethics' content area, which is worth asking about directly.

PSY 5154 (quarter) units

Healing and Transforming Trauma

"This course explores current issues in the field of psychological trauma through personal, historical, cultural, and archetypal perspectives. Its intent is threefold: To develop the student's ability to engage their own personal traumatic material (generally experienced through fragmented images that are the common aftermath of overwhelming experience); to integrate imaginal approaches to trauma with principles inherent in the recovery model; and to identify key somatic, cognitive, affective, behavioral, and neurological effects associated with trauma."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingNote the first of the three stated aims: the required trauma course asks you to work your own traumatic material, not only to learn protocols. Combined with the 40 required hours of personal psychotherapy, this is a programme that treats the student's own psyche as coursework.

PSY 5184 (quarter) units

Psychology of Power, Privilege, and Culture

"In this course, we will explore the internalization of cultural oppression as well as oppressive voices towards 'the other' that live in our own hearts. The course will review forms of systematic oppression such as racism, sexism, classism, able-bodyism, adultism, ageism, and homophobia..."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the single course that carries the entire BBS cultural-competency requirement, and it is framed inwardly — internalised oppression and one's own oppressive voices — rather than as a survey of working with diverse populations. Useful to know if you want the latter.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You find your own site

Securing a placement is your responsibility. A student who cannot find a site cannot graduate, and that risk is highest if you are rural, studying online, or need Spanish-language or specialty hours.

Students in degree programs that require field placement (internship) are responsible for utilizing the University's Fieldwork Handbook and support services to find their own employment/internship site. The University does not provide students with internal field placement/internship. the school’s own page →
StartsStudents become eligible to begin fieldwork after completing their first year of coursework; MFT/LPCC-bound students must have started by the beginning of the third year
Hours requiredA 340-hour traineeship including a minimum of 280 direct service hours, done in full accordance with the BBS practicum requirement; carried as 11 credits of PSY 597 Supervised Fieldwork

There is no in-house training clinic. Meridian is an online school with two small centres (Petaluma and Santa Monica), and students arrange their own traineeship sites in their own region — the independent comparison site MFT California summarises it as students who "conduct their fieldwork requirement in their local region and set up their own fieldwork, with support from Meridian," and the catalog's own language is the softer "Every effort is made to support students in selecting and completing required fieldwork" via ongoing contact with fieldwork staff and clinical faculty plus a Fieldwork Handbook discussed in the first year. Planning your own placement is treated as coursework: PSY 525 Career Development is where you build the fieldwork plan and weigh "the pros and cons of having fieldwork fulfill California licensing board hours, time frames and procedures for applying to and completing internships, the use of one's job as an internship site, and options for arranging alternative field placements." The academic side of practicum is a three-course sequence, PSY 751–753, covering intake assessment, case formulation, treatment planning, note-taking and crisis management, with PSY 752 focused on multicultural themes and PSY 753 introducing the scope of practice for LMFT and LPCC; MFT-bound students additionally take PSY 754 Clinical Practicum Extension so that coursework overlaps fieldwork as the BBS requires. The catalog does not publish a relational/couples sub-minimum, a supervision-hours formula, or a list of affiliated sites, and it states the fieldwork must be "clinical in nature" — a real constraint, since Meridian's fieldwork credits at the doctoral level can otherwise be earned through non-clinical work. Forty hours of personal psychotherapy are separately required and may in some cases be completed before enrolment. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

How its graduates did on the Board’s exam

Too few candidates to publish a rate

The Board recorded 24 first-time candidates from this school across the whole window, and this page does not print a percentage below 40. On a handful of candidates a single result moves the figure by tens of points, and a small programme would be described by noise. It usually means the programme is small, new, or both.

Recorded by the Board as Meridian University. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A programme that enrols more career-changers and more students of colour will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the programme changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No published term-by-term plan of study for the M.A. in Counseling Psychology — the curriculum above is reconstructed from the catalog's BBS content-area mapping, which happens to total exactly the stated 92 coursework credits.
  • No published cohort size, acceptance rate or application deadline; Meridian states class size varies by quarter.
  • No list of affiliated traineeship sites, no relational/couples hour sub-minimum, and no supervision-hours formula published for the 340-hour traineeship.
  • No BBS/LMFT clinical exam pass rate published; Meridian's own 2024 BPPE filing answers 'No' to whether the M.A. leads to a state-licensed occupation, so no exam data is reported there either.
  • No credible independent student discussion found on Reddit or Student Doctor Network. Niche (19 reviews) and a heavily promotional, undisclosed-operator site (meridianuniversityreviews.org) are essentially the only review sources; Yelp blocks automated access.
