# The h1 — 24 options

Your starting point was **"The Ultimate Resource For California MFTs."**

What's right about it: it names the audience and the geography, which most sites
fail to do. What's weak about it: *"ultimate resource"* is a claim anybody can
make and nobody can check, and it's the exact phrase a hundred thin affiliate
sites already use. It asserts authority instead of demonstrating it.

The strongest headline for cold traffic does three jobs at once — names **who**
it's for, names the **problem** they already feel, and hints at **why this one**
rather than the other twenty. Below, grouped by what each one competes on.

Every option is written to sit above a subhead and the four audience doors, so
none of them has to carry the whole load alone.

---

## A. Closest to your instinct — "ultimate", made checkable

These keep your framing but replace the unprovable superlative with something a
stranger can verify in one scroll.

**1. The complete California MFT resource — and every number in it is sourced.**
The claim plus the receipt, in one line. Completeness is the promise; sourcing is the proof.

**2. Everything a California MFT needs to know after the degree.**
Quietly ultimate. "After the degree" does the segmenting work without a list.

**3. The California MFT reference that shows its working.**
Short, confident, and it's a claim your competitors structurally cannot copy.

**4. California's most complete MFT resource. 121 pages, every figure checked.**
The number *is* the credibility. Update it as the site grows.

**5. The reference California therapists actually needed.**
Past tense implies it didn't exist before. Modest and slightly warm.

## B. Problem-first — the 37signals move

Basecamp opens with *"Remember when companies cared?"*; Fizzy with *"Trello put
on 40 pounds of cruft."* They name the reader's frustration before they name
themselves. This is the register you asked for.

**6. Grad school taught you therapy. It didn't teach you any of this.**
My favourite of the set. Universally true for the audience, instantly recognisable, and it defines the site's scope in one sentence.

**7. You trained to sit with people. Then someone handed you a tax bill.**
Concrete and a little funny. Strong for forum and Facebook traffic where tone matters.

**8. Nobody teaches California therapists the business part. So here it is.**
Problem, gap, solution. The "so here it is" is the 37signals shrug.

**9. Everything about being an MFT in California, except the therapy.**
Defines by exclusion, which is memorable and unusually precise.

**10. The MFT questions your programme skipped and your supervisor guessed at.**
Names two specific failed sources. Slightly pointed — good if you want an edge.

**11. Therapy school prepares you for the room. Not for the rest of it.**
Softer version of 6. Better if the audience skews pre-licensed and anxious.

---

## C. Trust-first — competing on being *right*

This is your genuine differentiator and no competitor can match it without
rebuilding from scratch. If you want the headline to defend the moat, pick here.

**12. Every number a California MFT needs — traced to the statute it came from.**
The single most defensible sentence on this list.

**13. California MFT answers you can check. All of them.**
The two-sentence rhythm lands hard. "All of them" is the dare.

**14. The only California MFT resource that cites where the numbers came from.**
Strongest claim here — only use it if you're confident it's literally true.

**15. Free answers for California MFTs, with the receipts.**
Casual, forum-native, and pairs "free" with "credible", which usually fight each other.

---

## D. Journey-first — pairs best with the four doors

If the hero shows aspiring / student / associate / licensed, a headline that
names the arc makes those four doors feel inevitable rather than bolted on.

**16. From your first application to a practice that pays you properly.**
The whole career in nine words. Best fit for the four-door hero.

**17. The California MFT route, end to end, with the numbers at every step.**
"End to end" is the completeness claim; "the numbers" is the differentiator.

**18. Aspiring, in a programme, accruing hours, or licensed — start here.**
Literally names all four segments. Maximum self-identification, minimum poetry.

**19. Ten years of California MFT decisions, worked out in advance.**
Reframes the site as foresight rather than reference.

---

## E. Cost-first — the pain of not knowing

**20. The answers accountants charge by the hour for. Free.**
Names the alternative and its price. Very strong; the two-word second sentence does the work.

**21. What guessing costs a California therapist, and how to stop.**
Loss-framing. Higher tension, slightly heavier tone.

**22. Free, for California therapists, because the arithmetic should be.**
Lifted from your own About page voice. The most *you* line here.

---

## F. Blunt — the Once/Campfire register

**23. California MFTs: everything, checked, free.**
Six words. Reads like a manifesto title. Needs a strong subhead beneath it.

**24. The California MFT handbook. Free, sourced, and not selling you anything.**
"Not selling you anything" is a real differentiator in a space full of lead-magnet sites — but check it against your affiliate links before using it.

---

## My shortlist

If you want one, take **6** — *"Grad school taught you therapy. It didn't teach
you any of this."* It's the most 37signals line here: it opens on the reader's
problem, defines the site's entire scope in one clause, works for all four
audience segments at once, and needs no proof to land because the reader already
knows it's true.

If you'd rather lead with authority than empathy, take **12** — *"Every number a
California MFT needs — traced to the statute it came from"* — because it's the
one claim nobody else can make.

If the four doors are going in the hero, take **16** — *"From your first
application to a practice that pays you properly"* — it sets up the doors better
than anything else on the list.

**Pairing note.** The best structure is a problem headline with a proof subhead,
not both doing the same job:

> # Grad school taught you therapy. It didn't teach you any of this.
> Fees, hours, tax, panels, programmes and rates — for California MFTs, AMFTs
> and anyone on the way. 121 pages, every figure traced to the statute it came
> from, free and without an account.
