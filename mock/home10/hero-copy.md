# The hero: h1 + the secondary line

Locked: **The complete California MFT resource.**

What follows builds it out. The secondary line's job is no longer "prove the
numbers are sourced" — that argument moves further down the page where it
belongs. Up here it does two things instead: **name who this is for**, and
**say what it covers**, in the voice of someone who has been through it.

---

## Part 1 — small refinements to the h1 itself

Same claim, slightly different weight. All work with every subhead below.

1. **The complete California MFT resource.**
   The line as-is. Clean, confident, nothing wasted.

2. **The complete resource for California MFTs.**
   Puts the *person* before the category. Marginally warmer, one word longer.

3. **The complete California MFT and AMFT resource.**
   Names associates explicitly. Worth it if associates are a big share of traffic — they often don't think "MFT" means them yet.

4. **The complete resource for California therapists.**
   Widest net. Use only if LCSWs and LPCCs matter as much as MFTs to you.

5. **The complete California MFT resource. Every stage of it.**
   Two sentences. The second does the segmenting work before the doors do.

6. **Finally — the complete California MFT resource.**
   One word, and it changes the whole register from claim to relief. Very 37signals.

## Part 2 — the secondary line

### A. Stage-naming — pairs best with the four doors

**1.** From your first grad-school application, through the 3,000 hours, to a practice that actually pays you — it's all here, and it's free.
> *Best all-rounder. Names the whole arc, ends on the benefit, closes with the price.*

**2.** For aspiring MFTs, students, registered associates and licensed therapists. Wherever you are on the route, start here.
> *The most literal. Maximum self-recognition, minimum poetry.*

**3.** Whether you're choosing a programme, counting hours, or running a full caseload — everything you need is in one place.
> *Three concrete verbs beat four job titles. Reads faster.*

**4.** Written for California therapists at every stage — before the degree, during the hours, and long after the licence.
> *"Long after the licence" is the bit that tells a licensed LMFT this isn't just a student site.*

---

### B. Coverage — what's actually here, in a therapist's words

**5.** Programmes, licensure, the 3,000 hours, insurance panels, fees, taxes and rates. Everything about being a therapist in California except the therapy.
> *The list is the proof. The last clause is the memorable part.*

**6.** Everything a California therapist needs to know outside the therapy room.
> *Nine words. The strongest short option on this page.*

**7.** Licensure, programmes, hours, panels and pay — the parts of this career nobody teaches you.
> *Ends on the shared grievance, which is what makes a stranger keep reading.*

**8.** The programmes, the Board, the hours, the panels, the money. All of it, in one place, for California.
> *Rhythm carries this one. Good for a large centred hero.*

---

### C. For-therapists — the care angle

**9.** You take care of your clients. This takes care of everything else.
> *The warmest line here, and the most likely to be quoted back to you.*

**10.** Made for California therapists by someone who got tired of guessing. Free, no account, nothing to buy.
> *Founder-voice in the hero. Very 37signals, and it makes the site feel like a person rather than a company.*

**11.** Built for therapists, not for accountants. Plain answers to the questions this career keeps asking you.
> *"Not for accountants" is a real positioning claim — it says this is readable.*

**12.** Nobody sits you down and explains any of this. So here it is, for California therapists, free.
> *Problem, gap, solution, price — in one breath.*

### D. Two-part — a claim, then a promise

**13.** Everything California therapists need outside the therapy room. Free, no account, and nothing to sign up for.
> *Safe, complete, and answers the "what's the catch" question immediately.*

**14.** One place for the programmes, the licence, the hours and the money. Made for California therapists, free for all of them.
> *"Free for all of them" is warmer than "free".*

**15.** For MFTs, AMFTs, LCSWs and LPCCs in California. Everything the career asks of you, answered in one place.
> *Only option that names all four licences. Use if you want LCSWs and LPCCs to feel included from the first screen.*

### E. Short — for the huge-type directions (Slab, Manifesto, HEY)

**16.** Everything, except the therapy.
**17.** For every stage of the route.
**18.** Free, for California therapists.
**19.** The whole career, in one place.

## My recommended pairings

**The safe one — use this if you want to stop deciding:**

> ### The complete California MFT resource.
> From your first grad-school application, through the 3,000 hours, to a practice
> that actually pays you — it's all here, and it's free.

Names the arc, ends on the benefit, kills the price objection in four words. Works
at any type size and reads well on a phone.

---

**The warm one — best for forum and Facebook traffic:**

> ### Finally — the complete California MFT resource.
> You take care of your clients. This takes care of everything else. Programmes,
> licensure, hours, panels and pay, free and in one place.

"Finally" turns a claim into relief. The second sentence is the one people repeat.

---

**The bold-type one — for Slab, Manifesto and HEY:**

> ### The complete California MFT resource.
> Everything about being a therapist in California, except the therapy.
> Free, no account, nothing to buy.

Short enough to sit under 90px type without fighting it.

## Where the sourcing claim goes now

It was doing the wrong job in the hero, but it's still your best argument — it
just belongs at the **Desire** stage, not **Attention**. Keep it as the proof
chips directly under the subhead, where it answers "is this any good?" *after*
the reader already knows what it is:

> `Free, no account` &nbsp; `Every figure checked` &nbsp; `Updated August 2026` &nbsp; `California only`

and then in full further down, in the *"Why you can trust a number on this site"*
section, which already carries it.
