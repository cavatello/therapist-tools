Navigation, and content blocks.

Two separate proposals, both built only from what the site already has. No new pages, no new sections, no new copy — every label and one-line description below is lifted verbatim from the live header on 7 August 2026. The only thing that changes is how the existing 121 pages are arranged, and how existing figures are drawn.

Part one — the top navigation

five ideas

The problem to solve first: Tools holds six items, About holds three, and Learn holds twenty-seven — articles, both directories and all three index pages in one flat panel. Every proposal below is a different way of splitting that one panel.

01

Five topics

Use the taxonomy the site already publishes.

Retires the 27-item “Learn” panel. Every article already belongs to exactly one of these five hubs, so nothing has to be re-filed and no new page is created.

Five top-level items is more to scan than three, and “Training” is 82 pages against Getting paid’s four — visibly lopsided.

02

Task vs. reading

Keep today’s shape, but split Learn four ways.

The current split is right — a calculator and an article are different things. It only fails because Learn absorbed the directories and the indexes too. Pulling those into their own menus fixes it without changing the idea.

“Guides” still carries nineteen items. Better than 27, not solved.

Career stage

The same four doors as the landing page.

A reader knows which stage they are at before they know which topic their question belongs to. It also makes the nav agree with the home page instead of offering a second, different structure.

Several pages belong to two stages. The Rate Gap matters to an associate and to an established practice; you would either duplicate it or pick.

04

Search-first

Shrink the nav; let search and the hub carry it.

Most arrivals are from Google with a specific question, and they do not browse. This stops pretending a menu can hold 121 pages and puts the effort into the hub, which is already the best page on the site.

Needs a real search index, which does not exist yet. And it hides the breadth — a first-time visitor cannot see how much is here.

five ideas

Five reusable ways to draw something the site already knows. Each one takes figures that are currently set as plain sentences and gives them a shape. No block below invents a number, a claim or a line of copy.

A single computed number, its label, and the date it was checked. The stamp is the whole point — it is the site’s actual claim, and no competitor carries one.
