What this programme actually is

OrientationSchool-Based Family Counseling — family-systems practice applied jointly to the home and the school, with an explicit social-justice and urban-Los Angeles framing ("a specific emphasis in training our students to become culturally competent, social justice oriented counselors").

This is not really an MFT programme with a school-counselling flavour; it is two credentials welded together, and you cannot buy them separately. A graduate leaves with four things: the M.S. in Counseling, the Pupil Personnel Services (PPS) Credential in School Counseling, the Advanced Authorization in Child Welfare and Attendance (CWA), and eligibility for both the LMFT and the LPCC. The programme's own framing is that "MFTs are better therapists when they understand how to assist their clients in navigating and succeeding within the school system," and School-Based Family Counseling as a discipline treats school and family as "inextricably interconnected systems." The CWA authorisation is the least familiar piece: it qualifies you for the school jobs that deal with attendance, truancy, foster and homeless youth and welfare referrals. If that combination is what you want, there is no other California programme that packages it this way and this is a genuinely unusual, well-defined thing to be.

If you only want the LMFT, the honest answer is that you should not be here. The degree is 73–77 units against the BBS's 60-unit floor, and four courses in the required list exist because of the school credential rather than the licence: Disability in Schools and Society (3, listed as EDSP 5000 on the 2019 flyer and as COUN 5004 on the current roadmap), COUN 5257 Career Education and Guidance in Schools (3), COUN 5281 Seminar: Leadership in Pupil Personnel Services (3), and COUN 5286 Supervised Field Experience in School Counseling including the CWA requirements (6). That is fifteen units — strip them and you are left with 58, which is to say a conventional 60-unit MFT curriculum. The credential also drags in its own compliance work: a Certificate of Clearance from the Commission on Teacher Credentialing in your first semester and the CBEST basic-skills requirement, which you must pass to move from conditional to full admission. And the fieldwork doubles: COUN 5289 (MFT site practicum) and COUN 5286 (school and CWA) are listed as parallel options every semester of years two and three, so students commonly carry a clinical placement and a school placement at once, 15–20 hours a week. Sacramento State, which offers PPS as a separate post-graduate track, prices that credential at 15 units too — the number is not unreasonable, it is just fifteen units you have no use for if you never intend to work in a school. The FAQ closes the reverse door as well: "we do not offer a PPS credential only program. We do not offer a CWA only Program." No MFT-only variant is described anywhere on the programme's pages.

The logistics are unusually restrictive and unusually clearly stated. Full-time and cohorted, in person, evenings: classes run 4:30–7:15pm and 7:30–10:10pm, two to four days a week, and "Students need to be living in the Los Angeles area in order to start the program. There is NO on-line option for this program." Two cohorts of twelve are admitted once a year for fall, from roughly 300 applications, and clinical class sizes stay at twelve. First-year students train in the School-Based Family Clinic in King Hall C-1067, an on-campus clinic offering free counselling to the surrounding community in English and Spanish, Mondays to Thursdays; you work in co-therapy pairs under direct supervision by licensed faculty, recording and reviewing sessions, starting in the first semester with shadowing, peer counselling and role plays. On cost, treat the programme's own figure with care: the FAQ's $24,380 total is built on "$4200 per semester" full-time and "$2800 per semester" part-time, but Cal State LA's published Fall 2026 rate is $4,821.04 full-time (6.1+ units) and $3,027.04 part-time for California residents. Rerunning the programme's own formula at those rates gives roughly $27,300 rather than $24,380.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

COUN 52306 (3 + 3) units

Family Counseling Laboratory Fieldwork

“Taken twice, in the fall and spring of the first year, meeting in the on-campus School-Based Family Clinic. The programme states: "During the first and second semesters of your first year, you will complete the majority of your practicum hours in this clinic... you and a co-therapist will assist parents and their children with issues that range from school behavioral problems to family separation issues.”catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingVery few California programmes put students in front of real families in the first semester of the first year, and fewer still do it as co-therapy pairs in a faculty-supervised university clinic with recording equipment. It is the strongest argument for the programme: the clinical exposure front-loads, so by the time you go out to a community site in year two you have already carried families under direct observation rather than only role-played.

COUN 52866 (3 + 3) units

Supervised Field Experience in School Counseling (Includes requirements for Child Welfare and Attendance Services)

“The school-side fieldwork strand, listed on the programme flyer with the parenthetical "(Includes requirements for Child Welfare and Attendance Services)", and scheduled on the roadmap as an option in every fieldwork semester from the fall of year two through the spring of year three.”catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the course that turns the degree into a credential and the six units you are paying for if you only want the licence. It is also the mechanism by which the CWA authorisation is earned rather than merely offered. Because it runs in parallel with COUN 5289, a student in year two is typically placed in a K–12 school and a clinical agency simultaneously.

COUN 52896 (3 + 3) units

Site Practicum in Marriage, Family, & Child Counseling

“The clinical fieldwork strand, listed on the flyer with the legacy title "Marriage, Family, & Child Counseling" and scheduled on the roadmap in every fieldwork semester of years two and three, with weekly supervision slots on Mondays (4:45, 6:00 or 7:15pm) or Wednesdays (3:00–4:15pm).”catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingTwo things a prospective student should read off this entry. First, the course title still uses California's pre-1999 MFCC language, which is a small sign of how long the curriculum documents have gone without a rewrite. Second, the supervision slots are fixed and evening — combined with 15–20 hours a week of fieldwork, this is why the programme warns that "full-time employment is often not possible."

COUN 52813 units

Seminar: Leadership in Pupil Personnel Services

Required course in the 36-unit block, taken in the fall of the second year (Wednesdays 7:25–10:10pm on the current roadmap). It is one of the four school-credential courses that sit outside a standard MFT curriculum. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingIncluded here not because it is appealing but because it is diagnostic: a leadership seminar in pupil personnel services is a school-administration course, not a clinical one. If the description of that course makes you impatient, this is the wrong programme — three more like it are required, and they are the reason for the 73–77 unit total.

COUN 43303 units

Trauma and Crisis Counseling

“Appears only on the current course roadmap, in the fall of the third year, flagged "(Optional for LPCC only)." It does not appear on the programme flyer's required course list.”catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the hidden price of the LPCC. The programme's FAQ says plainly "Does this program also prepare students for the LPCC... Yes, it does," but the roadmap shows it costs an extra three-unit course beyond the 73–77, taken in a year when most students have no other coursework. Worth raising with an adviser before assuming dual eligibility is free here.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You find your own site

You are responsible for securing a placement. Most programmes are like this and most students manage it, but a student who cannot find a site cannot graduate - and that risk is highest if you are rural, online, or need Spanish-language or specialty hours.

StartsFirst semester of the first year, in the on-campus clinic (COUN 5230). Community and school fieldwork begins in the fall of year two and runs to the end of year three.
Hours required18 fieldwork units total — COUN 5230 Family Counseling Laboratory Fieldwork (6), COUN 5286 School Counseling PPS & CWA Fieldwork (6), COUN 5289 MFT Site Practicum (6). The programme quantifies field time as "15-20 hours per week of fieldwork in their second year and third year." No total client-contact-hour figure and no relational sub-minimum is published.
In-house clinicSchool-Based Family Clinic, King Hall C-1067, on the Cal State LA campus

The first year is the school's to run and the last two are yours. From the first semester you are in the School-Based Family Clinic in King Hall — shadowing, peer counselling and role plays at first, using the clinic's recording equipment to review your own work, "closely supervised by faculty members" — and by the second semester you are carrying real cases with a co-therapist, working with parents and children on problems ranging from school behaviour to family separation. The clinic is free to the surrounding community, runs Monday to Thursday, and offers services in English and Spanish; its clinicians are described as advanced graduate students supervised directly by licensed Counseling faculty in the Charter College of Education. The handover happens in February of year one, at a fieldwork placement fair, and after that the programme is explicit that it does not place you: "Not directly. We provide a fieldwork placement fair the year before you begin fieldwork. We also maintain a list of agencies and schools with which we have a current fieldwork contract. You are responsible for finding the school and/or agency that feels like the right fit for you." The stated rationale is that fieldwork is "a bridge to employment" and that many students are hired by their sites. From then on the structure is two parallel tracks — COUN 5289 for MFT hours and COUN 5286 for school counselling and CWA hours — each appearing as an option in every fieldwork semester, with fixed weekly supervision slots on Monday and Wednesday evenings, and a separate application for fieldwork enrolment filed the semester before. Fifteen to twenty hours a week of placement, on top of evening classes, is why the programme says outside employment is possible but "full-time employment is often not possible." source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 53 of the 65 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

Getting in

Cohort size"We typically receive about 300 applications and accept a maximum of 24 students" — two cohorts of twelve, admitted once a year for fall. Class sizes run from 12 for clinical courses up to 24 for lectures.
GRENot required — "no standardized tests (e.g., GRE) are needed to apply"
PrerequisitesOne prerequisite course, in Counseling or Psychological Theories, completed before the programme starts (COUN 4500 is offered at Cal State LA the preceding summer); waived for psychology majors. A 2.75 GPA in the last 60 semester units is the published minimum, though the programme notes that historically it interviews applicants at 3.0+ with mental-health field experience. Two letters of recommendation, a two-page resume, a personal statement of no more than three pages, unofficial transcripts, and an interview with the departmental screening committee. After admission: a Certificate of Clearance from the CTC, a negative TB test, and the CBEST basic-skills requirement to convert conditional admission to full admission.
DeadlineApplications open 1 October and close 15 January for the following fall (15 January 2026 for the Fall 2026 cohort). Interviews are held on the last two Fridays and Saturdays of February, by Zoom.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • The Cal State LA academic catalog (ecatalog.calstatela.edu) sits behind an AWS WAF bot challenge and could not be fetched, so no official catalog course descriptions were obtained. Every course code, title and unit value here comes from the programme's own flyer PDF and its current course roadmap; where a description is given it is drawn from those documents or the clinic page, never from the catalog.
  • The two curriculum documents disagree in places. The flyer says "73-77 units" in its total row but "76-80 units" in the paragraph above it; Disability in Schools and Society is EDSP 5000 on the flyer and COUN 5004 on the roadmap; the comprehensive exam is COUN 5960 on the flyer and 5096 on the roadmap. The flyer is dated "Effective Fall 2019" and "Revised 01/2019"; the roadmap was revised 21 April 2025.
  • No total client-contact-hour requirement is published for any fieldwork course, and no relational (couple/family) hour sub-minimum. Field time is quantified only as "15-20 hours per week" in years two and three.
  • The split between the school and MFT fieldwork strands is not fully specified: COUN 5289 and COUN 5286 are listed as "fieldwork options" every semester without a rule for how many of each a student must take, so the 18-unit fieldwork block cannot be allocated with certainty.
  • The programme does not state anywhere whether the four school-credential courses can be dropped by a student who wants only the LMFT. It says only that PPS-only and CWA-only routes do not exist; the reverse question is unanswered on its pages.
  • The cost figures on the SBFC FAQ are stale — they use $4,200 per full-time semester and $2,800 part-time against a published Fall 2026 rate of $4,821.04 and $3,027.04. The $990-per-class summer Open University rate could not be independently confirmed.
  • The SBFC site's application-process page still shows a January 2021 deadline; the deadline used here is the one on the official calstatela.edu programme page.
  • Nothing credible was found in student discussion forums. Reddit search endpoints were unreachable during this pass, and an earlier survey of r/therapists, r/askatherapist, r/psychologystudents, The GradCafe and Student Doctor Network found no Cal State LA SBFC thread worth citing. The voices above are all from the programme's own documents.
