01

The answer

  • Build it — the data is there. 313 Bay Area nonprofit clinical mental health organizations and 517 active health-center service-delivery sites are reachable from federal files that anybody can re-download and check. This is not a scraping project.
  • Two directories, not one. A trainee cannot lawfully work in a private practice; a registered associate can. The two eligible universes are genuinely different sets and merging them would send students somewhere they cannot go.
  • Claim eligibility, never availability. No public source says who takes trainees. The directory answers “is this the kind of setting the code allows, and how do I ask?” — which is a question it can answer completely.
  • Not 600 individual pages. The coverage audit already found 36% of this library serving the quietest stage. One directory plus depth on the 22 organizations large enough to write about, and the long tail stays in the table.
  • Build the data statewide, publish the Bay Area first. Both federal files are statewide already. A Bay-Area-shaped ETL would have to be rewritten the first time Los Angeles is wanted.
The one that changes the shape

Group private practices are unreachable.

They are the largest employer of Bay Area associates and there is no public register of them — not by IRS status, not by license, not by anything. The associate directory can cover county, safety-net and nonprofit employers completely and the private sector not at all.

That has to be said on the page, in the first screen. A directory that looks complete and is missing the biggest category teaches people the wrong shape of the market.

02

Why it is two directories

This is not an editorial preference. The two readers have different legal universes, one strictly inside the other.

The practical consequence

Roughly half of what a Bay Area student is told to try — “ask around, someone’s supervisor has room” — points at private practices, which are the one setting the code rules out entirely for a trainee. The trainee directory’s single most useful function is being the list of places that are not that.

04

What may and may not be claimed

This is the section that decides whether the directory is worth having. Everything reachable describes what an organization is. Nothing reachable describes whether it has a seat.

May be published
  • That the organization exists, with its registered name and the address it filed
  • Which of the four universes it belongs to, and what that means under § 4980.43.3
  • Its county, and its size band from reported revenue
  • Its website — fetched before publication, the way the 218 health centers and 57 county plans already are
  • Whether the county it sits in is a designated mental health shortage area, and what the county pays its own clinicians
  • How to ask, and what to ask — including the question about who signs the school’s site agreement
May not be published
  • That an organization “takes trainees” or “accepts associates”
  • That it is hiring, or has openings
  • That it offers supervision, or free supervision
  • That it pays, or what it pays
  • Any named individual — a supervisor, a training director, a contact
  • Anything at all sourced from a program’s private approved-site list, which sits behind a login

Why the right column is strict

A wrong “yes” costs a student an application cycle at the exact moment they have least slack. There are 313 organizations; nobody can verify placement availability across them and keep it verified, and a directory that is 80% right about availability is worse than one that is 100% right about eligibility.

It is also the same discipline the loan-forgiveness page already runs under, where >Eligible<, >You qualify< and >Qualifies< are banned as verdicts by a build guard. The same guard should run here.

05

What to build

Three pages and roughly two dozen profiles — not six hundred. The argument against the long tail is the site’s own coverage audit.

Two directory pages

practicum-sites-bay-area.html
associate-employers-bay-area.html

Each one filterable by county and universe, each opening with the statutory line that defines its own list, each linking every organization to a website that was fetched before it shipped. The trainee page leads with the five program clinics, because they are the only entries where availability is certain.

One method page

how-to-find-a-practicum-site-california.html

The part that is actually scarce: what to ask, in what order, and what the school owes you. § 4980.42(e) puts the site agreement on the school, which is the single most useful sentence a student can carry into that conversation, and no directory row can say it for them.

About 22 profiles, leaf-flagged

the organizations over $5m

Where there is genuinely something to write: what they do, who they serve, which counties, how they are funded, whether they hold a county contract. Flagged ts:leaf like the 78 program pages and the 48 case studies, so they are reachable and indexed without burying a topic hub.

The argument against 313 pages

The visitor-stage coverage audit found that 36% of this library — 66 of 182 pages — served the stage with the least community engagement, because program reviews were cheap to generate. Three hundred thin organization pages built from four federal fields each would be the same mistake at twice the scale, and it would dilute the internal link graph that is the site’s whole position. Depth where there is something to say; a table everywhere else.

06

Bay Area, or statewide?

Build the data statewide. Publish the Bay Area first. These are not in tension and getting it the other way round costs a rewrite.

$0marginal cost of statewide data — both federal files are already the whole state, and the county filter is one line
Highmarginal cost of statewide editorial depth — the profiles and the method page are regional judgment, not a query
1rewrite avoided — a Bay-Area-shaped ETL has to be unpicked the first time Los Angeles is wanted

Los Angeles is the obvious second region and it is far larger — the health-center file alone carries 831 Los Angeles sites against 517 for the whole Bay Area. Sacramento, San Diego and the Inland Empire follow. If the builder takes a region as a parameter from the start, each of those is a configuration entry and a week of editorial work rather than a new project.

The Bay Area is the right first region anyway: nine counties, the densest concentration of programs with their own clinics, and the two counties with the highest published county pay in the state.

07

Two defects this probe found

Both on a page that shipped this morning. Looking at Bay Area counties specifically is what surfaced them.

Fixed

Contra Costa was ranked on six positions. It has 531.

The county pay pass matched job titles on spelled-out keywords. Contra Costa writes “Mh Clinical Specialist”, not “Mental Health Clinical Specialist” — 232 people in that one title. Santa Barbara writes “ADMHS Practitioner II” after its old department name, and was ranked on five.

Both counties were on the published table with a median computed from a handful of rows. That is worse than the undercount the method note already admits to, because it is a wrong number wearing a county’s name.

Contra Costa    6 positions, $124,928  →  531 positions, $118,997
Santa Barbara   5 positions, $109,824  →  215 positions, $101,820
statewide      12,850 matched      →  13,184 matched
headline spread   2.8×             →  2.8× (unchanged)

Fixed and redeployed. The top and bottom counties and the headline finding are unchanged, which is the reassuring part. A guard now prints a note for any county ranked on fewer than 25 positions, and the pass carries a comment saying the durable fix is to scope on the department name rather than keep adding acronyms — every county invents its own and a keyword list will keep losing that race.

Open

San Francisco is absent from the county pay page entirely.

Not under-counted — absent. There is no employer named San Francisco anywhere in the State Controller’s county file, because it is a consolidated city-county and files in the cities dataset instead. The page silently reports 55 of 58 counties and San Francisco is one of the missing three.

For a Bay Area directory that is not a footnote: San Francisco is one of the largest public behavioral health employers in the region and the page currently says nothing about what it pays. The fix is to pull the city file for the same three years and merge the one employer.

Queued, not fixed — the city file is a separate download and this pass never touches the network.

08

Cost, and the order

  1. Merge the San Francisco city file into county pay

    Smallest item here and it is a correctness fix on a live page, not a feature. It also fills the largest hole in the Bay Area picture before anything is built on top of that picture.

    One ETL change. No new pages.

  2. _dev/nonprofits.py — statewide, region as a parameter

    The IRS extract and the ZCTA crosswalk, reduced to a committed data module. Substance-use and non-service organizations separated rather than dropped, because an associate looking at a substance-use agency should know that is what it is.

    One data module. Nothing published yet, so nothing to get wrong in public.

  3. Domain verification, then practicum-sites-bay-area.html

    Every candidate URL fetched before it ships as a link, exactly as the 218 health centers were. The trainee page first because it is the smaller universe, the higher-confidence one, and the reader with the least help available anywhere else.

    One page. The five program clinics lead it.

  4. how-to-find-a-practicum-site-california.html

    The method page. Statewide, not regional — the questions to ask and what the school owes you do not change by county, and this is the page that would rank.

    One page, statewide, no new data.

  5. associate-employers-bay-area.html

    The larger universe, and the honest hole in it. Leads with the county and safety-net employers where the site already knows what the work pays, and says plainly that private practices are missing and why.

    One page, linked from the hiring and county pay pages.

  6. About 22 organization profiles

    Only the ones over $5m, only where there is something to write. Leaf-flagged. Stop when the writing stops being worth reading rather than when the list runs out.

    ~22 leaf pages behind two directories.

  7. Los Angeles, as the test of the parameter

    831 health-center sites against the Bay Area’s 517. If the second region is a configuration entry and a week of editorial work, the build was right; if it is a rewrite, it was not.

    Two more pages, no new machinery.

Net new: 5 pages plus about 22 profiles for the Bay Area, one correctness fix, and one new ETL. Everything else is a filter over data this site already holds and has already verified.
