Licensure · checked 13 August 2026 · 30 sources

Finding a clinical supervisor in California.

The Board does not certify supervisors and keeps no roster, so this is where the lists actually are.

California licenses 165,000 people and publishes a list of exactly zero supervisors. What exists instead is nine county chapter directories, one statewide association list with no contact details on it, and two commercial products.

In a private practice, you cannot simply hire your own supervisor.

The statute requires them to be employed by, contracted by, or an owner of your employer. Those are the three permitted statuses, and weeks under a privately retained supervisor are not creditable.

There is a lawful route, and it needs your employer to act. The employer contracts the supervisor, and a written oversight agreement is signed before supervision starts — not within any grace period.

What it costs

No fee schedule exists. The Board’s own 2024 survey found 18% of 3,168 respondents paid at all, and of those, 35% paid more than $300 a month.

The one thing to take away

A privately retained supervisor is not a supervisor, in a private practice.

Somebody who spends three months paying a supervisor they found themselves, at a practice that never contracted them, has bought nothing. Ask before the first session, not at the end.

Which one

A, with one component taken from C.

Ship A, and put C’s waterfall inside the tool card. A wins because it shows the product working before it explains anything, which is the whole Basecamp argument and the thing this site can do that no competitor can. What A is missing is the picture of the answer — the tool screenshot shows one number, and C’s waterfall shows where it came from. Put the waterfall directly under the two inputs and A carries both.

B is the one to keep in reserve. If the six paths become the primary navigation and traffic starts arriving on them rather than on the home page, the bento is the better front door and it can be swapped in without touching anything else.

On the infographics. All four are CSS — no images, no chart library, no build step. That matters more than it sounds: a chart that is HTML can carry real numbers out of the same data the pages are built from, so it cannot drift from the figures beside it. A PNG of a chart always eventually disagrees with the page it sits on.
