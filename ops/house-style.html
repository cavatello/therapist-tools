01

The system

Four borrowed moves, one rule on each. The rule matters more than the move — three of these four fail if they are used more than the rule allows.

BASECAMP

Restraint is the default

Tinted paper and never pure white. One accent. Flat fills with a hairline ring and a soft shadow rather than borders. And small type by marketing standards — 16.5px body, because these pages run six thousand words with tables in them, not four hundred words with a screenshot.

FIZZY

Six hues, one per path

Used as a 10px chip and a 4px left rule. Never as a background, never as body text, never on a button. The job is that a reader recognizes their path in a list without reading it, and that job is done with a rule and a dot.

CAMPFIRE

A made-by band closes anything that makes a claim

Home page, about page, and every article. Campfire ends a page with a handwritten signature; this ends it with the sentence that signature was there to support, and no name. The author does not want one on the page, so the band has to carry the job alone — who checked this, and what happens when it is wrong. That is a fair trade: the sentence was always doing more work than the signature was.

HEY

One slab per page. One.

Deep pine, scalloped top and bottom edges, and it goes to the single claim that page is making — not to a feature list. A second slab on the same page halves the value of the first, which is why the guard on this document fails a build that ships two.

Each clears 4.5:1 against the paper, so a hue may carry a label if it ever needs to. None of them is used as a fill behind body text, which is the failure mode of every color-coded navigation ever shipped.

What is deliberately not borrowed. No gradients, from anywhere. No 32px body text, which three of the four use and which would make a reference page unreadable. No full-page saturated canvas. No pill buttons — Basecamp’s 6px is the right amount of soft for something clicked twenty times a session.

What differs by registration

If you are an ASW, two rules apply that nobody else has.

1,700 of your 3,000 hours must be supervised by an LCSW, and 13 of your 52 individual or triadic weeks must be too. They are independent — meeting one does not meet the other. APCCs have no equivalent rule, and neither do AMFTs.

Finding a clinical supervisor in California.

The Board does not certify supervisors and keeps no roster, so this is where the lists actually are — and the rule that decides whether the person you find can count your hours at all.

California licenses 165,000 people and publishes a list of exactly zero supervisors. It does not approve them in advance, does not keep a register of them, and its license lookup has no supervisor field. What exists instead is nine county chapter directories, one statewide association list with no contact details on it, and two commercial products.

In a private practice, you cannot simply hire your own supervisor.

BPC §4980.43.4(b)(1) requires a supervisor of an associate in a private practice to be employed by, contracted by, or an owner of the associate’s employer. Those are the three permitted statuses. “Paid by the associate” is not one of them, and weeks under a privately retained supervisor are not creditable.

There is a lawful route, and it needs your employer to act. The employer contracts the supervisor, and a written oversight agreement is signed before supervision starts — not within any grace period.

Where the lists actually are

Every candidate was fetched on 13 August 2026. Nine of the twenty-three CAMFT chapters publish a supervisor list; fourteen do not, and two of the chapters that are supposed to carry one no longer exist as organizations.

What it costs

There is no fee schedule for supervision in California and no survey of what supervisors charge. What exists is one question in the Board’s own 2024 Pathway to Licensure survey: 18% of 3,168 respondents paid at all, and of those who did, 35% paid more than $300 a month.

A privately retained supervisor is not a supervisor, in a private practice.

The finding

Coverage is not where the people are.

Marin publishes 116 names and Redwood Empire 99, for a combined population under a million. San Diego, Sacramento, San Francisco, Santa Clara Valley, the San Fernando Valley and Ventura publish none at all — which is most of the state’s associates.

Checked, and there is nothing there

Nine places people are sent that publish no list: the Board itself, NASW California, CALPCC, CalSWEC, TherapyDen, Open Path, Alma and Headway, CounselingCalifornia, and CalMHSA — which buys supervision in bulk and sells it to counties, not to people.

Still ranked, no longer there

rrccamft.org resolves and lands on an unrelated commercial site — the domain was sold. sierrafoothillscamft.com and supervisiondirectory.com do not resolve at all. CAMFT’s own chapter-links page still points at two of them.

Counts are what each source reports about itself. Nothing from any of these directories is reproduced here, and a link is an address rather than a recommendation — this site has not met these supervisors and takes nothing from anybody for a mention.

How to build it

In an order where nothing has to be finished before anything else is useful.

STEP 1

The tokens and the two type sizes

One stylesheet. Everything above is eight color tokens, six hues, four heading sizes and one body size. It replaces the current palette rather than sitting beside it, so this is the step that cannot be half done.

STEP 2

The navigation, the footer and the signature block

Three components on 203 pages, all three generated by existing passes. The signature is a partial with one sentence that varies per page.

STEP 3

The home page

It is the page in section 02, and every link on it already exists. No URL moves.

STEP 4

The six path pages

Generated from the stages tagging already in registry.json. Path 3 stays folded into path 4 until it has content of its own — four pages is not a destination.

STEP 5

The article and directory templates

These two shapes cover roughly 190 of the 203 pages, so this is the step where the redesign is actually felt by a reader.

STEP 6

The email page, the about page, the slab audit

Last, and the audit is the part to not skip: walk every page and confirm it carries exactly one slab. The rule is the design.

What this does not decide. The typeface. Everything above is set in Inter, which is free, loads fast and is deliberately unremarkable — it is the right default and it is also the easiest thing to change later, because it is one token. If a licensed face is bought, buy it after the structure ships, not before.

And still true: a redesign and a move to Rails are two projects. This ships on the current static build; Rails would inherit finished templates.
