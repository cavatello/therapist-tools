Ten logos, all standing on something.

The icons were the last document. This is the logo — the whole lockup, and the question of whether the wordmark can carry the support idea by itself rather than borrowing it from a symbol standing next to it. The good ones do, which means no icon is needed at all: one less asset, and a mark that cannot be separated from the name. Each is shown horizontal, stacked, at 13px, reversed, and cropped to a favicon.

01

The idea, applied to type

Same brief as the icons — support in the load-bearing sense — but the name has to do the work. Three ways type can carry weight, and all ten below are one of them.

SIT ON

The name rests on a rule, a slab or a band

The Rule, the Plinth, the Stack, the Ground, the Shelf. The supporting element runs the full width or wider, so the name is visibly carried rather than merely underlined.

BE HELD

Something grips the name from the sides

The Bracket, the Column Rule. Weaker on brief, because holding from the side is a different physical idea from bearing weight — and the Column Rule is included to prove that.

CARRY

One part of the name holds the other

The Weight and the Underline. The cleverest of the three, and the most fragile: both depend on a contrast that disappears at small sizes.

THE RULE

Gold is always the thing doing the supporting

Carried over from the icon set. Ink is what is being held. It is what makes these a family rather than ten typographic ideas.

None of these is an image file. Every lockup below is the wordmark plus one or two CSS elements, sized in em so the whole thing scales from a single font-size. That means the header logo, the footer logo and the favicon are the same markup, and there is no export step and no second copy to drift — the same argument as the icons, and it applies harder here because a wordmark is the asset most often re-cut badly.

04

Which one

The Rule. The name over a two-weight baseline, ink above gold, running past the word on both sides. It is the only lockup here that needs no icon, no container and no color block — which means it works in an email signature, in plain text on a share card, and at 13px in a footer, all without being redrawn. It is also already the site’s own gesture: the marker underline that has been decoration since the first page becomes the structural idea.

The Ground is the one to test it against. It is the only lockup where the type and the supporting element actually overlap, so it reads as weight rather than as a line drawn nearby. It is stronger reversed than the Rule is, which matters because the footer is dark on every page.

And keep the Stack as the secondary. A two-line lockup on a base, for the share card, the app icon and anywhere the horizontal one would have to be set too small to read.

The one to be careful with. The Underline is the wittiest of the ten — a rule under the word Support — and on the web an underline under one word reads as a link. That is testable in about ten minutes with five people and worth testing before it ships, because if it reads as a link it is not a charming logo, it is a broken one.

What is not on brief: the Column Rule says pull-quote, and the Dotted Floor says form field — a dotted line is the visual language of something not yet filled in, which is the opposite of the stability being asked for. Both are here as controls, and their failure is evidence the brief is doing real work in the other eight.
