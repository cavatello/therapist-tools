The sentence that catches people out

“A trainee shall not perform services in a private practice or a professional corporation.”

That is section 4980.43.3(b)(1), and it is absolute. Not as an employee, not as a volunteer, not with a willing licensed supervisor who has room and would be glad to have you. An associate may work in a private practice once the Board has issued the registration. A trainee never can. People arrange the placement first and read this second.

What a trainee may do

Seven rules, and where each one lives.

These are the ones that decide whether a placement is usable. They sit in four different sections of the Business and Professions Code, which is most of the reason hardly anyone has read all of them.

1Not in a private practice, and not in a professional corporation.

The setting has to be something that is neither — and that “lawfully and regularly provides mental health counseling or psychotherapy” and provides oversight of your work. § 4980.43.3(b). Agencies, clinics, schools, county programs and nonprofits qualify; the solo practice down the road does not, however good the supervisor.

2An employee or a volunteer. Never an independent contractor.

“A trainee, associate, or applicant for licensure shall only perform mental health and related services as an employee or volunteer, and not as an independent contractor.” The hour and supervision rules apply identically either way, so a 1099 does not buy anybody flexibility — it just makes the hours uncountable. § 4980.43.3(a).

3No money from a client, ever.

You may be paid only by your employer. You may not take a fee, a co-pay or a gift from the person you are seeing, and you may not have any proprietary interest in the employer’s business, lease or rent space from it, or pay for its furnishings, equipment or supplies. § 4980.43.3(e) and (f). The second half of that is what rules out the arrangement where a trainee “rents a room” from a practice.

4Not before 12 semester or 18 quarter units.

No hour of experience counts if it was gained before you finished them. § 4980.43(c)(6). This is why practicum sits in year two of nearly every program on the list below.

5Enrolled in a practicum course while you are seeing clients.

With one exception, and it is worth knowing precisely: you may keep counseling clients through a gap in enrollment of fewer than 90 calendar days, if that gap is immediately preceded by a practicum course and immediately followed by another one or by finishing the degree. § 4980.42(c). That is the rule that lets a summer work.

6Your school must approve the site and hold a written agreement with it.

“The school shall approve each site and shall have a written agreement with each site that details each party’s responsibilities, including the methods by which supervision shall be provided.” It must also provide for regular progress reports and evaluations. § 4980.42(e). Read that next to the table below: even where you find the site, the agreement is the school’s obligation.

7Not supervised by a spouse, a relative or a domestic partner.

Nor by anyone with whom you have a personal, professional or business relationship that undermines the authority or effectiveness of the supervision. § 4980.43.3(d). The second clause is broader than people assume and it is the Board that decides, after the fact.

The one that costs the most to learn late

If you gained hours at a school other than the one that confers your degree, you carry the burden of proving to the Board that they were gained in compliance — not the school. § 4980.42(e), last sentence.

Which is a reason to keep your own copies of the site agreement, the supervision log and the weekly hour sheets from the first week rather than the last. The Board is reading them up to six years later, and what happens when a signature cannot be produced is a matter of public record.

Who finds your site

29 of 78 publish nothing about it at all.

Every one of the 78 programs on this site was read for a single question: when practicum comes, whose job is it to find the seat? Five answers were possible. This is how they fell.

Where a program runs two stages — an in-house clinic first, an external traineeship second — it is classified on the stage that carries the risk, because that is the one that can go wrong. The notes explaining those calls are printed with the programs below.

Read the first row and the last row together. 10 programs tell you plainly that finding the site is your job, which is hard but honest. 29 say nothing either way — and a prospective student comparing two schools cannot tell which kind of year they are buying. That is not the same as a bad program. It is a gap in disclosure about the highest-variance part of the degree.

The close calls

8 needed a judgment, and here it is.

  1. Daybreak University — not published Has an on-site counseling center and says time there counts toward the requirement, but never says every student trains there or that a seat is assured. Owning a clinic is not a placement model.
  2. San Diego State University — not published Genuinely two-stage. The first practicum is in-house at the Dede Alpert Center; the external traineeship, where SDSU says the majority of hours accrue, does not say who secures the seat. Classified on the stage that carries the risk.
  3. California Institute of Integral Studies — approved-site list Owning three clinics is not the same as holding a seat in one. CIIS's own Field Placement Office says practicums are competitive and advises applying to at least six sites. This is the reclassification that moved a featured school down, and it is the honest reading.
  4. Cal Poly Humboldt — program places you “You will be placed in CAPS or a community agency”, and the handbook has placements arranged by the student's faculty supervisor. The program drives it, so this is placed rather than in-house even though year one is in its own clinic.
  5. Cal Poly San Luis Obispo — program places you “Interviews are then arranged and, eventually, trainees are selected and placed.” The on-campus clinic is stage one; the program places the community traineeship.
  6. California Lutheran University — guaranteed Its own words: a 12-month placement in the on-site Community Counseling Center, described as providing the clinical training itself. There is no external site search in the published model at all.
  7. Chapman University — guaranteed “All students in our program” complete the practicum year in the on-site Frances Smith Center. “All” is the load-bearing word.
  8. Sentio University — guaranteed The only school on the list that states the guarantee explicitly and draws the contrast itself: students in good standing “receive guaranteed clinical training placement”, unlike “programs that offer 'practicum support' but leave students to find their own field placements”.

33 of the 78 run a clinic of their own

33 / 78

An in-house training clinic changes the shape of the year: the first hours happen in a building the program controls, with supervision it employs, and the placement question moves to the second stage rather than the first. It is the single most useful thing to ask about on a campus visit, and it is marked for every program in the table below.

It is not automatically better. A clinic seat is often part time and the caseload is whatever walks in, so a student who needs relational hours — the couples-and-families hours the Board requires 500 of — can find them harder to get there than at an agency.

Hours your program wants

Half the published minimums sit exactly on a number the state wrote.

42 of the 78 state a direct-client-contact minimum you must reach before they will graduate you. They range from 150 to 500 — and the two commonest values are not arbitrary.

A program asking for 280 rather than 225 is usually not asking more of you for its own sake. If you are certain you only want the LMFT, the extra 55 hours are still real hours in a real chair — they are simply not being asked for by the MFT statute.

The supervision ratio

A trainee is supervised twice as intensively as an associate.

Section 4980.43.2 sets the floor, and the trainee line is the strictest number in the whole chapter. If your site cannot meet it, the hours do not count — which is a question worth asking a site before you accept a seat, not after.

Direct supervisor contact, per setting, per week1 hour1 hour

Face-to-face means in person or two-way real-time videoconferencing, or a combination. A supervisor has to assess in writing, within 60 days of starting, whether videoconferenced supervision is appropriate for you at all — and may not use it if the assessment says no. § 4980.43.2(d).

The practical reading of row two: at a site where you carry ten direct counseling hours a week, a trainee needs two hours of supervisor contact and an associate needs one. Sites that take both sometimes budget supervision at the associate rate. That is the site’s error and your uncountable hours.

What counts toward 3,000

Only one of the three licenses lets your practicum count.

This is the single largest difference between the MFT track and the other two, it is decided before you enroll, and almost nobody states it plainly.

LicenseHours requiredMay any be earned before the degree?Where it says so
LMFT3,000Yes — up to 1,300, of which no more than 750 may be counseling and direct supervisor contact§ 4980.43(c)(4), (5)
LPCC3,000No. The statute says “3,000 postdegree hours”§ 4999.46(c)(1)
LCSW3,000No. The statute says “post-master’s degree supervised experience”§ 4996.23(a)

All three also require the experience to span at least 104 weeks, so banking pre-degree hours shortens the hour count and not necessarily the calendar. What it buys is slack: 1,300 fewer hours to find after graduation, in the period when you are also job hunting.

1,300the ceiling on pre-degree hours

§ 4980.43(c)(4). Everything you accrue as a trainee counts against it, including nonclinical time.

750the ceiling inside the ceiling

Of those 1,300, no more than 750 may be counseling plus direct supervisor contact. § 4980.43(c)(5). So a program that wants 500 direct client contact hours is already two thirds of the way through your pre-degree counseling allowance before supervision is added.

500the hours that never expire

Hours older than six years at the date the Board receives your license application do not count — except up to 500 hours of clinical experience gained in the required practicum, which are exempt. § 4980.43(c)(7). If life happens between the degree and the license, those are the ones that survive it.

40the weekly ceiling

No more than 40 hours in any seven consecutive days, at any stage. § 4980.43(c)(2). A site offering to sign for more is offering you hours the Board will strike.

Once you are registered, the arithmetic of the remaining hours is the 3,000-hour calculator, and what the Board will and will not credit from out of state is on the out-of-state page.

Degree to registration

Ninety days, and the clock starts at the degree award date.

The gap between graduating and holding an associate registration is the one stretch of the path with no institution looking after you. Three things decide whether it costs you anything.

1The Board must receive your application within 90 days.

Not postmark, not submit — receive, counted from the date the qualifying degree was granted. Do that and postdegree hours gained before the registration is issued still count, as long as the registration is subsequently granted. Miss it and every hour between the degree and the registration is gone. § 4980.43(b)(1)(A).

2The workplace must have required Live Scan before you started.

For anybody who completed graduate study on or after 1 January 2020, those pre-registration hours only count if the workplace required completed Live Scan fingerprinting before you gained them, and you file a copy of the form with your license application. § 4980.43(b)(1)(B).

3Still no private practice until the registration is issued.

§ 4980.43(b)(2) repeats the trainee rule for this window specifically. The 90-day grace period covers agency and clinic work; it does not open the private-practice door early.

What the wait actually is

The Board publishes how long it is taking to process each application type every quarter, and the numbers move a lot. The processing-time page tracks them, and the fee page covers what the application costs since the July 2026 reduction.

The other half of this window is the job. Why agencies hire pre-licensed clinicians at all is a billing rule rather than an hour count, and what those first jobs pay is on the associate pay page. If a placement or a first post asks you to work unpaid, the wage-claim page sets out what the law says.
