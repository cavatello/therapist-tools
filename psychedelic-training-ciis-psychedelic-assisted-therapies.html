California Institute of Integral Studies — Center for Psychedelic Therapies and Research (CPTR)

Certificate in Psychedelic-Assisted Therapies and Research

The oldest and largest university-based psychedelic therapy training in the US — 12 months, ~150 hours, $8,900–$9,400 — that teaches you about psilocybin and MDMA but only qualifies you to practise ketamine-assisted psychotherapy and integration, unless you pay extra for the Oregon psilocybin-facilitator track.

What it is

CIIS created the Center for Psychedelic Therapies and Research in 2015 and describes it as "the first-ever university-affiliated psychedelics certificate program." The first cohort — 41 students — graduated on 4 December 2016. CIIS says the certificate has now trained "over 1,700 professionals," which makes it by a wide margin the longest-running and highest-volume programme of its kind in the United States. Founding director Dr. Janis Phelps still runs it.

The year is eight class weekends between October and May, plus two required virtual pre-programme sessions in late summer (Orientation, and a separate class on Ethics, Safety, and Legalities in the Field). CIIS publishes 140–150 hours of instruction — roughly 150 for the hybrid San Francisco cohort, 140 for the online cohort — plus 30 hours of individualised learning and mentor consultation and an estimated 120 hours of reading, which the FAQ translates to 6–8 hours a week. The hybrid cohort meets in person in San Francisco for three four-day weekends (Thursday afternoon through Sunday lunchtime) and online for five Saturdays; the online cohort does the same calendar entirely by video conference, with the January in-person Holotropic Breathwork weekend available as a $600 add-on. Everyone takes a final exam. Trainees are placed in "Home Groups" of 7–9 for the whole year and assigned a mentor.

The content is weighted toward the two drugs furthest through FDA review — CIIS says the programme "thoroughly covers clinical work with MDMA and psilocybin with additional coursework on ketamine-assisted psychotherapy" — which is precisely the mismatch a working California clinician needs to understand before paying. Of the three, only ketamine is prescribable in the US today. The experiential component of the training is Holotropic Breathwork, guided imagery, self-hypnosis, expressive artwork, drumming and rattling inductions, and role-plays drawn from real psychedelic therapy sessions. No psychedelic drug is administered to students at any point, and CIIS states this explicitly in its own materials.

Eligibility widened substantially in 2025. It is no longer a licensed-clinician-only programme: CIIS now accepts applicants with an advanced degree or graduate study in non-clinical fields (social sciences, humanities, sciences, law and others) and applicants holding only a bachelor's degree in those fields. CIIS's own announcement says non-clinical participants are being trained to be "secondary support as facilitators in required two-person dyads during psychedelic-assisted therapy sessions." Licensed and ordained professionals — the list explicitly includes LMFTs, LCSWs, LPCCs and psychologists — remain the "prioritized" tier. Residents of twelve states (including DC, New Jersey, North Carolina, Minnesota and Wisconsin) cannot enrol at all, for state-authorisation reasons.

What it lets you do

This is the section the programme’s own marketing is least specific about, so it runs before everything else. It was established against the programme’s own materials and against the two state registers that matter — Oregon’s approved-curriculum list and Colorado’s.

This does not change your licence or your scope of practiceIt is a credential from a school, not from a regulator. California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognises nor requires it, and holding it adds nothing to what you may lawfully do as an LMFT in this state.
With this you can
  • Keep practising exactly as you already do. Nothing about your BBS licence, your scope of practice, your supervision obligations or your title changes. This is a credential issued by a university, not by a regulator; the California Board of Behavioral Sciences neither requires it nor recognises it as conferring anything.
  • Do psychedelic integration work — preparation and, more commonly, after-the-fact psychotherapy with clients who have used psychedelics on their own. This is the single most immediately usable outcome, and the honest framing is that it needs no special credential at all: it is ordinary psychotherapy. What the certificate buys here is competence and referral credibility, not permission. CIIS frames it exactly this way, saying graduates gain "an enhanced ability to serve people in our culture who have used psychedelics."
  • Work as the therapist on a ketamine-assisted psychotherapy team. Ketamine is the only psychedelic-adjacent medicine a California therapist can be part of administering today, because it is an FDA-approved anaesthetic prescribed off-label. CIIS teaches ketamine pharmacology, therapeutic interventions, drug interactions and "strategies for creating a ketamine clinic," and gives trainees a list of further ketamine trainings that do include an experiential component. In May 2026 CIIS opened its own university-affiliated KAP clinic in San Francisco (three-hour sessions at roughly $400), staffed in part by CPTR graduates.
  • Claim up to 30 hours of continuing education acceptable to the California BBS, for an extra $50–80 per weekend. CE is provided by the Spiritual Competency Academy, an APA-approved sponsor; the BBS accepts APA-approved-sponsor CE for LMFT, LCSW, LPCC and LEP renewal. Up to 10 hours of CME is separately available.
  • Be a materially more credible applicant to psychedelic clinics, trial sites and research teams. CIIS claims graduates have been chosen as study therapists by Heffter, MAPS and Usona researchers — that is CIIS's claim, and CIIS itself qualifies it as "dependent on the unfolding regulatory processes, the attainment of protocol-specific training, and the available number of therapist researcher positions."
  • Optionally, and only by paying more and travelling: apply for an Oregon psilocybin facilitator licence. CIIS is a genuinely OHA-approved training programme (Training Program ID #TP-ce7dd84c), independently confirmed on the Oregon Health Authority's public approved-programmes list on 6 August 2026 — one of 15 programmes listed. That requires completing the optional Oregon subcohort's 40 in-person hours in Portland and passing a CIIS Oregon exam. Oregon licenses non-residents, so a California LMFT can hold this licence — but all psilocybin work must be done at an OHA-licensed service centre in Oregon.
  • Teach, write, present, consult, screen, and refer knowledgeably — the ordinary professional dividends of a year of structured study with the people who ran the trials.
You still cannot
  • Cannot administer, supply, sit with, or in any way provide psilocybin, MDMA, ayahuasca, or any other Schedule I psychedelic in California. Both psilocybin and MDMA remain Schedule I federally as of August 2026 and neither is approved by the FDA. California has repeatedly declined to build a therapeutic framework: SB 58 was vetoed in 2023, SB 1012 was pulled in 2024, and a Senate committee killed SB 751's veterans/first-responder psilocybin pilot in May 2025. The one psychedelics bill Newsom did sign in October 2025 (sponsored by Asm. Christopher Ward) only lets California's Research Advisory Panel review Schedule I research proposals faster — it changes nothing about clinical practice.
  • Cannot prescribe or administer ketamine. In KAP the therapist is not the prescriber; a physician, nurse practitioner or physician assistant prescribes and is responsible for the medical side. This certificate does not change that and does not give you any medication authority.
  • Does not expand your BBS scope of practice, does not shorten or substitute for any part of the California licensure path, and does not entitle you to any protected new title. "Psychedelic-assisted therapist" is not a regulated credential in California.
  • Does not qualify you to facilitate in Colorado. CIIS does not appear on Colorado DORA's list of Approved Natural Medicine Training Programs — verified 6 August 2026 against the 16 programmes then listed (which do include Fluence, Numinus, InnerTrek, Changa Institute, Entheogen Institute, CU Denver's Center for Psychedelic Research and others). A Colorado facilitator licence would require enrolling in a different, Colorado-approved programme.
  • The base certificate on its own does not qualify you for Oregon either. Without the optional Oregon subcohort, the additional 40 in-person hours in Portland and the CIIS Oregon exam, an Oregon facilitator licence application is not available to you.
  • Does not by itself make you a clinical-trial study therapist. Sponsors set their own protocol-specific training requirements; CIIS says so in its own outcomes language.
  • Does not include any legal psychedelic experience for the student. CIIS provides none, and says so: the experiential curriculum is Holotropic Breathwork, guided imagery, self-hypnosis, expressive artwork, drumming and rattling, and role-play. If you want a legal personal psychedelic session, this programme is not where you get one — CIIS instead hands out a list of other trainings that include an experiential component.
  • Does not make you eligible to enrol at all if you live in one of twelve restricted states (Alabama, Arkansas, Delaware, DC, Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Mississippi, New Jersey, North Carolina, North Dakota, Wisconsin) — a state-authorisation limit, not a scope limit, but a hard bar.
  • Does not confer academic credit toward a degree. It is a non-degree professional certificate; CIIS notes elsewhere that Continuing Education Units are not transferable as academic credit.
It is important to note that at no time does the program promote or require the use of psychedelic drugs in any manner, in accordance with the CIIS Student Handbook and CIIS Academic Catalog policies, as well as state and federal law. Trainees taking the certificate will learn about the competencies required of therapists (licensed clinicians) and facilitators (unlicensed trained professionals) in federally approved medical treatment and psychedelic research, as well as key aspects of guidelines in decriminalized states and regions.— California Institute of Integral Studies — Center for Psychedelic Therapies and Research (CPTR), in its own materials

Cost, length and who can apply

Cost2026–27 cohort, published by CIIS: Hybrid San Francisco cohort $9,400 (includes Holotropic Breathwork); fully Online cohort $8,900. Non-refundable application fee $150. Optional extras: $600 for online-cohort trainees who want the in-person Holotropic Breathwork weekend in SF; the optional Oregon OHA coursework in Portland is billed by the service centres, which CIIS says "will likely charge from $2,000 to $2,600"; continuing education credits cost an extra $50–80 per weekend (up to 30 CE hours). No separate deposit is published — CIIS instead says the first tuition payment is due within three weeks of the admission offer or by June 1, 2026, whichever comes first, and that "the first payment will secure your space in the program." Payment plan: yes — CIIS's tuition page states there are "both tuition payment options: one or three payments," with the detail in a gated Information Packet. For comparison, the prior 2025–26 cohort was $8,900 hybrid (Boston and San Francisco) / $8,300 online / $500 breathwork / $650 Oregon coursework — a roughly 6–7% year-on-year increase, and the Oregon add-on rose sharply.
Length12 months. Eight class weekends, October through May, plus two required virtual pre-programme sessions in August and September.
Hours140–150 hours of professional instruction (~150 hybrid, ~140 online), plus 30 hours of individualised learning and mentor consultation and an estimated 120 hours of preparatory reading. Hybrid trainees spend roughly 12 days in person in San Francisco across three four-day weekends. The optional Oregon track adds 40 hours of in-person instruction in Portland.
FormatTwo cohort formats. Hybrid (San Francisco): three four-day in-person weekends (Oct 8–11 2026 welcome; Jan 28–31 2027 including Holotropic Breathwork; May 20–23 2027 closing) plus five online Saturday classes (Nov 7, Dec 5, Feb 27, Mar 27, Apr 24). Online: two four-day online weekends, one two-day online class, and five online Saturdays, on the same calendar. Both cohorts: mandatory virtual Trainee Orientation (Sat Aug 22, 9–11am PT) and Ethics, Safety, and Legalities in the Field (Sat Sep 19, 9am–12pm PT). Learning formats include lectures, small-group discussion, review of recorded therapy sessions, "flip class" pre-watched lectures, Home Groups of 7–9 trainees, and a mentor assigned for the year. A final exam is required.
WhereSan Francisco, CA — hybrid (three in-person weekends in San Francisco) or fully online; optional add-on training in Portland, Oregon
Who can applyAs of the 2025 expansion, three tiers, all requiring at least a bachelor's degree. (1) An advanced degree (M.A. and above) that can lead to clinical licensure, ordainment or commission. (2) An advanced degree or current graduate study in clinical or related fields — medicine, mental health and allied health, social sciences, humanities, sciences, law "or other affiliated disciplines (case by case)." (3) A bachelor's degree in those fields. CIIS lists 22 "prioritized" credentials, which include Marriage and Family Therapists (LMFT), Clinical Social Workers (LCSW), Professional Clinical Counselors (LPCC/LPC), Clinical and Counseling Psychologists (PhD/PsyD), Physicians and Psychiatrists (MD/DO), Nurse Practitioners, Registered Nurses (BSN or higher), Physician Assistants, Naturopathic Doctors, Acupuncturists, Chiropractors, Pharmacists, Occupational, Physical and Speech Therapists, Certified Midwives, Behavioral Analysts, Advanced Alcohol and Drug Counselors, Counseling Nutritionists, Attorneys in counselling specialties, TCM practitioners, and Ordained Clergy / Commissioned Chaplains with an M.Div. The non-clinical track is not a therapist track: CIIS's own announcement says these participants train to be "secondary support as facilitators in required two-person dyads during psychedelic-assisted therapy sessions." Application materials: a 2–4 page Background and Goal Statement answering seven set questions (one of which asks directly what you would tell a colleague whose client wants to use unlawful psychedelics in their practice), a CV, credential documents (licence, ordination papers, or transcripts), and contact details for two references — CIIS says it prefers to phone references rather than read letters. Admitted applicants sign an Integrity Agreement and a Beloved Community Initiative Agreement. Residents of Alabama, Arkansas, Delaware, District of Columbia, Iowa, Kansas, Minnesota, Mississippi, New Jersey, North Carolina, North Dakota and Wisconsin are ineligible for either cohort. International applicants are explicitly welcomed; Phelps says 130 international trainees have come through.

What you would study

  1. Module 1 — Research Foundations and Current Trends

    Resurgence in Research: Psychedelic-Assisted and Entactogen-Assisted Therapy; Pharmacology and Neuroscience; Legal Issues and Regulatory Processes; Contemporary Clinical Research on Classic Psychedelics, MDMA and Ketamine; Current Trends and Controversies.

    source ↗
  2. Module 2 — Practices for Transformation of Consciousness

    Sacraments, Indigenous Wisdom Traditions, and Mystical Experiences; Psychedelics and Entactogens: Community, Spirituality, and Wellness; Studies of Consciousness; Group Ceremonial Traditions.

    source ↗
  3. Module 3 — The Facilitator/Therapist: Core, Cultural and Ethical Competencies

    Core Competencies and Healing Presence of Facilitator; Cultural Competency, Cultural Humility, and Structural Competency; Access and Equity in Psychedelic Healthcare and Research for Diverse Populations and Under-Resourced Communities; Ethics, Safety Issues, and Support Skills.

    source ↗
  4. Module 4 — Therapeutic Processes

    Group and Individual Formats; Meaning Making: Preparation; Psychedelic Session; Integration: Practices and Goals.

    source ↗
  5. Module 5 — Experience of Guiding and Being Guided in Expanded States of Consciousness

    Holotropic Breathwork; Guided Imagery, Expressive Artwork, and Self-Hypnosis; Role-Play Exercises from Actual Psychedelic Therapy Sessions; Drumming and Rattling Inductions. This is the programme's entire experiential component — none of it involves a drug.

    source ↗
  6. Module 6 — Mentoring Program

    Mentor–Trainee Consultation: each trainee works with a mentor for the duration of the programme. CIIS describes mentors as "advanced practitioners with clinical, facilitation, and/or research expertise," and the FAQ specifies 30 hours of individualised learning and mentor consultation, with trainees meeting their mentor at least monthly. Professional development discussions, role plays and networking.

    source ↗
  7. Required cross-cutting sessions (not numbered as modules)

    A mandatory virtual Trainee Orientation and a separate mandatory three-hour class on "Ethics, Safety, and Legalities in the Field," both held before the programme proper begins. A final exam at the end of the year: "By passing the exam, trainees will demonstrate competence and understanding of important concepts in the field of psychedelics and psychedelic-assisted therapies."

    source ↗
  8. Optional Oregon subcohort (additional cost, additional travel)

    40 hours of in-person instruction in Portland, Oregon: didactic training, observation of psilocybin sessions with groups or individuals supervised by senior certified facilitators, and consultation work at CIIS-approved Oregon licensed service centres. Followed by a CIIS Oregon exam. Only this track leads to an Oregon facilitator licence application.

    source ↗

Who teaches it

Faculty is a large part of what these programmes actually sell, and it changes between cohorts — check the current list before you decide on this basis.

  • Dr. Janis Phelps — founder (2015) and still Director of the Center. A CIIS clinical psychology professor before founding it; she is the person most associated with the programme publicly (quoted in the NYT, National Geographic, NPR, the WSJ and CapRadio on the Center's behalf) and she authored the widely cited framing of psychedelic therapist 'core competencies'.
  • Laura Pustarfi — Executive Director of Certificates and Continuing Education (administrative lead across CIIS certificates).
  • Kelson Pamarang — Assistant Director of Admissions for Certificate Programs; the named admissions contact and the person whose Calendly the site links for prospective-student meetings.
  • Michael Mithoefer, M.D. and Annie Mithoefer, B.S.N. — the husband-and-wife team who ran MAPS' MDMA-assisted therapy trials and largely wrote the MDMA therapy protocol. On the faculty since the earliest cohorts.
  • Rick Doblin, Ph.D. — founder of MAPS; the central political figure of the modern MDMA effort, including its 2024 FDA rejection.
  • William A. Richards, Ph.D. and Brian D. Richards, Psy.D. — Johns Hopkins psilocybin researchers; Bill Richards is one of the few people who worked with psychedelics both before and after prohibition and wrote 'Sacred Knowledge'.
  • Anthony P. Bossis, Ph.D. — NYU; ran the psilocybin/end-of-life-anxiety trials, and the CIIS faculty member NPR featured in its 2023 story on chaplains entering the field.
  • Charles Grob, M.D. — UCLA; ran the first modern US psilocybin cancer-anxiety study.
  • Jeffrey Guss, M.D. — NYU psychiatrist, a prominent voice on psychedelic therapy ethics and on the therapeutic relationship.
  • David E. Nichols, Ph.D. — medicinal chemist, founder of the Heffter Research Institute; effectively the field's pharmacology authority.
  • Rachel Yehuda, Ph.D. — Mount Sinai; leading PTSD and trauma-biology researcher.
  • Mary Cosimano, M.S.W. — Johns Hopkins; has guided more psilocybin sessions than almost anyone alive.
  • Dennis McKenna, Ph.D. — ethnopharmacologist, ayahuasca researcher.
  • Dr. Beatriz (Bia) Caiuby Labate — anthropologist, executive director of Chacruna; the main academic critic-within-the-field on Indigenous reciprocity and appropriation.

  • Diane Haug, M.A., LPCC — senior Grof Transpersonal Training facilitator; leads the Holotropic Breathwork weekend.
  • Torsten Passie, M.D. — German psychiatrist and historian of psychedelic medicine.
  • Devon Christie, M.D. — Canadian physician, Numinus; MDMA and ketamine clinical training.
  • Charles Raison, M.D.; Peter Hendricks, Ph.D.; Stephen J. Haggarty, Ph.D.; Franklin King IV, M.D.; Albert Yeung, M.D. — academic research faculty (Wisconsin, UAB, Harvard/MGH).
  • Andrew Penn, M.S., PMHNP; Julie D. Megler, NP; Kwasi Adusei, DNP, PMHNP — psychiatric nurse practitioners, i.e. the prescriber side of a KAP team.
  • Courtney Watson, LMFT; Urana Jackson; Shirley Strong; Sizwe Andrews-Abakah; Mizan Alkebulan-Abakah; Ayize Jama-Everett — faculty associated with the programme's access, equity and Beloved Community strand. Phelps says one third of instructors and mentors are people of colour.

  • Anja Loizaga-Velder, Ph.D.; Susana Bustos, Ph.D.; Gogo Ekhaya; Dawn D. Davis — Indigenous and traditional-practice strand.

Where it stands right now

Not currently enrolling. Verified 6 August 2026: the programme page states "Applications for the 2026-2027 cohort are now closed. Our 2027-2028 application cycle will open on January 15, 2027." The 2026–27 cohort runs 8 October 2026 – 23 May 2027, with required virtual sessions on 22 August and 19 September 2026. Note that the same page contradicts itself further down, in the tuition section, saying "The 2026-2027 application cycle is open through July 13, 2026" — a stale line; the banner is the operative one.

What I could not establish

These are the things I looked for and could not establish. Take them to the programme, and notice how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • THE VIDEO THE SITE OWNER SUPPLIED IS THE WRONG ONE. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OozCl_m-Fi8 is 'Integral Counseling Psychology at CIIS | A Mind, Body, Spirit Approach to Therapy' — a different CIIS programme entirely, nothing to do with psychedelics. Verified via YouTube's oEmbed API on 6 August 2026. Replaced with the Janis Phelps CPTR video above.
  • No applicant numbers, seat count, or acceptance rate is published anywhere. The programme's reputation for being heavily oversubscribed could not be confirmed from any source. The only hard cohort figure found is Phelps' 'we've grown from 45 students to as many as 300' and the 7–9-person Home Group size. If the directory wants to claim it is competitive, that claim currently has no citable basis.
  • No deposit figure is published for this certificate. CIIS lists enrollment deposits for degree programmes ($125 undergraduate, $300–500 graduate) but not for CPTR; the certificate instead uses 'first tuition payment secures your space,' due within three weeks of the admission offer.
  • The exact terms of the one-payment vs. three-payment plan (amounts, dates, whether interest or fees apply) are only in a gated 'Information Packet' that requires submitting a Request Info form. Not obtainable without giving CIIS contact details.
  • No current BIPOC- or equity-specific scholarship is published. CIIS' present offering is a single partial, need-based scholarship allocated first-come, first-served on a 1-page financial-need statement (deadline 1 June 2026, decisions rolling through 15 August 2026), with more than $1.4 million dispersed since 2016. There WAS a named diversity fund: on 22 March 2017 CIIS announced $32,750 for scholarships for trainees 'from underrepresented communities,' including $15,000 from the Sarlo Foundation establishing the 'Ricci Coddington Diversity Scholarships,' plus full-tuition gifts from trustee Betsy Gordon and Robert Barnhart. That fund does not appear in any current tuition or scholarship material, and CIIS does not say whether it still exists.
  • The Boston training site is unaccounted for. CIIS opened it in 2021 and the 2025–26 tuition table still reads 'Hybrid Boston and San Francisco Cohorts,' but the 2026–27 schedule offers only San Francisco hybrid and online. CIIS does not say whether Boston has been discontinued.
  • No completion rate, final-exam pass rate, or post-certificate employment data is published — only anecdotes from the director.
  • No independent student reviews found. Reddit and Trustpilot are blocked from this environment, and no journalism reviewing the certificate from a student's perspective surfaced. Every evaluative quote available about this programme is either from CIIS itself or from CIIS quoting someone.
  • The independent 'Psychedelics/Therapy Assessment Group' audit CIIS announced in April 2022 has no published findings. CIIS said to 'look for updates in the coming months'; four years on, none were found.
  • YouTube upload dates, durations and descriptions could not be retrieved (rate-limited); titles and channel ownership were verified via oEmbed.
