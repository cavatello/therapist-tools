The Embody Lab

Somatic Psychedelic Facilitator Certificate

A $1,047 self-paced online certificate that teaches somatic and harm-reduction skills for preparation, sitting and integration — open to anyone, licensed or not, and it changes no one's scope of practice.

What it is

This is a 50-hour, entirely online, entirely on-demand certificate. There is no cohort, no start date, no application and no admissions screening — you buy it and get lifetime access to a video library. Three modules of 12 hours each (Preparation, Facilitation Skills, Integration) plus pre-recorded extras and three hours of reflection and self-practice work. It is the cheapest and lowest-commitment thing in this directory by an order of magnitude, and it should be understood as a well-taught set of lectures rather than as training that qualifies you to do anything new.

The faculty list is genuinely strong and is most of what you are paying for: Bessel van der Kolk, Peter Levine, Gabor Maté, Richard Schwartz, Gül Dölen, Devon Christie, Atira Tan, Licia Sky, Chara Caruthers and Elder Duncan. Three further faculty — Rae Johnson, Scott Lyons and 'Michaela' — are named as running live Q&A support sessions. The content genuinely does what the title says: it maps somatic-psychology skills onto the three phases of a psychedelic session. Preparation covers screening tools, consent and containment, somatic intention-setting and resourcing. Facilitation covers a framework for supportive touch and touch alternatives, music, therapeutic ritual and cultural appropriation, transference and countertransference, rupture and repair, dosage and the psycholytic/psychedelic distinction, and risk mitigation. Integration covers a somatic integration model — normalizing, regulation, meaning-making, metabolising, transpersonal support, action planning — plus practitioner self-care.

It is open to non-clinicians, and this matters more than the marketing suggests. The 'who this is for' list runs to twenty-odd categories including coaches, yoga teachers, massage therapists, meditation teachers, clergy, educators and 'other wellness professionals' alongside psychologists, social workers, physicians and nurses. The Embody Lab is explicit: the program 'caters to a diverse audience, including licensed therapists and unlicensed practitioners.' On completion it tells graduates the title they may use is 'Somatic Psychedelic Facilitator.' That title is not regulated, not protected and not recognized by any licensing board in California or anywhere else in the United States. A California LMFT taking this course should understand that they will hold the same certificate as a yoga teacher who took the same videos, and that the certificate confers nothing on either of them beyond the knowledge. What a licensed therapist has, and the yoga teacher does not, is a license — and the license is what governs what either of them may legally do.

Two practical cautions. First, the page you buy is titled for a 2025 cohort and reads like the archived version of what was once a live program: the FAQ says 'an orientation session was only provided for the live program,' 'scholarships were only available for the live program' and 'captioning was only available during the live sessions.' There is no separate 2026 cohort page — this product URL is the only Somatic Psychedelic Facilitator listing on The Embody Lab's site as of 6 August 2026. Second, the continuing-education claim needs reading twice. The program page says '50 hours — this program offers 50 hours of continuing education credits,' but the FAQ says the APA continuing education (48 hours, via co-sponsor Spiritual Competency Academy, which the California BBS accepts for LMFT, LCSW, LPCC and LEP renewal) applied to the live program and that '*Live* attendance was required.' If BBS-countable CE is why you are buying this, confirm in writing before you pay.

What it lets you do

This is the section the program’s own marketing is least specific about, so it runs before everything else. It was established against the program’s own materials and against the two state registers that matter — Oregon’s approved-curriculum list and Colorado’s.

This does not change your license or your scope of practiceIt is a credential from a school, not from a regulator. California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognizes nor requires it, and holding it adds nothing to what you may lawfully do as an LMFT in this state.
With this you can
  • Offer psychedelic integration work in California. This is the honest core use of the certificate. Integration is talking to a client about an experience they had somewhere else, and for a licensed therapist it is ordinary psychotherapy — no special credential required, and the somatic integration model taught here is directly usable.
  • Bring somatic, touch-framework and harm-reduction skills into ketamine-assisted psychotherapy, which is the only psychedelic modality a California therapist can currently practice. The therapist does not prescribe; a physician, NP or PA evaluates and prescribes, and the therapist does preparation, sitting and integration.
  • Use the material as harm-reduction and screening literacy — how to talk to clients who are already using psychedelics outside a clinical setting, what psychological flags to watch for, when to refer.
  • Possibly count it toward California BBS continuing education, but verify first. APA CE (48 hours) was offered through co-sponsor Spiritual Competency Academy, and the California BBS accepts APA-sponsor CE for LCSW, LPCC, LEP and LMFT renewal — but the FAQ states that live attendance was required for those CEs, while the program page advertises '50 hours of continuing education credits.' These two statements are not reconcilable from the page alone.
  • Use the title 'Somatic Psychedelic Facilitator,' which The Embody Lab explicitly grants. Understand that it is a marketing title, not a credential.
You still cannot
  • Change your license or scope of practice. The California Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognizes nor requires this certificate, and holding it adds nothing to an LMFT, LCSW, LPCC or psychologist license.
  • Legally administer, supply or sit with psilocybin, MDMA, LSD or ayahuasca for clients in California. Psilocybin and psilocyn are Schedule I under California Health & Safety Code §11054(d)(18)-(19); MDMA-assisted therapy has no FDA approval following the August 2024 complete response letter to Lykos Therapeutics, and no psychedelic other than ketamine is available to prescribe as of August 2026.
  • Qualify for the Oregon psilocybin facilitator license. The Embody Lab does not appear on the Oregon Health Authority's list of training programs with approved curriculum (checked 6 August 2026, 15 programs listed).
  • Qualify for the Colorado natural medicine facilitator license. The Embody Lab does not appear on Colorado DORA's list of approved Natural Medicine Training Programs (checked 6 August 2026).
  • Give an unlicensed graduate any legal ability to provide mental health treatment. A coach or yoga teacher who completes this certificate and then offers 'somatic psychedelic facilitation' involving a Schedule I substance is committing a crime and, if they present it as treatment, potentially unlicensed practice as well. The certificate does not insulate them and it does not insulate a licensed therapist who supervises or partners with them.
  • Substitute for supervised, hands-on training. The program says so itself.
  • Provide any live, in-person or experiential component. There is none. Everything is video.
Upon successful completion of our 50-hour certificate program in "Somatic Psychedelic Facilitation," participants will gain a foundational understanding of the intricate interplay between somatic psychology and psychedelic therapy. This program is not just a culmination but a beginning, marking the initiation of a lifelong learning journey. Cost, length and who can apply

Cost$1,047 non-member, one-time payment. $894 for members; membership is being promoted at $10 for the first 30 days then $26/month, so a first-year member route works out cheaper than the non-member price. Payment plans offered at checkout (terms not published). No application fee. Registration Guarantee: full refund up to 48 hours before the course start date, no questions asked.
LengthSelf-paced with lifetime access. No fixed duration, no cohort, no registration deadline.
Hours50 hours total: three modules of 12 hours each (36 hours), plus pre-recorded video content and 3 hours of reflection and self-practice work. Certificate completion additionally requires 10 hours of practice coaching sessions arranged outside class time, self-evaluation and reflection forms for each module, and a conversation with another student about the program.
FormatOnline, self-paced, on demand. All content sits in a content library available on registration, with downloadable video and audio, PDFs and English transcripts. Three live faculty Q&A support sessions (Rae Johnson, Scott Lyons and Michaela) are listed for 30 September, 15 October and 5 November, recorded and added to the library within 48 hours. An online community forum is included.
WhereFully online — self-paced, on demand
Who can applyOpen to everyone. No license, degree, prerequisite or admissions process. The site lists the intended audience as psychedelic facilitators, psychedelic support care workers, integration practitioners, social workers, mental health and pastoral counselors, yoga therapists and yoga teachers, movement therapists, coaches, physicians (MD, DO, ND, DC), physician assistants, nurses and nurse practitioners, psychologists, emergency medical personnel, meditation teachers, educators, massage therapists, acupuncturists and body workers, hypnotherapists, kinesiologists, complementary and alternative medicine professionals, clergy and religious leaders, and other wellness professionals.

What you would study

  1. Module 1 — Somatic Psychedelic Preparation (12 hours)

    Part 1 theory: a theoretical orientation to somatic psychedelic-assisted therapies; core principles of somatic psychology; mindfulness/embodiment science and its intersection with psychedelic science; clinical versus ceremonial contexts; asynchronous reviews of psychedelic research history, drug classes (classical, empathogens, dissociatives, atypical), macro versus microdosing, traditional plant medicines and Indigenous histories, and mechanism theories (neurochemical, connectome/DMN). Ethics, duty of care and informed consent, framed around 'the enhanced and unique vulnerabilities of participants seeking psychedelic-assisted therapies.' Part 2 skills: five elements of preparation (creating a healing context/set and setting, psychoeducation, consent and agreements, somatic intention setting, somatic resourcing); building a screening approach using team-based care, a list of psychological flags and assessment of a participant's socio-environmental safety nets; working within an interdisciplinary team and within scope of practice; a preparation checklist.

    source ↗
  2. Module 2 — Psychedelic Facilitation Skills (12 hours)

    Part 1 theory: overview of a psychedelic session; Kolb's experiential learning cycle applied to somatic psychedelic sessions; a framework for supportive touch and touch alternatives; principles for using music; therapeutic ritual with cultural safety and sensitivity, and avoiding cultural appropriation; trauma-informed language and the difference between reparative/corrective and retraumatising experiences; risk mitigation and safety strategies during the session; co-therapy and individual versus group facilitation; transference and countertransference, rupture and repair, and appropriate self-disclosure; dosage considerations across substances including the difference between psychedelic and psycholytic therapy; immediate post-session care. Part 2 skills: therapeutic presence, invitational embodied inquiry, interoceptive self-awareness, dual awareness and tracking, somatic communication, and using states of awareness to contain, protect and shape the space.

    source ↗
  3. Module 3 — Somatic Psychedelic Integration (12 hours)

    Part 1: post-session sensitivity, enhanced learning and neuroplasticity; assessing integration needs (normalizing, metabolising, regulation, meaning-making, connection and community, transpersonal/spiritual needs, action planning); embodied integration via Kolb's cycle, bottom-up processing and somatic anchoring; steps for integration recovery and 'understanding a practitioner's scope of practice'; self-directed integration versus integration therapeutic support. Part 2: applying the steps of the Somatic Integration Model; supporting new patterns during the window of enhanced learning; therapist self-care practices.

    source ↗
  4. Homework and completion requirements

    3 hours of reflection and self-practice work; watching all program videos; self-evaluation and reflection forms per module; 10 hours of practice coaching sessions outside class times; a meaningful conversation with another student about the program; full payment.

    source ↗

Who teaches it

Faculty is a large part of what these programs actually sell, and it changes between cohorts — check the current list before you decide on this basis.

  • Bessel van der Kolk, MD — author of The Body Keeps the Score; the best-known name on the roster and the main draw for many buyers.
  • Peter Levine, PhD — originator of Somatic Experiencing.
  • Gabor Maté, MD — physician and author on trauma and addiction, long associated with ayahuasca-adjacent work.
  • Richard Schwartz, PhD — developer of Internal Family Systems, the parts model most commonly paired with psychedelic work.
  • Gül Dölen, MD PhD — neuroscientist whose work on psychedelics reopening critical periods of neuroplasticity is the mechanistic argument behind most integration-window claims.
  • Devon Christie, MD — clinician with hands-on MDMA and ketamine-assisted therapy trial experience; the most directly relevant practical voice on the list.
  • Atira Tan — trauma therapist and the lead voice on the somatic-psychedelic curriculum.
  • Licia Sky — somatic and embodied-movement educator, frequent collaborator of van der Kolk's.
  • Chara Caruthers — yoga therapist and somatic educator.
  • Elder Duncan, PhD — listed lead faculty.
  • Rae Johnson, Scott Lyons and 'Michaela' — named as the three faculty who run the live Q&A support sessions.

Where it stands right now

Enrolling — verified 6 August 2026. Sold as an on-demand product with lifetime access and no registration deadline. The URL still carries a '2025' slug and the FAQ describes the live cohort in the past tense; there is no separate 2026 cohort page. This is the only Somatic Psychedelic Facilitator product listed on The Embody Lab's site (checked against their full certificate collection and product sitemap).

What I could not establish

These are the things I looked for and could not establish. Take them to the program, and notice how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • Whether the on-demand version currently carries any CE that the California BBS would accept. The program page and the FAQ contradict each other, and no CE certificate sample or SCA co-sponsorship page for the on-demand version was found.
  • The year of the three live support sessions (30 September, 15 October, 5 November are listed with no year), and whether they still run for on-demand purchasers.
  • The full identity of 'Michaela,' one of the three named live-support faculty — only a first name is given.
  • Enrollment numbers, completion rates, or how many people hold the certificate.
  • Any independent review or journalism about this specific certificate. Google News returned nothing about The Embody Lab's psychedelic program; Trustpilot returned HTTP 403; Reddit is unreachable from this environment; general web-search engines were blocked. This is a genuine gap rather than a finding that no criticizm exists.
  • Whether the 10 hours of required 'practice coaching sessions' are supervised, peer-to-peer, or self-arranged — the FAQ does not say, and elsewhere the same FAQ says 'there were no homework assignments for the program,' which conflicts with the stated 3 hours of reflection work.
