Integrative Psychiatry Institute (IPI)

IPI Psychedelic Therapy Level I Certification (Psychedelic-Assisted Therapy Training)

A four-month, fully online, 140-hour certificate that explicitly states it is not approved for Oregon or Colorado licensure — but IPI's separate $3,000 psilocybin practicum in Eugene turns it into a combined program that does appear on Oregon's approved-curriculum list.

What it is

IPI runs what is probably the highest-volume psychedelic therapy training aimed at working clinicians in the US: it claims 2,200+ providers trained. The current offering is 'Level I Certification', a four-month online course running 4 September to 11 December 2026. Nothing about it requires travel. You get a set of asynchronous 'Foundations' modules before the start date, two one-day live virtual intensives that bookend the course, a weekly 60-minute Tuesday lecture, a bi-weekly two-hour Thursday seminar in breakout rooms, a weekly hour-long small group, and a private forum. Everything is recorded and stays available for a year after the program ends. IPI counts the whole package as 140 hours, 55 of them live.

The tuition is $7,000, discounted to $5,000 for registrations before 31 August 2026, with payment plans discussed on an admissions call. That buys the online course and two advertised bonuses: 40 hours of recorded real ketamine and psilocybin sessions, and a 20-hour 'Psychedelic Therapy B-School' on running a practice. Continuing education is real and externally accredited: up to 49.0 credits through AKH Inc., which is jointly accredited by ACCME, ACPE and ANCC, with psychologist credit through APA and social-work credit through ASWB ACE. For a California LMFT that matters — the BBS recognizes APA and ASWB as CE approval agencies, and requires 36 CE hours per two-year renewal.

The experiential component is a separate purchase, not part of the certificate price. IPI operates its own Oregon psilocybin service center, Conscious Encounters, in downtown Eugene, and sells a 'Certificate in Psilocybin-Assisted Therapy': a 4-day, 40-hour practicum, Tuesday to Friday, capped at 24 people, for $3,000 (payment plan $500/month), plus $46–$90 paid on-site for the psilocybin itself because Oregon rules require the service center to sell it separately. You get one personal dosing session (up to 35mg psilocybin analyte) and act as student facilitator in a second, with Oregon-licensed facilitators as the primary facilitators. This is the only in-person, hands-on part of IPI's offering.

The scope claim on the sales page is unusually careful for this field — IPI says in plain text that Level I is not approved by Colorado DORA or the Oregon Health Authority. That is true of Level I on its own. But OHA's live list of training programs with approved curriculum does contain an IPI entry, listed as 'IPI Psychedelic Assisted Therapy Training with an Advanced Certificate in Psilocybin-Assisted Therapy', registered to Niwot, CO, pointing at this same page. So the combined online course plus psilocybin practicum is an OHA-approved curriculum; the online course by itself is not. IPI's public marketing does not spell this distinction out, and a prospective student should get it in writing from both IPI and OHA before paying.

What it lets you do

This is the section the program’s own marketing is least specific about, so it runs before everything else. It was established against the program’s own materials and against the two state registers that matter — Oregon’s approved-curriculum list and Colorado’s.

This does not change your license or your scope of practiceIt is a credential from a school, not from a regulator. California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognizes nor requires it, and holding it adds nothing to what you may lawfully do as an LMFT in this state.
With this you can
  • Keep practicing as a California LMFT, with a school-issued certificate that says you completed a 140-hour course. It is a credential from IPI, not from a regulator.
  • Count up to 49.0 continuing education credits toward your BBS renewal. IPI's CE is provided through AKH Inc. (jointly accredited by ACCME, ACPE and ANCC), with psychologist credit via APA and social-work credit via ASWB ACE — and the California BBS recognizes APA and ASWB as CE approval agencies for its 36-hour-per-renewal requirement. That is the one concrete, regulator-recognized thing this certificate does in California.
  • Practice ketamine-assisted psychotherapy in California, working alongside a physician, NP or PA who prescribes and is responsible for the ketamine. You do not prescribe. Nothing about the certificate creates this ability — an LMFT can already do KAP in collaboration with a prescriber — but the training is aimed squarely at it, and IPI is candid that ketamine is the only modality available now.
  • Offer psychedelic preparation and integration therapy, which is ordinary psychotherapy about a client's experience and needs no special credential.
  • Have a personal, legal psilocybin experience and a supervised turn as facilitator, if you also buy the $3,000 Eugene practicum. Under Oregon's framework this is psilocybin services at a licensed service center, not therapy.
  • Pursue an Oregon psilocybin facilitator license off the back of it — but only via the combined pathway. OHA's live list of training programs with approved curriculum includes 'IPI Psychedelic Assisted Therapy Training with an Advanced Certificate in Psilocybin-Assisted Therapy'. Approved curriculum is one of six requirements; you also need to be 21+, hold a high school diploma, pass a criminal background check, complete the program's practicum and comprehensive exam, and pass an OHA-administered exam.
  • Be more credible to a ketamine clinic that hires. IPI is the highest-volume trainer in this space and clinic job listings recognize the name; that is a market fact, not a regulatory one.
You still cannot
  • Change your BBS license, scope of practice or title in any way. The BBS does not issue, recognize or require a psychedelic-therapy credential, and does not maintain a list of them.
  • Administer, prescribe, supply or possess psilocybin or MDMA in California. Both remain Schedule I federally and there is no California therapeutic framework — SB 58 was vetoed in October 2023 and the state's 2025 action was a bill to accelerate study of psychedelics for PTSD, not to legalise therapeutic use.
  • Use the Level I certificate on its own for Oregon or Colorado licensure. IPI says so itself, verbatim, on the sales page: Level I 'is not an approved training' by DORA or OHA.
  • Get anywhere with Colorado. IPI does not appear on DORA's list of Approved Natural Medicine Training Programs (checked 6 August 2026).
  • Practice as a psychotherapist while facilitating in Oregon, if you go that route. OHA is explicit: licensed facilitators 'may not practice on other professional licenses while providing preparation, administration, or integration sessions to clients.' An Oregon facilitator license is a separate, non-clinical role — it is not LMFT work with mushrooms.
  • Do anything with MDMA outside a research protocol or MAPS' expanded access. The FDA issued a complete response letter on Lykos' MDMA-assisted therapy application in August 2024 and there has been no approval since. IPI's own page says MDMA and psilocybin 'may only be a year away from a full FDA approval' — that is IPI's marketing claim, not a regulatory position, and similar claims have been made for several years.
While ketamine is the only nationwide psychedelic treatment available, MDMA and Psilocybin may only be a year away from a full FDA approval. However, Level I certification is not an approved training by the Colorado Department of Regulatory Agencies (DORA) for purposes of licensure under the Colorado Natural Medicine and Natural Medicine Product Act, nor is it an approved training by the Oregon Health Authority (OHA) for licensure under the Oregon Psilocybin Services Act.— Integrative Psychiatry Institute (IPI), in its own materials

Cost, length and who can apply

CostRegular tuition $7,000; 'Registration Discount until August 31, 2026' of $5,000. 'Additional discounts and payment plans may be available on your admissions call.' The 4-day psilocybin practicum in Eugene, Oregon is a separate $3,000 (or $500/month over 6 months), and does not include the psilocybin itself, which the service center must sell on-site separately for an estimated $46–$90.
Length4 months. The 2026 cohort runs 4 September 2026 (Virtual Intensive I) to 11 December 2026 (Virtual Intensive II). The optional psilocybin practicum is 4 days, scheduled separately. Note the URL still says 'year-long' — a legacy slug; the program described on the page is four months.
Hours140 hours of learning, of which 55 hours are live virtual sessions. Advertised on top of that: 40 hours of recorded real psychedelic therapy sessions and a 20-hour business module. The Eugene psilocybin practicum is 40 hours over four days, 8:00am–6:00pm.
FormatFully online. Asynchronous 'Foundations of Psychedelic Therapy' modules available before the start date; one-day Live Virtual Intensive I (4 Sept 2026) and II (11 Dec 2026); weekly 60-minute online lectures (Tuesdays 9:00am MT); bi-weekly 120-minute large group seminars in breakout rooms (Thursdays 9:00–11:00am MT); weekly 60-minute small 'home group' Zooms; a private community forum. All sessions recorded, library access for one year after the program ends. The only in-person element is the separately-priced Eugene, Oregon psilocybin practicum.
WhereNiwot, Colorado — training delivered fully online; the only in-person component is a separately-priced 4-day psilocybin practicum in Eugene, Oregon
Who can applyOpen to LMFTs. IPI states it is 'now accepting applications from' Professional Clinical Counselors (LPC), Clinical Psychologists (PhD/PsyD), Physicians (MD/DO), Nurse Practitioners (NP), Marriage and Family Therapists (LMFT), Clinical Social Workers (LCSW), Physician Assistants (PA), and Pre-licensed Therapists (MA). It is not restricted to prescribers — the majority of the named alumni and testimonials on the page are LMFTs, LCSWs and LPCs. Pre-licensed associates are explicitly eligible. Admission runs through a 'See If I Qualify' form and an admissions call.

What I could not establish

These are the things I looked for and could not establish. Take them to the program, and notice how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • IPI does not publish a page describing the 'Advanced Certificate in Psilocybin-Assisted Therapy' under that name, even though that is the exact title under which the program is listed on Oregon's approved-curriculum list. The nearest public page is the 'Certificate in Psilocybin-Assisted Therapy' practicum page. The precise combination of components OHA has approved is not stated anywhere publicly.
  • The page says the program 'focuses on eight modules' but only six module blocks are published. The other two are not named.
  • CE credit figures are inconsistent between IPI's two versions of the sales page: the main page says 'up to 49.0 continuing education credits'; the /psychedelic2/ variant says 'We are anticipating approval of 20 CE/CME credits for the Foundations of Psychedelic Therapy asynchronous portion'. It is not clear how the two relate or which applies to the 2026 cohort.
  • No refund or cancelation policy is published for the main program.
  • No published class size for the online program (the Eugene practicum is capped at 24).
  • The '4.7 out of 5 from over 2,200 practitioners' rating has no stated source, platform or sample.
  • Independent review sources are unavailable from this environment: Reddit and Trustpilot both returned 403.
  • Whether IPI's Oregon service center and practicum are currently operating in 2026 is not fully clear — the practicum page carries dated language ('as early as next year') and IPI's own internal page title marks it 'Expired'.
