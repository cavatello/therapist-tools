What it is

Read the organizational chart before the curriculum. Naropa University does not offer this program any more. In December 2024 Naropa announced the separation and renaming of the Naropa Center for Psychedelic Studies into an independent 501(c)(3), the Memoru Center for Visionary Healing Arts, a collaboration between the NCPS founding team and Boulder MDMA researchers Marcela Ot'alora G., Bruce Poulter and Sara Gael Beauregard. All NCPS staff and operations moved. Naropa's own transition FAQ gives the reason plainly: 'Because Naropa receives federal funding and is subject to federal regulations, we cannot administer these substances or operate related clinics.' What remains at Naropa is a BA Psychedelic Studies minor, some existing graduate courses, and an exploratory natural-medicines track in the Master of Divinity. The Naropa PFT page is still live and still advertises a cohort that started 21 January 2025 with applications that closed 24 November 2024 — a stale page, not a current offering. If you are pricing this program in 2026 you are pricing Memoru.

The regulatory position is the reason this record matters, and it is genuinely different from almost every other training in this set. Colorado has a real, operating, licensed facilitator profession under Proposition 122 and SB23-290, administered by DORA's Division of Professions and Occupations. As of 6 August 2026 the DORA list of Approved Natural Medicine Training Programs includes 'Memoru Center for Visionary Healing Arts, Boulder, CO' with didactic, practicum training and consultation all marked Yes — one of only about fifteen approved programs statewide. 'Naropa University' does not appear on that list. So the training pathway is intact, it just has a different name on the certificate.

Oregon, however, has fallen away. Naropa's page still claims Oregon approval, and the December 2024 press release said Memoru 'has inherited Naropa's Colorado state certification to offer psilocybin training and transferring the Oregon certification is in process'. It did not complete. Oregon Psilocybin Services' live list of training programs with approved curriculum, checked 6 August 2026, contains fifteen programs and neither Naropa nor Memoru is among them (UC Berkeley's BCSP and CIIS both still are). Memoru's own training page now says only that its program 'is designed to prepare facilitators for licensure in Colorado and we remain open to complying with additional state models in the future'. Treat every Oregon claim on the Naropa page as expired.

What Colorado licensure actually costs you in hours: 150 hours of approved didactic training with a topic-by-topic minimum set by rule (25 hours ethics and Colorado natural medicine rules, 25 hours physical and mental health and state, 10 each on relational boundaries and touch, trauma-informed care, Indigenous/social/cultural considerations, preparation, administration, integration, group facilitation and facilitator self-care, 5 each on best practices, drug effects and interactions, suicide risk and screening), plus 40 hours of supervised practice (the practicum), plus 40 hours of consultation with a licensed Consultant, of which 10 must be on ethical issues, completed within two years of the Training License being issued. You also need to be 21+, hold current Basic Life Support certification, clear criminal-history screening, complete the Healthcare Professions Profile, and pay a non-refundable application fee. Notably, DORA does not publish a state examination requirement for facilitators — that is an Oregon feature, not a Colorado one. Naropa's page states a 50-hour consultation figure; DORA's own March 2025 facilitator guide says 40, so use 40. Licenses run to 31 January annually and continuing education is required — DORA held a stakeholder meeting on 20 August 2026 on revising the CE rule, so treat the exact annual CE hours as in flux.

For a California LMFT, LCSW or LPCC the answer to 'what can I do with this' is: work in Colorado, on Colorado's terms, under a Colorado license, and nothing at all in California. Colorado's Facilitator tier has no clinical-license requirement, so a Californian can complete the training and license as a plain Facilitator — but a Facilitator explicitly 'cannot engage in psychotherapy, the practice of medicine, or any secondary profession that would require the use of touch beyond the scope allowed by 4 CCR 755-1 6.6, Supportive Touch'. The higher Clinical Facilitator tier, which does let you practice your secondary profession alongside natural medicine services, requires an active Colorado license as a PSY, LSW, LCSW, LMFT, LPC, LAC, MD, DO, APN, NP or PA. A California LMFT license does not satisfy that; you would need Colorado licensure. Colorado also offers licensure by endorsement to someone holding an Oregon facilitator license in good standing for a year plus a valid Colorado secondary license.

What it lets you do

This is the section the program’s own marketing is least specific about, so it runs before everything else. It was established against the program’s own materials and against the two state registers that matter — Oregon’s approved-curriculum list and Colorado’s.

This does not change your license or your scope of practiceIt is a credential from a school, not from a regulator. California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognizes nor requires it, and holding it adds nothing to what you may lawfully do as an LMFT in this state.
With this you can
  • Actually lead to a real state license — which almost nothing else in this field does. Complete the 150 didactic hours with a DORA-approved program, 40 practicum hours and 40 consultation hours, hold current Basic Life Support certification, clear criminal-history screening, complete the Healthcare Professions Profile and pay the application fee, and Colorado will license you as a Natural Medicine Facilitator.
  • Legally administer psilocybin to adults in Colorado once licensed, and conduct preparation, administration and integration sessions — the three components of natural medicine services — for participants whose safety screen does not indicate the need for a medical or behavioral health provider.
  • Be completed by a Californian. There is no Colorado residency requirement for the Facilitator tier: eligibility is 21+, high school diploma or equivalent, US resident. The didactic phase is delivered live online, so the travel burden is the practicum and any in-person retreats.
  • Be stacked onto a Colorado clinical license for the Clinical Facilitator tier, which uniquely allows you to practice your secondary profession simultaneously while providing natural medicine services. A California LMFT would first need Colorado LMFT licensure.
  • Be reached by endorsement from Oregon: Colorado grants Clinical Facilitator licensure by endorsement to applicants who hold a valid Colorado license in their secondary field plus an Oregon facilitator license held in good standing for one year.
  • Give you a real, current grounding in the operational parts of the job — screening, informed consent, safety planning, dosing, de-escalation, group work — which generic 'psychedelic-assisted therapy' certificates skip.
You still cannot
  • Do anything for you in California. A Colorado facilitator license has no effect on a California BBS license or scope, and psilocybin remains Schedule I federally and unlawful for therapeutic use in California. California has no regulated access program: SB 58 was vetoed in October 2023, and the 2025-26 bills (AB 1103, SB 751, SB 1224, AB 2489) are research-funding and study measures.
  • Be obtained from Naropa University. Naropa transferred the program to the independent Memoru Center in December 2024 and does not appear on Colorado DORA's approved-program list. The Naropa web page describing this program has not been updated since late 2024.
  • Qualify you for Oregon. Neither Naropa nor Memoru appears on Oregon Psilocybin Services' list of training programs with approved curriculum as of 6 August 2026, despite the Naropa page's Oregon claims and a December 2024 statement that the Oregon transfer was 'in process'. Memoru's current training page claims Colorado only.
  • Let you do psychotherapy inside the psilocybin work, if you hold only the plain Facilitator license. DORA is explicit: 'A facilitator cannot engage in psychotherapy, the practice of medicine, or any secondary profession that would require the use of touch beyond the scope allowed by 4 CCR 755-1 6.6, Supportive Touch.'
  • Be practiced remotely. Licensed facilitation happens in Colorado, in person, under Colorado's regulatory structure — which means relocating, commuting, or building a bi-state practice.
  • Be budgeted for yet, because no tuition figure is published for the didactic program, the practicum or the consultation, and DORA does not publish its license fee schedule on the public site.
Psilocybin facilitation under the state regulatory adult use model is an emergent and evolving field. Naropa's non-degree PFT certificate program is designed to prepare facilitators for licensure in Colorado and Oregon. Because regulations vary state by state, additional training may be required for credentialing depending on the legal context of the facilitator's intended practice location. The future trajectory and guidelines for psilocybin facilitation vary by region, regulatory agency, and protocol, and are subject to changes in federal, state, and local guidelines.— Formerly the Naropa Center for Psychedelic Studies, Naropa University; separated from the university in December 2024 and now operating independently as the Memoru Center for Visionary Healing Arts, Boulder, CO, in its own materials

Cost, length and who can apply

CostNo tuition figure is published anywhere. Naropa's PFT program page, its application-and-policies page (which now redirects to the psychedelic-studies transition FAQ), Memoru's training page at memorucenter.org and Memoru's site at memoru.com all describe the program without stating a price. The practicum and consultation are separately priced add-ons and were listed as 'Dates and Costs TBD' even when Naropa ran the program. Colorado's DORA application fee is described as required and non-refundable but the amount is not published on DORA's public web pages. The one price Memoru does publish is unrelated: its consultation/supervision group with Sara Lewis, PhD, LCSW is $60 per group for pre-licensed therapists and $80 for licensed therapists.
LengthSix months of didactic training, plus a separately scheduled 40-hour practicum and then 40 hours of consultation spread over the two years following the Training License.
Hours150 hours of didactic instruction (Colorado's rule minimum) + 40 hours supervised practicum + 40 hours consultation, of which 10 hours must address ethical issues arising in the trainee's work. Weekly workload during the didactic phase: a two-hour live class, a two-hour Pod group twice monthly, and four to five hours of independent work.
FormatHybrid. As run by Naropa: virtual, with required live attendance — weekly live classes on Zoom, Wednesdays 5:00-7:00pm Mountain Time, plus small-group 'Pods' on the second and fourth Tuesday of each month with three time-slot options (12:00-2:00, 4:00-6:00 or 6:00-8:00pm MT) assigned with time zones in mind, plus asynchronous content, reflection journalling and assignments. Attendance at live classes and Pods was mandatory. Memoru now describes the format as 'online learning + in-person retreats' with supervised practicum, consultation and ongoing mentorship.
WhereBoulder, CO — six-month hybrid program, live online plus in-person components; practicum in person in Colorado
Who can applySet to match the state rules rather than to gatekeep clinically: at least 21 years of age, a high school diploma or equivalent, US resident, and a professional license in good standing only if applicable — that is, only for those pursuing Colorado's Clinical Facilitator tier. No clinical degree, no prior therapy experience and no psychedelic experience are required for the plain Facilitator tier. Colorado's Clinical Facilitator tier additionally requires an active Colorado license as a Psychologist, LSW, LCSW, LMFT, LPC, LAC, MD, DO, APN/NP or PA — a California license does not qualify.

What you would study

  1. Module 1 — Traditional Roots, Healing Justice, Health Equity

    Traditional cultural use and decolonial approaches to psilocybin facilitation; racial justice and intergenerational trauma; empathy versus compassion and facilitator self-care.

  2. Module 2 — Presence, Ethics, Harm Reduction, Boundaries

    Facilitator best practices and core competencies; mitigating risk; abuse of power and ethical facilitation.

    source ↗
  3. Module 3 — Trauma-Informed Care, Establishing Rapport, Screening and Intake

    Trauma physiology; trauma-informed communication skills; determining client eligibility; safety considerations and referral in screening.

    source ↗
  4. Module 4 — Preparation, Set and Setting, Safety Planning

    Developing therapeutic rapport; informed consent; developing a safety plan.

    source ↗
  5. Module 5 — Administration, Navigating Challenging Experiences

    Dosing strategies and subjective experience; challenging behaviors in administration sessions; traumatic stress, de-escalation and safety in administration sessions.

    source ↗
  6. Module 6 — Integration, Group Work

    Integration modalities and resources; boundaries and group dynamics in group facilitation; providing individual care and navigating challenging experiences in group administration sessions.

    source ↗
  7. Add-on — Practicum (40 hours)

    Required for licensure in both Oregon and Colorado; 'an in-depth opportunity for trainees to facilitate a non-ordinary state of consciousness experience under the guidance of a qualified supervisor'. Naropa listed an Oregon option at the Cora Center in Portland and a Colorado option, both with dates and costs TBD, with registration prioritizing in-state residents. Memoru now lists Practicum as an approved offering on the Colorado DORA register.

    source ↗
  8. Add-on — Consultation (Colorado)

    Colorado requires post-training consultation before licensure. DORA's current guidance sets this at 40 hours, of which 10 hours must focus on ethical issues arising in the trainee's course of work and the remainder a mix of case review and structured learning, all completed within two years of the Training License being issued. Memoru is registered with DORA as offering consultation.

    source ↗
  9. Colorado rule-set the 150 hours must cover

    Facilitator Best Practices (5h); Ethics and Colorado Natural Medicine Rules and Regulations (25h); Relational Boundaries and Introduction to Physical Touch (10h); Physical and Mental Health and State (25h); Drug Effects, Contraindications and Interactions (5h); Introduction to Trauma-Informed Care (10h); Introduction to Suicide Risk (5h); Indigenous, Social and Cultural Considerations (10h); Screening (5h); Preparation (10h); Administration (10h); Integration (10h); Group Facilitation (10h); Facilitator Development and Self-Care (10h). These are minimums; programs may exceed them.

    source ↗

Where it stands right now

Running, but under a different organization. Verified 6 August 2026: Naropa University's own pages state the Center for Psychedelic Studies separated in December 2024 and that its certificate programs moved to the Memoru Center for Visionary Healing Arts; Naropa's PFT page is stale and still advertises the January-June 2025 cohort with applications closed 24 November 2024. Colorado DORA's Approved Natural Medicine Training Programs list carries 'Memoru Center for Visionary Healing Arts, Boulder, CO — Didactic Yes, Practicum training Yes, Consultation Yes' and does not carry Naropa. Oregon Psilocybin Services' approved-curriculum list carries neither. Memoru is publicising a 2026 Psychedelic Facilitator Training with recorded info sessions but has not published dates or tuition.

What I could not establish

These are the things I looked for and could not establish. Take them to the program, and notice how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No tuition published for the didactic program, the 40-hour practicum or the 40 hours of consultation — not by Naropa, not by Memoru at memorucenter.org, and not at memoru.com.
  • Colorado's DORA license application and renewal fee amounts are not published on DORA's public web pages; the guides say only that a non-refundable fee is required. (Oregon's, by contrast, are public: $150 application plus a $2,000 annual license fee, $1,000 reduced.)
  • No 2026 cohort start or end dates announced by Memoru.
  • Whether Memoru intends to pursue Oregon curriculum approval at all, having said in December 2024 that the transfer was in process and having since narrowed its published claim to Colorado only.
  • Colorado's annual continuing education requirement is in flux — DORA scheduled a stakeholder meeting for 20 August 2026 on revising Rule 5.4 CE requirements for Facilitators and Clinical Facilitators. Naropa's page cites 20 hours a year; do not rely on that figure.
  • Naropa's page says 50 hours of consultation over six months; DORA's own March 2025 facilitator guide says 40 hours within two years of the Training License. The discrepancy is a rule change, and Naropa's page was not updated.
  • No independent reviews, alumni interviews or journalism about the PFT program specifically could be found — only coverage of Naropa's broader psychedelic expansion and its 2024 spin-out and 2026 layoffs.
  • Cohort size, completion rate, and how many graduates have actually obtained a Colorado license are not published by anyone.
  • Could not verify any FDA action on MDMA-assisted therapy after the August 2024 complete response letter within reachable sources; this record assumes psilocybin and MDMA remain Schedule I federally and that no FDA-approved MDMA product exists.
