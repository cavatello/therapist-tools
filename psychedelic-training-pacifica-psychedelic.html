Psychedelics and Depth Psychology certificate series (Level 1 introductory; Advanced Level 2)

What it is

Pacifica does have psychedelic-specific programmes, but they are continuing-education courses, not a facilitator credential, and it is important not to confuse the two. They live in Pacifica Extension — the institute's non-degree arm — as a two-level series called 'Psychedelics and Depth Psychology'. Level 1, 'Treatment and Transformation in an Animated Cosmos', ran 16 weeks (May-August 2025) for 14 CE credits. Level 2, the 'Advanced Psychedelics Graduate Certificate', ran 12 weeks (September-December 2025) for 10 CE credits. Both are $1,095 at the general rate, both are delivered as a weekly pre-recorded lecture plus a two-hour live Zoom discussion, and Level 1 is explicitly not a prerequisite for Level 2. Despite the words 'Graduate Certificate' in the title these are non-credit Extension offerings — the course page says so bluntly: 'This Certificate neither requires nor confers a license or degree.'

What you actually get is a very good reading list and unusual access to people who built the field. The Level 2 faculty includes Michael Mithoefer, MD, who ran the first MAPS Phase 2 MDMA trial and supervised therapists on the Phase 3 trials; Rosalind Watts, PhD, former clinical lead of the Imperial College psilocybin-for-depression trial and author of the ACE/ACER integration models; Saj Razvi, a former MAPS Phase 2 clinical researcher and co-developer of Psychedelic Somatic Interactional Psychotherapy; Neil Hanon, MD, who leads Grof Legacy Training in Canada; Jungian analyst Linda Carter on ketamine as an adjunct to analytic work; and psychiatrist Brian Stafford, MD, MPH, who anchors both levels. Level 1 added Robin Carhart-Harris, Bill Richards, Janis Phelps (founder of the CIIS programme), Bia Labate and Michael Winkelman. The framing throughout is depth-psychological and mythopoetic — Jung, Grof, Hillman-adjacent language, IFS, ecopsychology — rather than protocol-and-checklist clinical training.

For a California licensee the practical value is narrow but real and cheap. Pacifica is an approved CE sponsor for the California Board of Behavioral Sciences via CAMFT (provider #60721), so the 10 or 14 hours count toward LMFT, LCSW, LPCC and LEP renewal, and separately for psychologists through the California Psychological Association (PAC014) and for RNs (CEP 7177). CE credit costs an extra $30 and requires attendance at every live Zoom session — recordings alone earn the certificate of completion but not the CE hours. Discounts exist: $930.75 for Pacifica alumni and seniors, $876 for Lifelong Learner members, $657 for Pacifica Extension students on Level 1, with a 50% deposit option, instalments, and a limited scholarship pool.

The honest headline is that Pacifica runs retreats, short courses and CE certificates rather than a facilitator training credential, and that it has never sought or held state approval to train psilocybin facilitators. There is no practicum, no supervised client contact, no consultation hours, no admissions screening beyond payment, and nothing that any regulator recognises. There is also nothing currently scheduled: as of 6 August 2026 the Level 2 course is listed with dates 'TBD' under Coming Soon on Pacifica Extension's programme index, and Level 1 sits under 'Previous Programs'. Earlier one-off offerings — the eight-week 'Sacred Medicine and the Psyche' (2021, $595-$895, run with the Institute of Transpersonal and Archetypal Studies) and the two-day 'Psychedelia' film symposium (2021, co-sponsored by Chacruna, Fluence and MAPS) — are likewise archived. Note also that Pacifica's retreat site, retreat.pacifica.edu, now redirects to the Extension site; there is no separate psychedelic retreat programme.

What it lets you do

This is the section the programme’s own marketing is least specific about, so it runs before everything else. It was established against the programme’s own materials and against the two state registers that matter — Oregon’s approved-curriculum list and Colorado’s.

This does not change your licence or your scope of practiceIt is a credential from a school, not from a regulator. California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognises nor requires it, and holding it adds nothing to what you may lawfully do as an LMFT in this state.
With this you can
  • Bank 10 or 14 hours of California BBS-qualifying continuing education toward LMFT, LCSW, LPCC or LEP renewal for an extra $30 on top of tuition — Pacifica is a CAMFT-approved CE provider, #60721 (and CPA PAC014 for psychologists, BRN CEP 7177 for nurses).
  • Get a genuinely well-taught theoretical grounding in psychedelic-assisted therapy models from people who ran the pivotal trials, without pretending it is clinical training.
  • Do better preparation and integration work with California clients, which is legal psychotherapy and requires no special credential of any kind.
  • Bring depth-psychological, Grofian, IFS and somatic framing to ketamine-assisted psychotherapy in California — the only psychedelic modality a California therapist can practise today, and one where the therapist provides the psychotherapy while a physician, NP or PA handles the prescribing.
  • Add a recognisable, low-cost line to a CV while deciding whether a $10,000-plus facilitator training is worth it.
You still cannot
  • Change your licence or scope of practice in any way. Pacifica states it directly: 'This Certificate neither requires nor confers a license or degree.' The California BBS neither recognises nor requires it.
  • Qualify you to facilitate psilocybin anywhere. Pacifica is not on the Oregon Health Authority's list of programmes with approved curriculum, nor on Colorado DORA's list of Approved Natural Medicine Training Programs — both checked 6 August 2026.
  • Give you any supervised client contact. There is no practicum, no consultation hours, no case supervision and no skills assessment — only lectures, readings, a discussion forum and live Zoom seminars.
  • Let you administer psilocybin or MDMA to anyone in California. Both remain Schedule I federally and California has no regulated access programme.
  • Screen you for suitability. Enrolment is open to anyone who pays, including people with no clinical licence at all, which is a real difference from the admissions-gated programmes at Berkeley or CIIS and should factor into how a hiring clinic reads it.
Because of the availability, power, and growing legalization of these medicines for clinical, ceremonial, and recreational uses, these ideas and conversations around psychedelics are open to everyone. This Certificate neither requires nor confers a license or degree.— Pacifica Graduate Institute — Pacifica Extension, in its own materials

Cost, length and who can apply

Cost$1,095 general rate for each level, taken separately. $930.75 Pacifica alumni and senior rate; $876 Lifelong Learner member rate; $657 Pacifica Extension student rate (published on Level 1). Continuing education credits are an additional $30. A 50% deposit at registration with instalments is available, and a limited scholarship / reduced-tuition pool exists with a stated application deadline before each cohort. Cancellation: full refund 14+ days before start, 50% from 14 to 7 days, none inside 7 days. Prices are the ones published for the 2025 runs; no 2026 pricing has been posted.
LengthLevel 1: 16 weeks (described as a four-month course). Level 2: 12 weeks (described as a three-month course).
HoursLevel 1: 14 CE credits, 16 live two-hour sessions plus recorded presentations and readings. Level 2: 10 CE credits, 11 live two-hour webinar sessions (12 scheduled weeks, one reading-only week) plus 12 recorded video sessions. No practicum, no supervised contact hours, no consultation hours.
FormatFully online. Each week a pre-recorded lecture to watch at your own pace, followed by a live two-hour Zoom session with a faculty member and fellow students at 4:30-6:30pm PT (occasionally 8:30-10:30am PT for internationally based faculty). Course materials on the D2L platform, plus a private online discussion forum and a learning resource guide. All live sessions are recorded; watching recordings and keeping up with the forum earns the certificate of completion, but CE credits require live attendance at every session.
WhereCarpinteria / Santa Barbara, CA — delivered entirely online via Zoom
Who can applyOpen enrolment — no licence, degree, prerequisite or application required. The course page states the material is 'open to everyone' and names its intended audiences as Level 1 alumni and experienced practitioners; scholars, educators and researchers; mental health professionals (therapists, psychologists, social workers, counsellors); medical professionals and prescribers (MDs, RNs, NPs); and psychedelic facilitators, coaches and ceremonialists. Level 1 is explicitly not a prerequisite for Level 2.

    Case-study work on ketamine infusions alongside analytic treatment for depression, dissociation and suicidal ideation, and using active imagination afterward for integration.     Eco-depth work of the Animas Valley Institute; adaptations for preparation, journeying and integration. Brian Stafford.

    Small and large group synthesis of the twelve weeks. Brian Stafford and Katie Stone.

Who teaches it

Faculty is a large part of what these programmes actually sell, and it changes between cohorts — check the current list before you decide on this basis.

  • Brian Stafford, MD, MPH — anchors both levels; triple-board-trained psychiatrist, former Anschutz Family Chair in Early Childhood Psychiatry, completed the CIIS/MAPS psychedelic-assisted therapy programme, now a Senior Guide and trainer at the Animas Valley Institute. He teaches psychedelics at Pacifica and facilitates preparation, journeying and integration privately.
  • Michael Mithoefer, MD — ran the first MAPS-sponsored Phase 2 MDMA trial for PTSD with Annie Mithoefer, and supervised therapists on the Phase 3 trials. The single biggest name on the roster.
  • Rosalind Watts, PhD — former clinical lead of the psilocybin-for-depression trial at Imperial College London; created the ACE/ACER integration models and the Watts Connectedness Scale.
  • Saj Razvi — founder and Director of Education at the Psychedelic Somatic Institute, former MAPS Phase 2 clinical researcher, co-developer of PSIP; teaches clinicians to work legally with ketamine and cannabis in private practice.
  • Neil Hanon, MD, FRCPC — Canadian psychiatrist, certified Holotropic Breathwork facilitator, co-founder of Jamaica Grief Retreats, former Medical Director of TheraPsil; since 2023 leads Grof Legacy Training's Canadian Breathwork and Psychedelic Therapy streams.
  • Linda Carter, MSN, CS, IAAP — Jungian analyst in Carpinteria, 45+ years in practice, on ketamine as an adjunct to analytic treatment.
  • Simon Yugler — depth-psychology MA from Pacifica, author of 'Psychedelics and the Soul'; has taught for five facilitator training programmes including CIIS, Naropa and InnerTrek.
  • Katie Stone — co-facilitator of the Level 2 orientation and closing sessions.
  • Level 1 additionally featured Robin Carhart-Harris, Bill Richards, Janis Phelps (founder of the CIIS certificate), Beatriz Caiuby (Bia) Labate, Michael Winkelman, Allison Hoots, JD, Geneen Marie Haugen, Brian Richards, Dylan Martinez Francisco and Henrique Fernandes Antunes.

Where it stands right now

No cohort scheduled. Verified 6 August 2026: the Advanced Psychedelics Graduate Certificate Level 2 is listed on Pacifica Extension's programme index and its own page with dates 'TBD', categorised under Coming Soon; the Level 1 introductory certificate is filed under 'Previous Programs' with its last run 7 May - 20 August 2025. Pacifica Extension's currently enrolling 2026 catalogue is otherwise mythology, Jungian, ecopsychology and astrology programming, with no psychedelic offering open.

What I could not establish

These are the things I looked for and could not establish. Take them to the programme, and notice how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No 2026 dates or 2026 pricing for either level — both are 'TBD'.
  • No stated intention either way about whether Level 1 will run again; it has been moved to 'Previous Programs'.
  • No enrolment numbers, completion rates or any outcome data for either level.
  • No independent reviews, journalism or third-party discussion of these specific courses could be found.
  • No verified programme-specific video. Pacifica's YouTube channel covers its degree programmes, not this certificate series.
  • Pacifica publishes no admissions criteria and no code of conduct for these open-enrolment courses, so it is not clear what, if anything, is screened for.
  • Whether the $30 CE fee is per level or per certificate is not stated explicitly beyond the line item.
  • Could not verify any FDA action on MDMA-assisted therapy after the August 2024 complete response letter within reachable sources; this record assumes no FDA-approved MDMA product exists and that psilocybin and MDMA remain Schedule I federally.
