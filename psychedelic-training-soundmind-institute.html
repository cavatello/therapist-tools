SoundMind Psychedelic Facilitator Training Program

A 12-month, mostly online facilitator training that is currently on Colorado's approved training programme list — so its Colorado track leads somewhere real — but is no longer on Oregon's, despite having been one of the first three programmes OHA approved back in 2022.

What it is

SoundMind is a nonprofit-affiliated training run by Hannah C. McLane, MD, MA, MPH, who designed and teaches it. The 2026–27 cohort structure is: enrol any time between 15 March and 15 September 2026, start with a self-paced prerequisite 'Psychodynamic Techniques for Psychedelic Facilitators' course, then from September 2026 attend required live Zoom sessions on Tuesdays 7–9pm ET with guest lecturers, plus a supervised weekly accountability group (tentatively Thursdays 7–8pm ET). Expect 8–10 hours a week outside class for readings and homework. To be certified you must complete all readings, homework and quizzes, pass a written comprehensive exam, and attend the main class — five absences are allowed. There is a 'Global Access Program' for people who cannot make the live hours.

Tuition is stated only as a range, $5,800 to $10,300, 'depending on the program you choose', with the specifics reserved for a call. The 40-hour in-person practicum required for the full certificate is a further $2,500–$4,500 and is not included in tuition; you have two years after graduation to complete it. Practicums use psilocybin or ketamine depending on whether you want the Colorado psilocybin certificate. Refunds are full and no-questions-asked up to three weeks before the programme starts. Roughly 30% of past cohorts were not clinicians — SoundMind takes an explicit position that restricting training to licence-holders is itself an equity problem.

On equity, the concrete things — as distinct from the mission statement — are: the BIPOC Psychedelic Leadership Program, a free 10-month fellowship for 15 BIPOC fellows meeting monthly with five core facilitators, whose graduates SoundMind lists publicly so organisations can book them; a Psychedelics and Identity Initiative building retreat protocols and clinician materials around sexuality and gender-identity shifts that surface in sessions; a stated requirement that all teachers be 'grounded in an anti-racist, anti-oppression framework'; a Native American Outreach Coordinator (Belinda Eriacho, Diné and Zuni) who also instructs; a Peer Support Model aimed at Veterans, BIPOC and LGBTQIA+ clients; and a curriculum module, 'Ethical and Inclusive Practice', covering consent, boundaries and BIPOC access. The affiliated Philadelphia clinic is the delivery vehicle for most of this.

On neurodiversity, be more careful. There is no neurodiversity module in the published curriculum. What exists is: McLane identifies as neurodiverse (dyslexia and ADHD) and researches cognitive diversity, psychedelics and ADHD; and SoundMind's own 2023 press release lists 'Neurodiverse individuals' among the populations its equitable-care models serve. That is a founder's research interest and an organisational claim, not a taught component you can point to in the syllabus. The one structural feature that arguably supports neurodivergent students — recorded lectures, five permitted absences, a self-paced Global Access track — is framed by SoundMind as scheduling flexibility, not as accommodation.

What it lets you do

This is the section the programme’s own marketing is least specific about, so it runs before everything else. It was established against the programme’s own materials and against the two state registers that matter — Oregon’s approved-curriculum list and Colorado’s.

This does not change your licence or your scope of practiceIt is a credential from a school, not from a regulator. California’s Board of Behavioral Sciences neither recognises nor requires it, and holding it adds nothing to what you may lawfully do as an LMFT in this state.
With this you can
  • Keep practising as a California LMFT with a certificate from a school. It confers nothing on your BBS licence; the BBS neither issues nor recognises a psychedelic-therapy credential.
  • Take the Colorado track and, on completion, pursue Colorado natural medicine facilitator licensure. This one is real and checkable: 'SoundMind Institute Psychedelic Facilitator Training Program' appears on DORA's Approved Natural Medicine Training Programs list, with Didactic, Practicum and Consultation all marked Yes (checked 6 August 2026). SoundMind states you will still need 40 hours of consultation supervision after the programme, at your own cost, to get a full licence.
  • Practise ketamine-assisted psychotherapy in California in collaboration with a prescriber — the training teaches you to hold space, prepare and integrate. SoundMind is unusually direct about the division of labour: 'To provide ketamine-assisted therapy, a facilitator must be affiliated with a physician who can prescribe ketamine.'
  • Do preparation and integration work, which needs no credential at all, and which SoundMind explicitly emphasises as a track in its own right.
  • Have supervised hands-on experience with psilocybin or ketamine via the 40-hour practicum, in a jurisdiction where that is legal.
  • Enter the field without a clinical licence, if that describes you — this is one of the few programmes that says so openly rather than quietly.
You still cannot
  • Give you any legal ability in California beyond what your LMFT licence already provides. Psilocybin and MDMA remain Schedule I federally and there is no California therapeutic framework: SB 58 was vetoed in October 2023, and California's 2025 legislative action was a bill to accelerate the study of psychedelics for PTSD, not to legalise therapeutic use.
  • Get you an Oregon psilocybin facilitator licence. SoundMind was approved by OHA in June 2022 and announced it as being 'one of the first three programs in the world' — but it does not appear on OHA's current live list of training programmes with approved curriculum (15 programmes, checked 6 August 2026). The programme's own 2026 page now markets state approval only for Colorado. If Oregon is your goal, this is the wrong programme unless SoundMind can show you a current OHA approval and HECC licensure in writing.
  • Prescribe or administer anything. Their own disclaimer: this training 'does not provide the ability to prescribe ketamine, psilocybin or administer them without proper medical licensure.'
  • Be presented as an accreditation. SoundMind says it plainly: outside Oregon and Colorado 'there is no standardization or credentialing board for psychedelic-assisted therapy at this time, which means no individual course or program can provide "accreditation" to practice with psychedelics.'
  • Qualify you to practise independently on completion. 'Continued hands-on learning and ongoing clinical supervision are strongly recommended before practicing independently.'
  • Count toward your California BBS continuing education, as far as anything published shows. No CE or CME credit is advertised anywhere on the programme page or FAQ — unlike IPI and PCH, which both publish accredited CE arrangements. Ask before you assume.
**Important Disclaimer: This training does not provide the ability to prescribe ketamine, psilocybin or administer them without proper medical licensure. It is designed for therapeutic and facilitative roles within a collaborative care framework.— SoundMind Institute (training arm of SoundMind; affiliated with SoundMind Center, Philadelphia), in its own materials

Cost, length and who can apply

CostSoundMind publishes a range, not a price: 'The cost of the program ranges from $5,800 to $10,300, depending on the program you choose. Please schedule a call to discuss your options.' The 40-hour in-person practicum required for the full certificate costs an additional $2,500–$4,500 and is explicitly 'not included in the course tuition fee'. Colorado's post-programme 40 hours of consultation supervision is also not included. Early-bird discounts are advertised without figures. Full refund, no questions asked, up to three weeks before the start.
Length12 months. You can start as early as 15 March 2026 and as late as 15 September 2026 to be part of the 2026–2027 cohort; required live sessions begin September 2026. The 40-hour practicum can be completed up to two years after graduation.
HoursNot published as a single total. Weekly: 2 hours of live class, 1 hour of small group, and 8–10 hours of readings and homework. Plus a required 40-hour in-person practicum for the full certificate. A 2023 SoundMind press release described an earlier version of the programme as '200-hour' over eight months; the current page does not give an hour total, so do not assume the two are the same programme.
FormatHybrid, weighted heavily online. Self-paced prerequisite psychodynamic course on the platform; required live Zoom classes Tuesdays 7–9pm ET with rotating guest lecturers; required weekly accountability groups supervised by two teaching assistants (tentatively Thursdays 7–8pm ET); self-paced video and reading modules; all classes recorded and available after the course. A 'Global Access Program' offers a more self-paced route for people who cannot attend live. The in-person element is a 40-hour practicum, offered by SoundMind and affiliated partners through the year, using psilocybin or ketamine; on some practicums students may receive treatment from another student, but this is not required to graduate.
WhereOnline nationally, with in-person practicum hubs; DORA lists the programme at 555 Martin Luther King Jr. Blvd, Suite 105, Portland, OR 97214. Retreats and practicums have run in Colorado, Oregon, Philadelphia, Jamaica, Costa Rica and Peru.
Who can applyDeliberately open. 'We open the application to everyone who is interested in psychedelic-assisted psychotherapy facilitation. About 30% of students in previous cohorts were not clinicians yet.' SoundMind states its position directly: 'We have always believed that an equitable and thriving psychedelic ecosystem is not one that limits training programs to those with pre-existing medical or mental health licensure.' It prioritises applicants with experience of non-ordinary states, a daily meditation practice, curiosity about the scientific literature, and skills such as bodywork, somatic training or herbalism. Applicants with no psychotherapy or clinical background may still apply and, if accepted, receive preparation homework. Process: apply, then a live interview with the team. A California LMFT is comfortably eligible; so is a member of the public, which is worth knowing about your future cohort.

Who teaches it

Faculty is a large part of what these programmes actually sell, and it changes between cohorts — check the current list before you decide on this basis.

  • Hannah C. McLane, MD, MA, MPH — founder and lead instructor; designed and teaches the programme; physician and psychoanalyst; research on cognitive diversity, psychedelics and ADHD, psychedelics and touch, and power dynamics including narcissism and cult behaviour in psychedelic communities; identifies as neurodiverse (dyslexia and ADHD).
  • Claudia Cuentas, LMFT — Peruvian immigrant, bilingual/bicultural artist and therapist; drama therapy, expressive arts, Somatic Experiencing; Aymara and Quechua lineage; MAPS MDMA training parts A and B. The named faculty member closest to an LMFT reader's own role.

What I could not establish

These are the things I looked for and could not establish. Take them to the programme, and notice how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No fixed price. Tuition is a $4,500-wide range and the actual figure is only given on a sales call — which makes it impossible to compare against IPI's published $7,000 or PCH's published $5,000.
  • No total hour count for the current programme. The only hour figure published is 40 hours for the practicum plus a weekly commitment.
  • No CE or CME credit is mentioned anywhere on the programme page or in the FAQ. Given the BBS requires 36 hours per renewal and competitors advertise accredited CE, this looks like a real absence rather than an oversight in my search, but I could not find a page confirming either way.
  • No explanation of why SoundMind is no longer on Oregon's approved list, and no statement on the current site acknowledging the change — the 2022 approval announcement is still published without amendment.
  • Neurodiversity is claimed at organisational level ('programs for Veterans, BIPOC, LGBTQIA+ and Neurodiverse individuals') but no such programme is described anywhere I could find, and there is no neurodiversity content in the published curriculum.
  • The tuition difference between the tracks is not broken out — what specifically the $5,800 buys versus the $10,300 is not published.
  • Independent reviews are unavailable from this environment: Reddit and Trustpilot both returned 403.
