California PsyD programs, and the one thing that decides what the degree is worth.

25 doctorates in psychology based in California, grouped by accreditation — because California will license you without APA accreditation, and the internship year very largely will not.

Training20 APA-accredited

In short

Which PsyD programs are there in California?

Every PsyD in the state, grouped by what its accreditation actually gets you

20 of 25 are APA-accredited

California does not require APA accreditation. Your internship does.

Every list of PsyD programs sorts on accreditation and none of them explains what it decides, so here it is in full.

For the license itself, the Board of Psychology asks about the institution, not the program. Business and Professions Code § 2914(b)(2)(A) requires a doctorate from “a college or institution of higher education that is accredited by a regional accrediting agency recognized by the United States Department of Education.” In California that means WSCUC. The word “APA” does not appear in the statute, and it does not appear in the implementing regulation, 16 CCR § 1386, either. This is not theoretical: the Board’s own 2025 figures show new California psychologists licensed from non-APA programs every year.

For the supervised experience, the rule is programmatic. 16 CCR § 1387 requires the internship or postdoc to be APA-accredited, or a member of APPIC or CAPIC. APA-accredited internship sites overwhelmingly take applicants from APA-accredited doctoral programs, so a non-APA doctorate narrows that funnel sharply and pushes candidates toward CAPIC placements.

And for everything after, the constraint is geographic and occupational rather than legal. Many other state boards, the ASPPB Certificate of Professional Qualification, the VA and most federal psychologist posts, and a lot of hospital and academic medical center jobs presume an APA-accredited doctorate and internship. A degree that works in California and nowhere else is a career geography decision, taken years early.

B&P § 2914(b) · 16 CCR § 1386 · 16 CCR § 1387 · verified against the Board’s published law and regulations, 8 August 2026

Every figure below is the institution’s own, with the year it applies to and a link to the page it came from. 20 of the 25 publish a tuition figure; the rest say nothing and this page says so rather than estimating. Accreditation status is APA’s own, read from its quarterly Notices of Actions rather than from a school’s description of itself — which matters, because at least one school’s site is a year out of date about its own status.

APA-accredited, on contingency

1

Real accreditation, not a provisional half-measure — a degree finished while a program holds this status counts as completed at an accredited program. It is the status APA grants a program too new to have graduated anyone, because there are no outcomes yet to judge. The program must apply for full accreditation within three years. The risk to weigh is small but not zero: ask the program how conversion is tracking.

Four schools whose PsyD no longer qualifies anyone for a California license

These are approved by the Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education but are not regionally accredited, and § 2914(b)(2)(A) requires regional accreditation. The pathway that used to accept them, § 2914(b)(4), expired on 1 January 2020, and the Board’s own list of them is now headed “Previous Approved Schools Accepted Prior to 2020.” There is a narrow grandfather clause at § 2914(b)(2)(B) for students who were already enrolled on 31 December 2016.

  • Ryokan College — Los Angeles Still advertises a “Doctor of Psychology in Clinical Psychology (for licensure in California).” That claim reflects the rule before 2020.
  • Professional School of Psychology — Sacramento
  • The Reiss-Davis Graduate Center — Los Angeles
  • San Diego University for Integrative Studies — San Diego

If you are looking at one of these, ask the Board directly before you pay anything. § 2914(b)(3): “The board shall make the final determination as to whether a degree meets the requirements of this subdivision.”

How this page was built

Nothing is ranked. There is no score and no best-of. Accreditation is a fact and it is used as a grouping, not as a verdict.
No tuition figure is estimated. Where a school publishes a per-unit rate but no total, this page shows the per-unit rate — multiplying it out and presenting the answer as the school’s would be inventing a number.
Accreditation status comes from APA, not from the schools. Read from APA’s quarterly Notices of Actions. California Baptist’s own site still described its program as on contingency after APA had moved it to full accreditation, which is exactly why.
MFT and LPCC are a different route entirely — different board, different statute, master’s level. Those are on the MFT programs page.
