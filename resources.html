The Board

Licence, renewal, exams

The BBS is where every deadline you have originates. Bookmark the lookup and the fee page; the rest you will need once or twice a year.

Board of Behavioral SciencesThe agency that issues, renews and disciplines your licence. Open →
Licence and registration lookupConfirm a licence number, status and expiry — yours, a supervisor's, or a referral partner's. Open →
Renew your licenceThe renewal hub. A licence cannot be renewed more than 90 days before it expires, and there is no grace period — late means a delinquency fee. Open →
Fees: the temporary reductionBBS fees roughly halved on 1 July 2026 and stay reduced until 30 June 2030. Associate registration and annual renewal are $75, the law and ethics exam $75, licensure application $125, LMFT clinical exam $125, and biennial licence renewal $100. A separate $20 Mental Health Practitioner Education Fund fee is not reduced. Note the Board's own renewal page still shows the old table. Open →
Statutes and regulationsEvery statute and regulation governing your practice in one PDF, January 2026 edition. Open →
What changed, and whenThree regulations took effect in 2026 — fee reductions and disciplinary guidelines on 1 July, and advertising on 1 April, which affects how you may describe yourself. Open →
California Law and Ethics examAdministered by Pearson VUE, and there is a separate exam for each licence type. Associates must take it annually until they pass in order to renew. Open →
Book an exam (Pearson VUE)Where the Law and Ethics and LMFT clinical exams are actually scheduled. The LCSW clinical exam is run by ASWB and the LPCC's by NBCC instead. Open →

CE and supervision

What you must do every cycle

The hour counts are exact and the Board publishes them. These are the two pages people misremember most.

Continuing education requirements36 hours every two-year renewal period, including 6 hours of law and ethics. Associates need 3 hours of California law and ethics each one-year period. One-time requirements: 6 hours of suicide risk assessment and 3 hours of telehealth. Open →
CE summary chartThe whole picture on one page — the fastest thing to hand a colleague who is confused about renewal. Open →
Supervisor qualificationsBefore you promise an associate hours you can sign for: you need two of the last five years licensed, then 15 hours of supervision training within 60 days of starting, and 6 hours of supervision CPD each renewal cycle after that. (It is 15 up front and 6 per cycle, not the other way round.) Open →
Supervision agreementMust be signed within 60 days of starting with a new supervisee. Without it the supervised experience does not count. Open →
Supervisor resourcesEvery form and rule governing the supervisory relationship. Open →

Your association

Legal cover, mostly

The concrete reason a solo practitioner joins is rarely the newsletter. It is having somebody to call about a subpoena.

CAMFTThe California MFT association. Clinical membership is $227 plus a $50 application fee; pre-licensed is $95. Open →
CAMFT legal consultationsFree, unlimited calls to staff attorneys about subpoenas, mandated reporting and confidentiality. This is the benefit most members actually use. Open →
NASWFor LCSWs. $236 a year at MSW/DSW/PhD level, and you are placed in the California chapter automatically. Includes free ethics and legal consultations. Open →
NASW California chapterThe state chapter that lobbies on scope of practice and reimbursement. Open →
CALPCCFor LPCCs. $255 a year professional, $155 associate, $80 student. The professional tier includes attorney consultations twice a year and 2 free CEUs. Open →

Getting paid

Panels, credentialing and self-pay

Credentialing is mostly one profile that every payer reads, plus waiting. Check a panel is open before you spend a week on its paperwork.

CAQH Provider Data PortalNearly every commercial panel pulls your licence, work history and malpractice details from this one profile, so a stale profile stalls every application at once. CAQH rebranded to DataSpring in June 2026 but the provider login is still here, still branded CAQH. Open →
Get an NPI (NPPES)The free, official place to get the Type 1 individual NPI that goes on every claim and superbill. Paid lookalike sites rank well in search; this is the real one. Open →
Medicare: what therapists need to knowCMS confirms LMFTs and mental health counselors can bill Medicare — payment began 1 January 2024. Enrol through PECOS or the paper CMS-855I; clean web applications are processed within 15 days. Open →
Medicare enrolment (PECOS)Where the CMS-855I is filed. It will reject you until you have I&A credentials, which is the step most people miss. Open →
Medi-Cal enrolment (PAVE)The only route into fee-for-service Medi-Cal. Help desk (866) 252-1949. Open →
Medi-Cal: who can enrolConfirms in writing that LMFT, LCSW, LPCC and psychologist are eligible applicant types, which saves guessing at the category. Open →
Anthem Blue Cross CaliforniaThe clearest California-specific statement of what a complete file needs. Credentialing typically takes 45 days from a completed CAQH application; recredentialing every three years. Open →
Blue Shield of California — behavioral healthNames LMFT, LCSW and LPCC explicitly. Applications must be dated no more than 30 days from submission; 45 to 60 days to process. Open →
Optum / United Behavioral HealthThe solo-practitioner track at the largest behavioral health network. Applications stall most often on a CAQH profile that does not match the Optum form. Open →
Aetna behavioral healthBehavioral health has its own request form, and contracting comes before credentialing — the reverse of what most people assume. Open →
Evernorth (Cigna) behavioral healthCurrently closed to new individual applicants. Evernorth paused new applications on 1 June 2026 and points providers back to this page after 1 September 2026. Check before you start. Open →
Availity EssentialsSeveral large payers route eligibility checks and remittances only through Availity, so you end up with an account whether or not you wanted a clearinghouse. Open →
Good Faith Estimates (self-pay clients)The federal rule that applies to essentially every private-pay client. Booked 3–9 business days ahead, the estimate is due within 1 business day; 10 or more days ahead, within 3. Open →
Good Faith Estimate — the noticeA ready-made compliant notice. A client can dispute a bill that exceeds the estimate by $400 or more. Open →

Telehealth

And clients who travel

One statute and one FAQ answer almost every question here, and the answer to the out-of-state one surprises people.

B&P Code §2290.5 — the telehealth statuteYou must inform the client and obtain and document consent before delivering care by telehealth. Failure to comply is unprofessional conduct. Associates and trainees are covered too. Amended by SB 402, effective 1 January 2026. Open →
BBS telehealth FAQThe Board's own answer to the question that quietly creates the most liability in a remote practice: you may treat a client in another state only if you meet that state's requirements to practise there. Licensure follows the client's physical location, not yours. Open →
Counseling CompactCalifornia is not a member state, so it grants a California licensee nothing — and the compact covers LPCs and LPCCs only, never LMFTs. Worth knowing precisely because it is widely misunderstood. Open →

Protecting the practice

Insurance, HIPAA, confidentiality

Malpractice cover and a risk assessment are the two things a solo practice is most likely to be missing.

CPH & AssociatesProfessional liability for therapists. Includes $35,000 of licensing board defence, $10,000 per deposition. There is a CAMFT-specific programme for California LMFTs. Open →
HPSOThe main alternative. Up to $1m per claim, but licence defence is $25,000 against CPH's $35,000 — a real difference worth comparing rather than assuming carriers are interchangeable. Open →
The TrustBest known for psychologists; this page confirms LMFT, LCSW and LPCC are eligible. Up to $2m per incident. Open →
HIPAA Privacy RuleWhat you may do with client information and what rights clients have to their own records. Open →
HIPAA Security RuleElectronic records only — which is the entire footprint of a practice running an EHR and telehealth. A significant tightening has been proposed but the current rule still stands. Open →
Security Risk Assessment toolThe Security Rule requires a risk assessment and this free tool is built for small providers with no IT department. Nothing you enter leaves your computer. Open →
Breach notificationA stolen laptop starts a clock: notify affected individuals within 60 days. Breaches under 500 people are reported annually, 500 or more within 60 days. Open →
Psychotherapy notes are differentThe most misunderstood point in the whole set: psychotherapy notes need the client's authorisation before disclosure for any reason, including treatment — with exceptions for mandated reporting and duty to warn. Open →
Confidentiality of Medical Information ActCalifornia's own confidentiality law sits on top of HIPAA and is stricter in places, so HIPAA compliance alone is not enough here. §56.10 is the section you reach for when a subpoena arrives. Open →
Mandated reporting — who and whenPhone the agency immediately, then send a written report within 36 hours. The two-step clock is the detail people get wrong under pressure. Open →
Suspected Child Abuse Report formThe form that 36-hour written report has to be made on. Download it before you ever need it. Open →

Money and structure

Entity, tax, retirement

The entity question has a wrong answer that gets repeated constantly. Start with the two code sections.

You cannot use an LLC — Corp. Code §17701.04A California LLC may not render professional services. For a licensed therapist the real choice is sole proprietorship or a professional corporation — never an LLC, whatever a generic small-business guide says. Open →
Professional corporations — Corp. Code §13401.5Sets who may co-own one. Other licensed professionals may hold no more than 49% — which is where the familiar “51% therapist-owned” figure comes from; the statute states the 49% cap, not the 51%. Open →
Naming the corporation — B&P §4987.7You cannot name it whatever you like. The name must contain one of “marriage”, “family” or “child” together with “counseling”, “counselor”, “therapy” or “therapist”. This is where filings get rejected. Open →
Secretary of State — forms and feesWhere the Articles of Incorporation for a professional corporation live. Open →
The $800 that decides itEvery corporation owes California $800 a year whether or not it made a profit (waived in the first year). This is the single biggest reason a low-revenue practice should stay a sole proprietorship. Open →
California and the S electionCalifornia does not honour the S election the way the IRS does — the entity still pays 1.5% on California source income, which erases part of the federal saving. Open →
Schedule CWhere a sole proprietor's whole practice income and expenses land. Open →
Estimated taxesNobody withholds tax from private-pay income. You generally must pay quarterly if you will owe $1,000 or more; the safe harbour is 90% of this year or 100% of last year's tax, whichever is smaller. Open →
Solo 401(k)The highest-capacity plan available with no employees. Once it holds $250,000 or more you must file Form 5500-EZ annually — the step that surprises people. Open →
2026 contribution limits$24,500 employee deferral, $8,000 catch-up at 50, and an overall cap of $72,000. SIMPLE deferral is $17,000 with a $4,000 catch-up. Open →
Social Security wage base$184,500 for 2026, up from $176,100. This is the cap where the 12.4% Social Security half of self-employment tax stops — the biggest kink in a solo practice's tax curve. Open →

Getting clients

Directories and networks

Two different commitments here. A directory lists you and you keep your own billing; a managed network credentials you and sets what you are paid.

Psychology TodayThe largest referral source most California therapists use, at $29.95 a month — and the line item that recurs whether or not it produced a client. Open →
TherapyDenFree, with no card required, so there is no financial reason not to have a second listing. An optional premium tier is $30 a month. Open →
Inclusive TherapistsFree tier available; paid tiers from $22 a month billed annually. Sliding-scale discounts of 15–35% for BIPOC therapists. Open →
Open Path CollectiveCosts the therapist nothing, but caps what you may charge those clients at $40–70 a session. It trades rate for referral flow. Pre-licensed and associate therapists are eligible. Open →
GoodTherapyThe priciest mainstream directory — $30.95 to $49.95 a month. Worth putting on a trial-and-cancel timer. Open →
AlmaNot a directory: $95 a month for credentialing, billing and negotiated insurance rates. A fixed cost that only pays back at volume. Open →
HeadwayNo membership fee, but Headway sets the insurance rate you are paid — the cost is inside the reimbursement rather than a subscription. Credentialing in as little as 30 days. Open →affiliate
Grow TherapySame model. Credentialing in 5–7 days on average. Compare the effective hourly against your private-pay rate before switching. Open →
