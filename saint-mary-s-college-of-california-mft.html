What this program actually is

OrientationHumanistic and integrative, with an explicit place for transpersonal, Eastern and spiritual material and a strong self-of-the-therapist emphasis

Of the three Catholic-affiliated Bay Area MFT programs, Saint Mary's is the one where the religious heritage actually shows up in the required course descriptions rather than only in mission statements. The required theories course, COUN 604, promises "an overview of the historical development of psychotherapy and counseling theories including both traditional and more recent theories, as well as transpersonal and Eastern approaches." The MFT/PCC capstone theory course, COUN 741, lists "mindfulness practices, and the inclusion of spiritual and religious issues in counseling" among its central topics and describes the instructor's job as guiding "the integration of spiritual concerns as a multicultural skill set." And the second skills course, COUN 602, states outright that the counseling immersion is "grounded in humanistic psychology." Read together, that is a coherent orientation — humanistic, integrative, explicitly hospitable to the transpersonal — and it is the clearest theoretical identity of the three programs.

The other structurally distinctive thing is the training-group model in the skills sequence. COUN 601 and COUN 602 each run as six all-day Saturdays (10 to 5) alternating with six two-hour small-group sessions in which "students work with a counseling skills trainer to practice and develop their emerging skills in effective listening, counseling, and communication." That is a dedicated skills trainer supervising a small group over a full academic year, not a professor watching role-plays in a lecture hall. Alongside it sits COUN 603, a required three-unit course on the counselor's own wellbeing — "designed to... inspire students to understand, respect, and care for themselves personally and professionally, now and throughout their lives" and to introduce "integral and holistic health as it pertains to counseling." A whole required course on your own health, before you have a client, is uncommon in California.

Third: the department is built around one shared 40-unit core taken by everyone, whatever their specialization. An MFT/PCC student sits with future school counselors, school psychologists and forensic-psychology students for those 40 units, then adds 28 units of MFT/PCC coursework for a 68-unit minimum, or 81 units if they add the School Counseling / PPS credential as a dual specialization. That makes the dual an unusually clean add-on: 13 extra units buys a K-12 credential on top of licensure eligibility. It also means the program is generalist by construction — the departmental learning outcomes are shared across all specializations, and there are no MFT-specific emphasis tracks in the Santa Clara sense. Everything happens on the Moraga campus, in the afternoon and evening, 4:15–7:00 p.m. and 7:15–10:00 p.m. Monday to Thursday plus some Saturdays. There are no online courses and no satellite locations. One more oddity worth knowing: SMC prepares students for the CPCE, the national Counselor Preparation Comprehensive Examination, via a dedicated three-unit course (COUN 797) — a counselor-education convention that most California MFT programs do not follow.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California program teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

COUN 6013 units

Counseling Skills I

"The course involves a lecture and discussion/activity component, as well as coordination and supervision of small training groups in which students work with a counseling skills trainer to practice and develop their emerging skills in effective listening, counseling, and communication." Delivered as six Saturdays, 10 to 5, alternating with six 2-hour training group sessions.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe delivery model is the point. A year-long pairing of all-day Saturday intensives with small training groups run by a dedicated skills trainer is a much heavier investment in supervised skill practice than the standard weekly role-play class, and it is the thing SMC graduates most often cite. It also means Saturdays are not optional here.

COUN 6033 units

Personal and Professional Development of Counselors

"Self-awareness and compassionate self-understanding, personal growth, and on-going professional development are essential to the well-being and effectiveness of counselors." The course introduces "integral and holistic health as it pertains to counseling" and aims to "inspire students to understand, respect, and care for themselves personally and professionally, now and throughout their lives."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required three-unit course whose subject is the student's own health and self-understanding — not ethics, not self-care as a footnote in a practicum seminar. Very few California MFT programs spend three units on this. It is the clearest signal of the program's humanistic center of gravity.

COUN 6043 units

Theories of Counseling

"This foundational course provides students with an overview of the historical development of psychotherapy and counseling theories including both traditional and more recent theories, as well as transpersonal and Eastern approaches." It also includes "an introduction to the findings of research evaluating the efficacy and efficaciousness of a wide variety of counseling theories."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe phrase 'transpersonal and Eastern approaches' inside a required survey course is the honest answer to whether the Lasallian Catholic identity shapes the curriculum: it does, but as an openness to spiritual and contemplative traditions generally rather than as Catholic doctrine. If you want depth or transpersonal work without going to a specialist school like CIIS, this is where it lives.

COUN 7413 units

Advanced Clinical Practice

"Central topics include the therapeutic relationship, evidence-based practice, integrative approaches to psychotherapy, mindfulness practices, and the inclusion of spiritual and religious issues in counseling." Taught as a seminar in which "the instructor's role is to act as facilitator" and to guide "the integration of spiritual concerns as a multicultural skill set."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe clinical capstone, and it treats spirituality as a competency rather than a topic to be avoided. Framing religious material as part of multicultural skill is a defensible, specific stance — and one you should want to agree with before enrolling.

COUN 7313 units

Treatment of Children

"Some of the modalities which will be introduced and practiced are play, art, and sand tray therapy." The course covers assessment, complex trauma, "historical and intergenerational trauma," infant/child and parent psychotherapy, divorce, "peace corners," domestic and community violence, and bullying, from "social justice, multicultural and trauma-informed frames."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingSand tray and expressive-arts modalities actually practiced, not just surveyed, plus intergenerational trauma named explicitly. For anyone planning to work with children in schools or community agencies this is a more hands-on child course than most single-course child requirements deliver.

COUN 7911–3 per term, 6 units total; repeatable units

Counseling Field Experience (MFT/PCC)

"The primary purpose of the course is to develop a learning network and consultation skills with other colleagues in the class... MFT/LPCC licensing requirements will be revisited as will strategies for working with particular client situations and specialized clinical areas. Time will be devoted to case discussions by seminar attendees... Up to three one-hour consultations are available with the professor upon request."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingNote the framing: the stated primary purpose is building a peer consultation network, not supervision per se — supervision itself comes from SMC campus supervisors and department-approved field supervisors. The offer of up to three individual hours with the professor is a concrete, small-program detail worth knowing.

COUN 7973 units

Counseling Exam Preparation

"This course is designed to prepare students to take the Counselor Preparation Comprehensive Exam (CPCE) and includes an overview of knowledge and skills in the field of counseling. The course covers eight common core areas of study to prepare students to sit for the CPCE as part of the completion of their master's program."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe CPCE is a national counselor-education instrument, and building a three-unit course around it is a counselor-education convention rather than an MFT one. It tells you SMC's counseling department thinks of itself as a counseling department first and an MFT program second — which is consistent with the shared 40-unit core across every specialization.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a program can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your program approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a program controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the license requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a program’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programs on this list.

You apply, with the school’s help

There is an approved-site list and real support, and the seat is still yours to win. Most programs are here and most students manage it. Ask for last year’s numbers rather than the policy: how many students placed on the first round, and how many had to extend.

Our program connects you with schools, clinics, and community organizations across the wider Bay Area, helping you secure field placements that match your goals and prepare you for professional success. the school’s own page →
StartsFinal year. In the published dual-specialization plan of study, COUN 791 begins in the fall of year three.

Fieldwork is COUN 791 Counseling Field Experience (MFT/PCC), taken 1–3 units per term across multiple terms to a total of 6 units. Per the program's own description, "Students are engaged in fieldwork in clinical, school or college settings appropriate to department specialization(s) and career objectives. Weekly seminars held for specific specializations. Supervision provided by Counseling Department campus supervisors and department-approved field supervisors" — so there are two supervision layers, one inside SMC and one at the site. The seminar itself is framed around peer consultation and case discussion rather than direct supervision, with up to three one-hour individual consultations with the professor available on request. On placement, the department says it "connects you with schools, clinics, and community organizations across the wider Bay Area, helping you secure field placements that match your goals" and refers to "thoughtfully selected field placements," which reads as an assisted search rather than an assignment. Students adding the School Counseling credential take a separate COUN 793 field experience and a COUN 703 school practicum, which includes 50 hours shadowing school counselors at one or two sites. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enroll, not after.

Counting every attempt rather than only first attempts, the figure is 73% across 218 sittings here, against 66% statewide. First attempts are the fairer comparison — counting resits measures how many tries people needed rather than how many passed, and it penalises a school whose graduates keep going.

On the separate California Law and Ethics exam, 86% of this school’s 184 first-time candidates passed, against 80% statewide.

Recorded by the Board as St. Mary's College of CA, Moraga. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A program that enrolls more career-changers and more students of color will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the program changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a program page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programs. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the program places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the program cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A program placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • Required fieldwork hours are not published. COUN 791 is described in units (6 total) rather than in face-to-face client hours, and no fieldwork handbook for the counseling department is publicly posted.
  • No term-by-term plan of study is published for the MFT/PCC-only 68-unit pathway. SMC says full course plans are shared with admitted students. Only the 81-unit dual School Counseling + MFT/PCC plan is public.
  • Cohort size is not published, though multiple reviewers describe small classes.
  • There is no in-house training clinic that I could find; placements appear to be entirely external.
  • Some SMC pages are internally inconsistent about course numbering: the learning-outcomes page still refers to the skills sequence as "COUN 520 and 521" and the FAQ calls it "COUN 520 and COUN 521," while the current catalog and the MFT/PCC course list use COUN 601 and 602. The FAQ also refers to practicum courses "COUN 235" and "COUN 527." Treat old course numbers on SMC pages with suspicion.
  • The legacy Acalog catalog (catalog.stmarys-ca.edu/*.php) returns 403 to automated requests, so most course descriptions here were read from Saint Mary's newer Coursedog catalog at /courses/COUN###. COUN 605's and COUN 711's descriptions came from that source too; COUN 791's came from the 2025–26 Acalog entry.
  • No Reddit discussion of the Saint Mary's counseling program was found, and Reddit blocked retrieval on this pass in any case. The only first-hand reviews located are three GradReports entries from 2014–2017, which is thin and old.
