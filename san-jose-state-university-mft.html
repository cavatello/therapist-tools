What this programme actually is

OrientationEvidence-based practice and case formulation; faculty are deliberately eclectic (CBT, behavioral, mindfulness, psychodynamic, cross-cultural, feminist, health psychology) rather than committed to one model

Start with the name, because it is the thing most likely to confuse you. This is an M.S. in Clinical Psychology, awarded by the Psychology department, not an MFT or counseling degree — and it is nonetheless a standard route to California LMFT licensure. The SJSU catalog puts it plainly: "The required academic course work and supervised fieldwork of 60 semester units meets the course work requirements for the California State Marriage and Family Therapist (MFT) license and most of the requirements for the Licensed Professional Clinical Counselor (LPCC) license." The operative words are "most of." Per the program's student handbook, LPCC candidates must additionally take EDCO 266 (Career Counseling), which sits outside the 60-unit degree and requires a special petition to enroll in extra units, and must accumulate 280 pre-degree field hours instead of the 150 required on the MFT track. So: MFT by default, LPCC only if you deliberately bolt on the extra course and hours.

Being housed in a psychology department rather than a counselor-education department changes the curriculum in visible ways. You take two separate semester-long psychopathology courses — PSYC 210 (adults) and PSYC 211 (children) — where most CA MFT programs teach one combined diagnosis course. You take a full graduate clinical assessment course (PSYC 203A) that also satisfies the university's graduate writing requirement, a clinical psychopharmacology course, and an applied research-methods course. The culminating experience is not a portfolio or a paper on a topic: it is a 30–35 page APA case conceptualization of a client you actually treated, followed by an oral defense in front of two faculty examiners in a professional case-conference format, with 80% required to pass and dismissal after a second failure. What you do not get is depth in family systems: only PSYC 208 (Family Assessment and Intervention) and PSYC 209 (Psychology of Contemporary Families) are systems courses, and there is no COAMFTE accreditation.

It is also, by a wide margin, the smallest of the three. SJSU says it "seek[s] to build a cohort of 12-14 students each year," full-time only, no part-time, evening, hybrid or online option, meeting on campus three to four days a week. The barrier to entry is not a test — no GRE — but coursework and prior clinical experience: six specific undergraduate psychology prerequisites, at least 30 semester units of psychology overall, 3.0 GPA both in psychology and in the last two years, at least 100 hours of applied clinical experience before you apply (crisis text-line volunteering explicitly does not count), and one of your three letters must come from a clinical supervisor. Applications close January 1, interviews are in March, offers go out mid-April.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

PSYC 203A3 units

Clinical Assessment I

An in-depth introduction to objective, projective and behavioral assessment techniques used in the clinical evaluation of the behavior and functioning of adults, children and families. Satisfies GWAR: Graduation Writing Assessment Requirement.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingProjective testing in a master's-level therapy curriculum is a psychology-department artifact — most MFT programs teach assessment as intake interviewing and DSM coding. That this course also carries the university's graduate writing requirement tells you the program treats assessment writing, not therapy technique, as the core professional literacy.

PSYC 2103 units

Advanced Psychopathology

Advanced course to provide knowledge and experience in the classification and treatment of disorders of adulthood as they are identified in mental health settings. Critical review of research related to the evaluation of treatment strategies.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingPaired with PSYC 211, this gives you two full semesters of psychopathology — adult and child taught separately. Almost no California MFT-qualifying program spends a full year on diagnosis, and the 'critical review of research related to the evaluation of treatment strategies' framing is what the department means by evidence-based practice.

PSYC 2113 units

Child Psychopathology

An overview on the nature, assessment and treatment of Child Psychopathology. Emphasis will be placed on understanding DSM diagnoses from a developmental perspective. Objective methods of assessment and empirically supported treatments will be covered.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingRequired in the very first semester, before any practicum. Reading DSM categories through a developmental lens and pairing them with 'empirically supported treatments' is the clearest single statement of the program's orientation — it is training you to be a diagnostician who treats, not a systems thinker who reframes.

PSYC 2223 units

Multiculturalism, Ethnicity and Gender in Psychotherapy

Theoretical and practical understanding of multiculturalism, ethnicity and gender in clinical practice, including social, cultural and psychological factors that affect therapeutic intervention within individuals, couples and families.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingWorth flagging because the course was recently rewritten: in the 2024-25 catalog it was titled 'Gender and Ethnic Issues in Counseling and Therapy' and described in terms of 'differing socialization, psychological structures, values and cultural assumptions.' The current title and text are broader. If you are comparing older reviews of this program, the multicultural requirement you read about is not quite the one you would take.

PSYC 2083 units

Family Assessment and Intervention

An interdisciplinary exploration of how therapists can analyze and change interpersonal dynamics in couples and families.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the program's single dedicated couples-and-families intervention course, and it sits in the final semester. If family systems work is why you want to be an MFT, note how little real estate it occupies here compared with SDSU's year-long theory sequence — and that its catalog description is one sentence long.

PSYC 2433 units

Field Work in Psychology

Supervised field work experience in outside agency settings. Course may be repeated twice for up to 6 units. Grading: Credit/No Credit. Note(s): Open only to students in the MS Clinical program.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticing'Outside agency settings' is the whole story: there is no in-house SJSU training clinic behind a one-way mirror. Your clinical hours come from a community agency you go out and secure yourself, and the course exists mainly as the registration vehicle that makes those hours count for the BBS.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You find your own site

You are responsible for securing a placement. Most programmes are like this and most students manage it, but a student who cannot find a site cannot graduate - and that risk is highest if you are rural, online, or need Spanish-language or specialty hours.

StartsSecond year, fall term — the entire first year is coursework
Hours requiredMinimum 150 face-to-face clinical hours for the MFT track (280 for students pursuing LPCC), plus 75 client-centered advocacy hours which may overlap. Supervision at one hour individual or two hours group per five client hours. These count toward the 3,000 hours of supervised experience the BBS requires after the degree.

There is no SJSU-run training clinic for this program. Students secure their own community fieldwork site; the program director provides information and contacts and the department runs an annual fieldwork fair in January, but choosing and landing the placement is the student's job, and most agency application deadlines fall in February and March. You must have a licensed supervisor on site or readily available. Registration is through PSYC 243 every semester you are at a placement, and there is a documented squeeze at the end: if you finish the fourth semester short of hours you must see clients over the summer within 90 days of the semester's end, or enroll (and pay) for an additional course for those hours to count. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 53 of the 65 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • SJSU does not publish an acceptance rate or an applicant count for this program. The only hard number is the target cohort size of 12–14, which is small enough that selectivity is presumably steep, but that is an inference, not a published figure.
  • The department's HTML curriculum page and its official 'Required Sequence' PDF disagree: the web page lists PSYC 223 (Multiculturalism and Diversity: Clinical Practice) and PSYC 265 (Advanced Conceptualization and Treatment Application), neither of which exists in the current SJSU catalog under those numbers. Verify the actual sequence with the department before relying on either.
  • No per-unit tuition figure applies — CSU charges a flat per-semester rate by unit band. For Fall 2026 SJSU lists $5,629.50/semester for full-time graduate CA residents (tuition plus mandatory fees) and $3,837.50 part-time. At four full-time semesters that is roughly $22,500, but SJSU does not publish an official program total.
  • No named training clinic, and the department does not publish a list of fieldwork sites or a placement rate.
  • No licensing exam pass rate, graduation rate or job placement rate is published (this program is not COAMFTE-accredited and so has no required disclosure).
  • Almost no independent student commentary is available. Reddit was unreachable through both search and direct fetch during this research; the only substantive third-party voice found was a single 2021 GradCafe post.
  • The catalog quotation establishing the MFT/LPCC relationship was read in the archived 2024-2025 catalog entry; the equivalent 2026-2027 catalog program page would not render during this research.
