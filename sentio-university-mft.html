Read this first

The Board has issued a Notice to Students about this school

Sentio holds provisional approval from the Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education, and that approval expires in July 2028. Per the Board’s notice, a Sentio graduate may register as an Associate and may sit the California Law and Ethics exam — but may not sit the Clinical Exam, and therefore cannot be licensed, until Sentio is fully accredited. If accreditation is not achieved by July 2028, Associates registered on this degree are disqualified and their supervised hours will not be accepted. That is the whole pathway, so read the notice itself before applying — and note that the school appears on the Board’s qualifying-degree list at the same time.

What this programme actually is

OrientationDeliberate Practice / evidence-based integrative, taught as a fixed sequence of named models (CBT/TEAM-CBT, EFT, IFS, EFCT, Schema, psychodynamic, MI, systemic, multicultural)

Sentio is the only California MFT programme built entirely around Deliberate Practice, and the pedagogy is real, specific and unusual. Founded in 2020 by Tony Rousmaniere and Alexandre Vaz — the authors of the American Psychological Association's "Essentials of Deliberate Practice" book series — it took three years of development before admitting its first cohort in Fall 2024. Every course incorporates videotaped skill rehearsal; every client session at the training clinic is recorded; every supervision session is recorded; outcome data is collected on every client and then brought back into academic classes. The transparency runs both ways in a way no other California programme claims: every faculty member receives weekly feedback on video of their own teaching, and every clinical supervisor receives weekly feedback on video of their own supervision. Prospective students are invited to sit in on a live class and to attend a residency where they can meet the entire student body, and the admissions application includes an optional exercise in which you record three one-minute videos of yourself doing Deliberate Practice. If you want a programme where you will be watched constantly and coached on micro-skills, this is the one that actually does it.

The business and regulatory picture has to be read separately from the pedagogy, and it is the single most consequential thing about this school. Sentio is a non-profit approved to operate by California's Bureau for Private Postsecondary Education, but it is NOT accredited by any agency recognised by the US Department of Education — not WSCUC, not DEAC, not COAMFTE. It is an APPLICANT to DEAC, the national distance-education accreditor: Sentio links to DEAC's own public notice page, where it appears under "Applicants for Initial Accreditation." In August 2025 the California Board of Behavioral Sciences published a formal Notice to Students on Sentio's status. The Board's determination: graduates may register as Associates and earn supervised hours, and may sit for the Law and Ethics Exam, but "they will not be eligible to sit for the California LMFT Clinical Exam—and therefore will not be eligible for licensure—until Sentio University becomes fully accredited." BPPE provisional approval expires in July 2028; if full accreditation is not obtained by then, Associates registered on a Sentio degree "will be disqualified from licensure based on that degree, and any supervised experience hours earned under those registrations will not be accepted." Sentio's own 2026–27 catalog does not mention this exam restriction; it says graduates "are eligible for licensure ... upon successful completion of the requirements put forth by the California Board of Behavioral Sciences." Sentio does disclose, prominently and repeatedly, that it takes no federal student loans, that graduates may not qualify for licensure outside California, and that it only admits California residents.

On price: there is a published sticker price, and it is easy to find. Tuition is $1,120 per semester credit × 60 credits = $67,200, with a total cost of attendance of $68,400 including a $200 registration fee and $1,000 estimated textbooks, plus unbudgeted travel and lodging for five in-person residencies. The scholarship structure is straightforward once you do the arithmetic: both the merit-based K. Anders Ericsson Memorial Scholarship and the need-based Jeremy Safran Scholarship are awarded in three tiers of $16,800 / $33,600 / $50,400 — exactly 25%, 50% and 75% of tuition — so the real range a student pays is roughly $16,800 to $67,200, with "a handful of scholarships available per tier, per cohort." Two caveats. First, Sentio's own pages disagree with each other about the ceiling: the programme overview advertises the Ericsson award as "$1,000 to $67,200" while the scholarships page says "$1,000 to $50,400," and the tier table on both pages tops out at $50,400. Second, the price has moved: Sentio's 2024 BPPE filing reported total programme charges of $48,440 for the inaugural cohort, against $67,200 today. Because there are no federal loans, the money is out of pocket or private, and the catalog states students "should not be employed full-time while attending."

Two more things a prospective student should weigh honestly. Cohorts are genuinely tiny — capped at 16, with the inaugural cohort at 7 students per Sentio's 2024 regulator filing and a graduate's own account ("the cohorts are so small (7 in my class)") — which means the 4:1 faculty ratio is real but also that the alumni network, and any independent review base, is close to nonexistent. And separately: Sentio publishes dozens of "MFT Program Review" pages about its California competitors (Pepperdine, CSUN, Alliant, CIIS, Chapman, USD, National, Biola and more). These carry a one-line footer disclosure — "Sentio University is an MFT program in California and a peer institution to the program profiled above" — and end with a link to Sentio's own programme, and they interleave factual competitor data with claims like "2 in 3 students at COAMFTE-accredited MFT programs do not feel prepared to see clients." They are marketing, and should be read and cited as marketing, not as neutral reviews of other schools.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

MFT551–5563 semester credits each (18 total) units

Theories and Models of Therapy Course Series

"This series of courses teaches major theories and models of therapy, including Common Factors, Emotion Focused Therapy, Cognitive-Behavioral Therapy, Internal Family Systems, Motivational Interviewing, Systemic Therapies, Schema Therapy, Multicultural Therapy, and Emotion Focused Therapy for Couples... In every course, students will practice exercises from the 'Essentials of Deliberate Practice' series of Deliberate Practice training books, authored by Sentio University staff and published by the American Psychological Association."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingSix required courses — nearly a third of the degree — devoted to rehearsing named models one at a time, using the faculty's own APA-published exercise books. Most California programmes teach one or two survey theories courses; this is the structural bet that makes Sentio different, and it is also why there are no electives.

MFT591–5943 semester credits each (12 total) units

Practicum I – Practicum IV

"California law requires students to attend a practicum class while they are seeing clients. Thus, Practicum classes are 17-20 weeks long, so students will not have an interruption in their work with clients throughout semesters two to five." Practicum classes feature "guest visits and presentations from various consumers and family members of consumers of mental health services."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe practicum course is stretched to 17–20 weeks while every other course runs 12, purely so the clinical caseload never pauses between terms. That is a scheduling detail, but it is the mechanism behind the 400+ hour claim — most programmes' students lose their caseload over breaks.

MFT5613 units

Person of the Therapist

"This course guides students through their personal development as a therapist. Topics include the rationale for developing the person of the therapist; the inner skills of psychotherapy; methods for psychological/emotional self-awareness, self-assessment, and growth; and common challenges in this area. An emphasis is placed on student self-agency and protecting students boundaries."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe explicit note about "protecting students boundaries" is unusual and is a direct answer to the obvious risk of a curriculum where you show video of your worst moments to peers every week. Worth asking in an interview how that protection actually operates.

MFT5633 units

Supervision and Consultation

"This course focuses on the practice of supervision and consultation in marriage and family therapy... Students will learn the major models of supervision and consultation; common challenges in the practice of supervision and consultation; and methods to enhance learner motivation and prevent burnout."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA full required course on how to supervise, in a master's degree, is rare in California — supervision training is usually a post-licensure CE requirement. It reflects the founders' own field (Rousmaniere's research is on supervision and outcome data) more than a typical MFT curriculum.

MFT5643 units

Therapist Career Development

"This course prepares students to start their careers in the field of marriage and family therapy, gain employment, and pursue career-long professional development, including preparation for the California Law and Ethics Exam and LMFT Clinical Exam. Topics include practice management, networking, guidance for starting a private practice, and applying for employment at agencies..."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required, credit-bearing course on getting paid — private practice mechanics, agency applications, exam prep. Most programmes leave this to a lunchtime panel. Note the irony that it prepares students for a Clinical Exam they are currently barred from sitting until the school is accredited.

Clinical Research Projectembedded units

Clinical Research Project (final semester)

"Students complete an empirical research project in their final semester using their own clinical outcome data from their work with clients at the Sentio Counseling Center," with an assigned Faculty Research Advisor; the project "tightly focuses on personally relevant clinical challenges and opportunities from students' therapy cases." catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingInstead of a thesis on someone else's literature, the capstone is an n-of-your-own-caseload study of your own measured outcomes. That is only possible because outcome data is collected on every client from day one — it is the pedagogy and the capstone being the same thing.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

A seat is guaranteed

The school states that every student in good standing gets a clinical placement. Three of the seventy-eight say this. It removes the single most common reason a two-year degree turns into a three-year one.

Yes. Students in good standing receive guaranteed clinical training placement at the Sentio Counseling Center, our telehealth training clinic. This is different from programs that offer "practicum support" but leave students to find their own field placements. the school’s own page →

The school runs its own training clinic.  Sentio Counseling Center (SCC) Owning a clinic and holding a seat in it are different things, so this sits beside the model above rather than replacing it.

StartsSecond semester (Spring of year 1) — roughly five months in, earlier than almost any other California MFT programme
Hours requiredAt least 400 hours of direct clinical contact, of which at least 100 must involve working with couples
In-house clinicSentio Counseling Center (SCC) — a 100% online non-profit training clinic serving low-income Californians, founded by Rousmaniere and Vaz specifically to be Sentio's practicum site

Placement is guaranteed, not searched for: every student in good standing is placed at the Sentio Counseling Center, so there is no scramble for a traineeship site and no risk of a placement falling through — the single largest practical advantage this programme has. Clinical work begins in semester two and runs continuously to graduation, with practicum courses stretched to 17–20 weeks precisely so the caseload never breaks between terms; the school starts students on a few clients and ramps up to a full caseload. The stated minimum is at least 400 direct client contact hours with a 100-hour couples sub-minimum, and all of it counts toward the 3,000 BBS hours; the SCC is telehealth-only across California, with sliding-scale fees starting at $15 a session and a client population weighted toward trauma, low income and marginalised communities. Supervision is unusually heavy and unusually visible: weekly supervision in individual, triadic and small-group formats — a published example week shows one hour of individual supervision, a one-hour couples-therapy triadic supervision, and two hours of group supervision alongside roughly nine to ten client sessions — with every client session AND every supervision session videotaped, so supervisors give feedback on specific recorded moments and students can rewatch their own supervision. Every supervisor has completed advanced Deliberate Practice supervision training and themselves receives weekly video-based feedback on their supervision. Routine outcome measurement runs throughout treatment and is fed back into both supervision and academic classes. The trade-offs are equally concrete: all your clinical experience is telehealth and all of it is at one agency, so you graduate without in-person, school-based, hospital or community-agency exposure, and without having built the site-hunting muscle you will need for your post-degree associate years. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No independent student discussion found anywhere — no Reddit, Student Doctor Network, GradCafe or Niche presence. Every student voice available is published by Sentio itself (its Student Reviews page republishes 40 Google reviews). With one graduating cohort of about 7, this is unsurprising but it means there is no adversarial account of the programme in existence.
  • No licensure or exam outcome data exists yet and cannot exist: the first cohort graduated in May 2026 and, per the BBS notice, is currently barred from sitting the LMFT Clinical Exam.
  • No public accreditation timeline beyond the statutory minimum. The BPPE notice Sentio is required to publish states it must "achieve accreditation candidacy or pre-accreditation ... by 7/25/2025, and full accreditation by 7/25/2028," and that failure means automatic suspension of its authority to offer degrees plus a teach-out or refund. Sentio links to DEAC's public notice page as evidence of progress, where it is listed under "Applicants for Initial Accreditation" (Torrance, CA) — i.e. an applicant, with no candidacy or pre-accreditation status shown, and DEAC's commission meets only in January and June. Sentio's FAQ separately says it is "pursuing regional accreditation" (DEAC is a national, not regional, accreditor). Whether the 7/25/2025 candidacy milestone was met is not stated anywhere Sentio publishes.
  • Sentio's own pages contradict each other on the top scholarship award — $67,200 on the programme overview vs $50,400 on the scholarships page, with tier tables on both topping out at $50,400. No stated criteria distinguish Tier 1 from Tier 3, and no data on what fraction of students receive any award.
  • No published attrition or completion data (0 graduates in the only filed BPPE report), and no employment or salary data.
  • The catalog's course table lists 'MFT652: Psychopharmacology' in semester 5 while its course descriptions section lists 'MFT562: Psychopharmacology' — an unresolved numbering inconsistency in the same document.
  • No freely-licensed photograph of Sentio (Torrance office) or the residency venue on Wikimedia Commons; the clinic is entirely virtual.
