What this program actually is

OrientationSystemic/relational, taught as an explicit arc from cybernetic to postmodern — Structural, Strategic and Experiential in one required course; Narrative, Solution-Focused, Collaborative Language Systems and Emotionally Focused Family Therapy in its sequel

The name has changed three times and the entity has changed hands twice, so it is worth getting the lineage straight. It began in 1958 as Chapman College teaching on-base classes at MCAS El Toro; became Chapman University College in 2001; was separately incorporated in 2008; and was renamed Brandman University in 2009 after a gift from the Joyce and Saul Brandman Foundation. In September 2021 Brandman was transferred to the University of Massachusetts under a change-of-control agreement and became UMass Global. The financial terms are public: UMass committed roughly $130 million, Chapman receives $96 million over ten years with payments beginning in fiscal 2028, and UMass Global bought the Irvine campus for $37 million. What did not change is where it actually is or who accredits it. UMass Global is headquartered in Aliso Viejo, California, and it is "institutionally accredited by the WASC Senior College and University Commission (WSCUC)" — the same California regional accreditor Brandman held. It is a Massachusetts-branded, California-accredited, entirely online institution.

That history is not incidental to the decision. Enrollment fell from about 22,000 at acquisition to roughly 19,000; Chancellor David Andrews told the Boston Globe "we took a big hit" and that "the fall-off in enrollment was a lot sharper... than we thought. I mean, it was over 20 percent that we lost within the first 18 months." By June 2024 UMass Global carried over $81 million in debt for which the UMass system bears ultimate liability, and the system has extended $8.6 million of credit on its behalf to guarantee student financial aid. None of that means the school is closing. It does mean that if you are choosing this program partly because "UMass" sounds like a state flagship, you should know the relationship is an affiliation with overlapping boards, not membership in the UMass campus system, and that the affiliation is currently a cost center rather than a windfall.

On the geography question: this is not a California-only program, whatever a directory listing may say. UMass Global's own state-authorization table lists the MA in Marriage and Family Therapy as offered in 42 states, including California, and "Program not offered" in seven — Florida, Mississippi, Missouri, New Hampshire, New York, North Carolina and Wisconsin. The combined MFT/PCC emphasis is much narrower, offered in about 29 states (California among them) and not offered in Connecticut, Georgia, Illinois, Indiana, Kentucky, Maryland, New Jersey, New York, North Carolina, Ohio, Pennsylvania, Rhode Island, South Carolina, South Dakota or Wisconsin. The catalog is blunt about the consequence: "If the student relocates to a state in which the program is not authorized, the student will be unable to complete the program." The three courses that turn the 60-credit MFT degree into the 69-credit dual degree — the ones that give California graduates APCC eligibility alongside AMFT — are named in the catalog and carry a different prefix: PSYU 521 Advanced Individual Therapy II, PSYU 539 Crisis and Trauma Counseling, and PSYU 545 Transition to Work and Career, three credits each.

The program is explicitly not COAMFTE-accredited — its own Graduate Achievement Data page says so — but unlike most non-accredited programs it publishes the data anyway, and the data is mixed in an informative way. Full-time cohorts have run 81, 77, 78 and 86 students (2021–22 through 2024–25). Job placement was reported at 100% for both completed cohorts (n=53 and n=36), and licensure at any MFT level at 95% and 85.71%. But graduation within the advertised 2.5 years was 65.00% for 2021–22 and 56.76% for 2022–23 — that is, more than four in ten recent full-time students did not finish on time, and the likeliest reason is in the practicum section below. At $770 a credit the degree costs about $46,200 for the 60-credit MFT emphasis or $53,130 for the 69-credit dual, which is materially cheaper than either California brick-and-mortar option in this comparison set.

The courses that define it

Every California program teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

MFT 512 / MFT 5263 + 3 units

Family Therapy Theories and Techniques: A Modern Emphasis / A Postmodern Emphasis

MFT 512: "Cybernetics family systems theory and therapy is emphasized. In addition, students will study several clinical models including Structural, Strategic, and Experiential Family Therapy." MFT 526 (prerequisite MFT 512): "in-depth study of major postmodern models such as Collaborative Language System, Narrative Therapy, and Solution-Focused Therapy, as well as the contemporary model Emotionally Focused Family Therapy."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingSix required credits split deliberately across the modern/postmodern break in family therapy, with the second gated on the first. Most programs compress this into a single survey. If you want to come out able to say which side of that break you work from and why, this pairing is the reason to look here.

MFT 5243 units

Clinical Issues in Human Diversity

"It seeks to deconstruct the sociohistorical implications of power, privilege, and oppression. Students will be challenged to consider their unique social location identity with the deliberate exploration of intersectionality aimed at increasing student competence in anti-racist practices."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe phrase "anti-racist practices" as a stated competency outcome is unusually explicit for a required multicultural course; most catalogs stop at "cultural competence." Worth knowing in both directions — it tells you what the classroom conversation will be like.

MFT 6601.5 units

MFT Practicum I: A Systemic Lens of the Developing Therapist

"Students will provide recordings (audio or video) of their live clinical cases in order to demonstrate the application of clinical skills in the beginning stages of therapy. In addition, students will engage in the exploration of self-of-the-therapist."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingEvery one of the six practicum courses requires a minimum of two recorded therapy sessions per term presented to the group, and at least 50 of the 100 program supervision hours must be observational supervision from those recordings. For a fully online program that is a real accountability mechanism — your supervisors watch you work, not just hear you describe it.

MFT 6641.5 units

MFT Practicum V: Social Context and Capstone 2

"As a final project, students will collaborate with classmates to create a community resource manual to provide referrals to diverse and vulnerable populations."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA concrete, portable deliverable rather than a paper — and a smart answer to the structural weakness of an online program, which is that students are scattered and know nothing about each other's local referral ecologies. It is the one assignment in the sequence that turns geographic dispersion into an asset.

MFT 5063 units

Ethical & Professional Issues

"Depending upon the student's state of residence, ethical and legal responsibilities and liabilities will vary. Students will learn to apply ethical decision-making skills to resolving conflicts, practicing appropriate boundaries, and assuring that confidentiality is maintained. Students will learn how to apply the AAMFT Code of Ethics."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingRead that first sentence carefully if you intend to be licensed in California. This is a single national law-and-ethics course taught to students in 42 jurisdictions; it is built around the AAMFT Code, not around California Business & Professions Code §4980 and the Tarasoff/mandated-reporting specifics a California-only program would drill. The BBS accepts the degree, but you should expect to do more of your California-specific law and ethics preparation on your own before the exam.

MFT 5423 units

Community and Environmental Mental Health

"The primary focus of this course is on the accessibility of mental health services by underserved populations and disenfranchised communities... In addition, environmental stressors and their negative impacts on mental health will be reviewed. Students will be introduced to interventions that model best practices, such as the recovery paradigm of treatment."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required course naming environmental stressors as a clinical subject is still uncommon, and pairing community access with the recovery paradigm signals the program expects graduates to work in agencies rather than cash-pay private practice — which is also where the practicum placements are.

The whole degree, term by term

What you would actually take, in the order you would take it. Sequences change; check the current catalog before you count on any single term.

Fully online, instructor-led, in eight-week sessions with six start points a year. No cohort in the ordinary sense — the program is 23 courses split into a 17-course didactic phase (51 credits) and a 6-course practicum phase (9 credits). Full-time is two courses per session, giving an advertised 2.5 years; the maximum is seven years. Eight named courses plus a passed oral "advancement" examination gate entry to practicum. Total is 60 credits for the MFT emphasis; the combined MFT/PCC emphasis adds three PSYU courses for 69. course sequence ↗

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a program can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your program approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a program controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the license requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a program’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programs on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You apply, with the school’s help

There is an approved-site list and real support, and the seat is still yours to win. Most programs are here and most students manage it. Ask for last year’s numbers rather than the policy: how many students placed on the first round, and how many had to extend.

Students are responsible for securing a practicum site while actively collaborating with their CFR and are encouraged to seek guidance and support from their CFR as needed throughout the process. the school’s own page →
StartsAfter passing the oral "advancement" examination — a vignette-based clinical exam — which requires the eight named didactic courses first, plus an application filed at least two sessions ahead, an approved Practicum Site Agreement, liability insurance, and the lifting of a standing Dean's Hold on registration. Then six consecutive 1.5-credit practicum courses over twelve months.
Hours required400 hours over a 12-month period: 300 direct client contact hours of which at least 100 must be relational (two or more people in an ongoing relationship), plus 100 supervision hours of which at least 50 must be observational supervision from your own audio or video recordings. Budget 8–10 hours a week, more if the site demands it.

Say this bluntly, because for an online program it is the whole decision: UMass Global does not place you. The Clinical Handbook's sentence is "Students are responsible for securing a practicum site while actively collaborating with their CFR" — the Clinical Faculty Representative reviews an approved-site list, discusses process, and vets agencies you bring, but you do the searching, the applying and the interviewing. The marketing line "our dedicated field support team helps you identify potential clinical sites in your community" describes that vetting function, not a placement service. Two details tell you how hard this actually is in practice. First, approval of a site that is not already on the list "may take 1–2 months or longer," and hours accrued at an unapproved site "may be considered unethical" and will not count toward graduation. Second — and this is the sentence to read twice — the professional conduct policy lists "Failure of the student to secure a practicum placement after interviewing at 7 sites" as a condition that automatically triggers a formal Step One professional conduct meeting, the same procedure used for a placement termination. A program that has written a disciplinary trigger for failing to get placed after seven interviews is a program that has seen students fail to get placed after seven interviews. That is very likely what is behind on-time graduation of 65.00% and then 56.76%: if you cannot find a site you cannot enroll in MFT 660, and if you run out of hours you are moved into the zero-credit MFT 696 continuation course and charged a fee until you finish or your seven-year clock expires. Two further constraints bind where you can look: "Students are mandated to provide clinical services within their state of residency and cannot provide clinical service across state lines or internationally," and if you move to a state where the program is not authorized you cannot finish at all. One clarification on the advertised "in-person" hours: the handbook defines direct client contact as occurring "in-person synchronously, either physically in the same location or mediated by technology," so supervised telehealth counts. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enroll, not after.

68%passed first time472 of 694 candidates

Counting every attempt rather than only first attempts, the figure is 55% across 1,185 sittings here, against 66% statewide. First attempts are the fairer comparison — counting resits measures how many tries people needed rather than how many passed, and it penalises a school whose graduates keep going.

On the separate California Law and Ethics exam, 71% of this school’s 890 first-time candidates passed, against 80% statewide.

Recorded by the Board as Brandman University &middot; UMASS Global. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A program that enrolls more career-changers and more students of color will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the program changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a program page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programs. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the program places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the program cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A program placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.
