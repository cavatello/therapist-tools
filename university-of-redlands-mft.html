What this programme actually is

OrientationGeneralist clinical mental health counselling (LPCC-shaped) with an optional systemic layer — the specialization's own branding, ‘Thinking Systemically’, is the closest thing to a stated theoretical commitment

The base degree here is not an MFT degree and Redlands does not pretend otherwise. The catalog opens: "The 60-unit Master of Arts in Clinical Mental Health Counseling (CMHC) prepares students for licensure as Professional Clinical Counselors (LPCC) in the state of California." That is the LPCC shape — CACREP-style core, career development, assessment, research, a counselling-identity spine — and the MFT specialization is bolted onto it. The arithmetic is unusually clean and unusually well published, which is worth crediting: the Fall 2026 pathway grid ends with a line reading "60-units: Master of Arts in Clinical Mental Health Counseling (CMHC) / +6-units: Specialization / Total: 66-units." So the MFT specialization costs six extra units, not twelve — twelve is the specialization's advertised size, but half of it (CMHC 630 Theory & Practice in Family Counseling and CMHC 631 Couples & Relationship Counseling) is already inside the 60-unit core that every CMHC student takes. The 2023 handbook says it plainly: "Course work includes 6-units taken as part of the 60-unit MA and 6 additional units offered in an online format for a total of 66 Units to earn the MA CMHC with MFT Specialization." The genuinely additional pieces are CMHC 632 (3 units) and three one-unit courses from the ‘Thinking Systemically Seminar Series’ — MFTS 700 Conflict Resolution, MFTS 701 Families & Violence, MFTS 702 Families & Gender.

Dual eligibility is the actual product. The program page states that MFT-specialization graduates can pursue "licensure as a Licensed Marriage and Family Therapist (LMFT), in addition to licensure as a Licensed Professional Clinical Counselor (LPCC)," and the 2025 handbook is more explicit still: "CMHC graduates meet the Board of Behavioral Sciences requirements leading toward California licensure as Professional Clinical Counselors (PCC), Marriage and Family Therapists (MFT), or they may pursue dual licensure." For six units and a set of relational client hours, that is one of the cheaper routes to two California licences on offer — the tuition delta at the 2026–27 rate is $5,592. What you trade for it is systemic depth: the whole family-and-couples content of this degree is three three-unit courses and three one-unit seminars, against a program built around individual clinical counselling. Two of the four named faculty hold PhDs in Marriage and Family Therapy from Loma Linda, and the program coordinator, Janee Both Gragg, is dually licensed LPCC and LMFT — so the systemic expertise is real, it is just not the organising principle of the curriculum.

Two structural facts matter more than the brochure. First, size: the handbook lists "four full and part-time faculty and several adjunct (i.e., part-time) faculty" in the whole Department of Counseling and Human Services, which also runs the School Counseling program and a China-based Counseling and Psychotherapy master's — and CMHC students "are likely to take classes with students in the School counseling program." Second, gatekeeping is formalised and visible in a way most programs keep quiet. Faculty "convene to formally evaluate students twice each academic year (Fall and Summer)" on a published 0–3 concern scale, where 3 means "Student's pattern of performance raises significant questions about his/her ability to succeed in the program" and triggers a referral to the Student Professional Development Committee. Failing the CPCE (EDUC 699, taken for zero units in the second spring) is one of seven listed triggers. If you want to know in advance what will get you remediated, Redlands has actually told you.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

CMHC 6323 units

Advanced Child & Adolescent Development and Intervention (labelled ‘Adv Human Development’ on the Fall 2026 pathway)

Redlands publishes no public course description for this course. What is published: it is the single three-unit course that is genuinely additional to the 60-unit core for the MFT specialization, it sits in the second summer, and it is shared with the Child and Adolescent Intervention specialization, which uses the same course as its own advanced offering. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is where half the MFT specialization's extra weight actually goes — into child and adolescent development, not into couples work. It tells you the specialization is aimed at family-system practice with children in it, and that a student can pick up both specializations by swapping which one-unit seminars they take.

MFTS 700 / 701 / 7021 each (3 total) units

Thinking Systemically Seminar Series — Conflict Resolution; Families & Violence; Families & Gender

No catalog descriptions are published. The handbook describes the parent block: "The Clinical Mental Health Counseling (CMHC) program offers 12 units of specialized training focused on therapeutic intervention applicable to a broad range of relational systems" — and the 2023 handbook notes the six additional units are "offered in an online format." catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingOne-unit topic seminars are rare in California MFT training and are the most distinctive thing on this transcript. They are also the mechanism that lets a student pick up a second specialization: the handbook says a student can earn both MFT and Child & Adolescent specializations "but would need to select different 1-unit Thinking Systeically Seminar Series courses." A Spanish-speaking-communities track uses the same one-unit format (MFTS 704, 705, 706).

CMHC 699A / 699B / 699C2 each (6 total), plus CMHC 699D continuation (2) if hours are unfinished units

CMHC Practicum I, II, III

"Students admitted into the CMHC program, upon completing their first year of coursework, are required to perform practicum hours in a clinical setting across three semesters for a total of at least 450 hours (280 hours must be face-to-face counseling). ... Students earning the MFT Specialization are required to complete (200) additional face to face hours working with couples and families."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingNote the unit weighting: three semesters of clinical work carry six units out of sixty. It also runs as a seminar with recorded sessions — sites must permit "the use of those recordings in the CMHC 699 seminar series," so your tape gets watched by your cohort, not just your supervisor.

CMHC 6983 units

CMHC Pre-Practicum

No published description. Its position is the information: on the Fall 2026 two-year pathway it sits in the first summer, immediately before the second-year practicum sequence begins, and on the three-year pathway it moves to the second summer to sit immediately before practicum there. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA dedicated three-unit pre-practicum term is more structure than most programs give the transition into client work, and the fact that it moves with the pathway confirms it is a gate rather than a filler course.

CMHC 6213 units

Counseling in a Global Context

No published description. Required in the second spring of both the two-year and three-year general tracks, alongside CMHC 610 Sociocultural Counseling & Intervention in the first fall — two separate required multicultural courses in a 60-unit degree. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingTwo required diversity/context courses rather than the usual one, plus a whole third specialization dedicated to Counseling Spanish-Speaking Communities (CMHC 650 Advanced Counseling Hispanic; MFTS 706 Mi Familia), is a genuine institutional commitment in a program serving the Inland Empire.

EDUC 6990 units

CPCE (Counselor Preparation Comprehensive Examination)

Carried at zero units in the second spring of both pathways. The handbook lists "Student fails the CPCE" as one of seven events that can trigger reassessment by the Student Professional Development Committee. catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA zero-unit line on the pathway that can nonetheless get you referred for remediation is worth seeing before you enrol. It is also a marker that the program is built on the counselling-profession (CACREP-style) exam culture rather than the MFT one.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

You apply, with the school’s help

There is an approved-site list and real support, and the seat is still yours to win. Most programmes are here and most students manage it. Ask for last year’s numbers rather than the policy: how many students placed on the first round, and how many had to extend.

The University of Redlands must have a memorandum of understanding (MOU) with the practicum site before you can begin. Please review our Active Partnership List to determine whether a partnership is in place with your potential site. the school’s own page →
StartsFinal year — "Candidates are expected to start practicum in their final year of the program (2nd Fall term for 2-year track; 3rd Fall for 3-year track)." Three consecutive semesters, CMHC 699A/B/C.
Hours required450 total practicum hours (direct + indirect). Of those, 300 must be direct client contact for Fall 2025 starts (280 for Fall 2024 starts). MFT specialization adds a relational requirement — and the two published figures disagree: the university catalog says "200 additional face to face hours working with couples and families," while the 2025–2026 Practicum Handbook's hour table says "A minimum of 40 of the direct client contact hours must be relational hours, but up to 500 relational hours can count toward MFT state licensure." Ask the program which governs before you enrol.
In-house clinicNo in-house client-serving training clinic. The School of Education runs a Mental Health Training & Integration Center (MHTIC), but the practicum handbook describes it only as offering peer group supervision — "a collaborative space for clinical mental health candidates to meet with peers, discuss cases and challenges, navigate ethical concerns observed in the field" — of which up to 12 hours per term may count as indirect practicum hours.

Practicum is the whole of the final year and finding the site is squarely on you: "Candidates are responsible for identifying and confirming their own practicum site and Site Supervisor for each semester enrolled in practicum." The program does scaffold the search — it hosts a Practicum Fair each spring so students can "meet supervisors and staff from many of our partner training sites," and it publishes an Active Partnership List of vetted sites — but if you want a site not already on that list you must file a Partnership Interest Form "at least 3 months before the anticipated start of the practicum placement," because every partnership needs a Memorandum of Understanding "often subject to legal review," and all new partnerships must be in place before Fall self-registration. The eligibility rules are restrictive and worth knowing early: the site "may not be a private practice setting," "may not be a for-profit practice setting," must deliver California-based services, must have a qualified supervisor working on-site, and must let you audio- or video-record your work both for site supervision and for use in the CMHC 699 seminar. Practicum at your own workplace is "very unusual and is discouraged" and requires a formal departmental appeal. The commitment is "an average of 15–20 hours per week, inclusive of supervision time," and supervision runs at BBS ratio — "One hour of supervision must be provided for every 5 hours of client contact," where an hour means one hour individual face-to-face or two hours in a group of no more than eight, in segments of no less than one continuous hour — carried by a site supervisor who has held an appropriate California licence for at least two years, plus a university CMHC 699 instructor who provides "group and individual supervision" and evaluates you alongside the site. Before you can start you need a Practicum Eligibility Form approved and professional liability insurance at $1,000,000 per incident / $3,000,000 aggregate. Hours are logged in Etrieve and must be site-supervisor-approved each term to pass the class; if the 450 hours are not done in three semesters you register for CMHC 699D and keep going. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • Redlands publishes no public course descriptions. The catalog runs on Coursedog and its course records are behind an authenticated API; the ‘Requirements’ tab of the MA.CMHC program page returns no course list to a plain fetch, and the Armacost Library research guides for CMHC courses carry only titles. Everything in this file about individual courses comes from the pathway grids and handbooks, which give code, title, units and term but no content.
  • The relational-hour requirement for the MFT specialization is stated two incompatible ways in two current official documents: "200 additional face to face hours working with couples and families" (university catalog and both pathway PDFs) versus "A minimum of 40 of the direct client contact hours must be relational hours" (2025–2026 CMHC Practicum Handbook). This is the single most important unresolved fact for an MFT applicant and it should be confirmed with the program in writing.
  • Whether the MFT specialization can be declared after matriculation is not documented. Mechanically the six extra units fall in the second spring and second summer and the 2023 handbook says they are delivered online, which suggests late entry is possible — but the relational client hours have to be accrued during the Year-2 practicum, which begins the preceding fall, so the practical deadline is before practicum starts. No published policy states this.
  • Which campuses offer CMHC. The university currently lists four locations — Redlands (main), Los Angeles/Woodbury, San Francisco Bay Area/San Anselmo, and a Riverside regional campus at 3610 Central Ave described as "Home to the School of Education graduate programs." The CMHC program page gives only the Redlands campus address (University Hall North, 1200 E. Colton Ave.) and lists format as On Campus. Whether the CMHC cohort also runs at Riverside could not be confirmed from any published source. Older Redlands regional campuses (Rancho Cucamonga, San Diego, Temecula, South Coast Metro) no longer appear on the university's campus-locations page at all.
  • Class meeting days and times, and whether the program is evening-scheduled for working students — not published.
  • Cohort size and admit numbers — not published.
  • CACREP status. No CACREP accreditation is claimed anywhere in the catalog, the program page or the handbooks; two faculty hold CACREP-accredited doctorates, which is a different thing. Treat the program as non-CACREP unless the program says otherwise.
  • No credible student discussion of this program was found on Reddit, Student Doctor Network or GradCafe.
  • The Fall 2026 pathway relabels CMHC 632 as ‘Adv Human Development’ while the handbooks call it ‘Advanced Child & Adolescent Development and Intervention’; only one course number exists, so this is a labelling inconsistency rather than two courses.
