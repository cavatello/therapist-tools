What this programme actually is

OrientationSystemic family therapy taught in a school of education, with an explicit critical / social-justice frame

The single most useful fact for anyone choosing between USC's two MFT options: they are the same degree, the same 60 units, the same twenty courses in the same order, and the same tuition rate. USC Rossier publishes one course schedule covering both, and the only published differences are meeting times (on campus, Mondays–Wednesdays 4:00–6:45pm or 7:00–9:45pm; online, the same days at 1:00–3:30pm or 4:00–6:30pm), mandatory fees ($977 on campus versus $31 online, because online students do not pay the health-center and transportation fees), one extra application component for online applicants (a recorded video response), and application deadlines four days apart. Tuition is $2,539 per unit for both in 2026–27 — 60 units is $152,340 before fees or living costs. Both tracks also require that you already live in California or plan to move there before you start; there is no out-of-state online option.

The online program is also newer, and more USC-run, than its size suggests. USC and 2U announced in November 2023 that they were winding down a partnership that covered Rossier's online programs, and Rossier announced its own online MFT in July 2024, with a part-time master lecturer, Linda Hoffman, leading a team whose stated job was "faithfully translating our comprehensive on-ground curriculum to an accessible and engaging online format." So this is not a legacy OPM-branded program; it is Rossier's in-house translation of the campus degree, marketed hard but built after the 2U era. Enrollment is also cyclical rather than continuous: as of August 2026 the program page states that applications "will reopen August 1 2026 for a July 2027 start." I found no teach-out, closure or suspension announcement of any kind.

What you actually study is an MFT degree taught inside a school of education, and it reads that way. Every course carries an EDUC prefix; there is no in-house family-therapy clinic; and the program's distinctive commitment is a critical, power-and-privilege frame stated up front rather than tucked into a diversity elective. The very first summer includes EDUC 508 Interrogating Systems of Inequity in Professional Practice, and the final term ends with a capstone leadership project framed explicitly around "approaches that are socially just and attuned to power and privilege." Against that, be clear-eyed about price: College Scorecard reports median federal graduate loan debt of $130,999 for USC master's completers in the mental-health-services field of study, against median earnings of $61,960 one year out. And in December 2022 three former students sued USC and 2U in a class action alleging Rossier submitted "cherry-picked" data that lifted its U.S. News rank from No. 38 to No. 10; Rossier withdrew from the rankings. Neither fact is about the MFT curriculum, but both are about the institution you would be borrowing against.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California programme teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

EDUC 5082 units

Interrogating Systems of Inequity in Professional Practice

"Establish a conceptual framework for a critical social justice perspective within the mental health field. Grapple with how to be an agent of social change as a clinician and global citizen."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis runs in the very first summer term, alongside the introductory counseling and ethics courses and before any family-systems theory. Most California programs put their equity content in a single mid-program multicultural course; USC front-loads it as a framing course and then also requires EDUC 638 Multicultural Counseling later. If a critical-theory lens is not how you want to be taught, you should know that before you apply, not after.

EDUC 6462 units

Marriage and Family Therapy Capstone: Leadership Project

"As the culminating academic, professional and reflective experience in the final year, engage in a research project to identify a gap in clinical practice and ascertain your role as change agent in addressing clinical problems with approaches that are socially just and attuned to power and privilege in the field of Marriage and Family Therapy and the global community."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThere is no thesis and no comprehensive exam here — the degree ends with a leadership/change project instead. That is a meaningful difference from programs like Alliant, which gate graduation on a comprehensive exam.

EDUC 5153 units

Theories of Marriage and Family Therapy

"Gain an introduction to major family systems theories and their unique assumptions, goals and techniques. Learn how to assess relationship dynamics and conceptualize cases with sociocultural attunement."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingWorth noticing where this sits: the actual family-systems theory course arrives in the second semester, after a full fall of generic counseling theory, psychopathology, research methods and lifespan development. This is a counseling-shaped curriculum with a systemic core inside it, not a systemic curriculum from day one.

EDUC 6363 units

Perspectives on Human Sexuality

"Learn about the physiological-psychological and socio-cultural variables associated with sexual identity and sexual behavior with an emphasis on increasing mastery in addressing sexual topics within a brief therapy context."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA full 3-unit sexuality course, where several California programs meet the same requirement in one or two units (Pepperdine's PSY 628 is a single unit; Alliant's sex therapy course is two). The 'brief therapy context' phrasing signals it is trained as a clinical skill rather than surveyed as content.

EDUC 645A / 645B3 + 3 units

Practicum II

645A: "Conduct supervised field experience in a clinical setting that focuses on the development of novice therapists in a small group setting." 645B: "...includes case presentations and development of critical thinking and holistic conceptualizations."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingPracticum is only 8 of the 60 units and does not start until the Year 2 summer term — you complete 32 units of coursework before you see a client. That is later than many California programs and it compresses your trainee hours into the last twelve months of the degree.

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a programme can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your programme approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a programme controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the licence requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a programme’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programmes on this list.

What happens after the degree →

The school does not say

Nothing on this school’s published pages states who secures your placement. That is not the same as a bad answer - but it is the first question to ask admissions, and the specificity of the reply tells you a great deal. Thirty of the seventy-eight are in this position.

Conduct supervised field experience in a clinical setting that focuses on the development of novice therapists in a small group setting. the school’s own page →
StartsYear 2, summer term (EDUC 644), then fall and spring (EDUC 645A, 645B) — three terms, 8 units total, after 32 units of coursework
Hours requiredUSC's four-way practicum agreement specifies 17–20 hours per week at the site, of which 7–10 are direct client contact, plus supervision at set ratios. The 3,000 supervised hours USC's program page cites is the California BBS licensure total, most of which is accrued after graduation as an AMFT, not during the degree.
In-house clinicNone in-house. USC's own Kortschak Center for Learning and Creativity runs a fieldwork placement reserved for Rossier MFT students — academic-coaching and psychoeducational counseling with 8–10 undergraduates per semester, 20 hours a week across summer/fall/spring, with 3 hours of weekly supervision and a $3,000 annual stipend.

Placement runs on a four-way agreement between the program, the site, the clinical supervisor and the trainee. Under that agreement the university "shall approve the placement of each trainee," ensures the placement fits the scope of MFT practice, and assigns a Clinical Coordinator as liaison to each site; the site must supply office space, an adequate client census, orientation and an emergency response plan; the supervisor must have held a California LMFT, LCSW, psychologist or psychiatrist license for at least two years. The trainee is responsible for liability insurance, weekly signed hour logs, and completing prerequisite courses. I could not find a current public practicum handbook stating whether students must source their own sites from an approved list or are assigned. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enrol, not after.

How its graduates did on the Board’s exam

96%passed first time244 of 253 candidates
80%statewide, same window14,096 of 17,698 candidates

Counting every attempt rather than only first attempts, the figure is 96% across 271 sittings here, against 66% statewide. First attempts are the fairer comparison — counting resits measures how many tries people needed rather than how many passed, and it penalises a school whose graduates keep going.

On the separate California Law and Ethics exam, 89% of this school’s 272 first-time candidates passed, against 80% statewide.

Recorded by the Board as University of Southern California, Los Angeles. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A programme that enrols more career-changers and more students of colour will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the programme changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a programme page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programmes. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the programme places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the programme cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A programme placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No published cohort size, admit rate, or entering class profile for either the on-campus or online track.
  • Could not confirm that online sections are taught by the same instructors as on-campus sections. USC confirms the same degree, same 60-unit curriculum and same tuition rate, and says the online build was led by Rossier's own faculty, but no public document states that the two modalities share a faculty roster.
  • No current practicum or field-placement handbook is public. The only detailed document I could find is a 2015 four-way trainee agreement, which is likely out of date on specifics; it does not state who is responsible for sourcing the site.
  • The USC Catalogue (catalogue.usc.edu) is behind a bot challenge and could not be fetched, so the official catalogue course descriptions and degree requirements could not be cross-checked against the program's own course-schedule page.
  • No COAMFTE accreditation. USC does not claim it in any program material, and mftcalifornia.com's accreditation roundup lists USC under WSCUC regional accreditation only. If you may want to license outside California, that matters.
  • Searched specifically for teach-out, closure, suspension or pause announcements and found none. The 'reopen August 1 2026 for a July 2027 start' notice appears to be an ordinary admissions cycle, not a wind-down — but note that it means an applicant reading in mid-2026 is applying for a start more than a year away.
  • College Scorecard reports at the 4-digit CIP level (51.15, Mental and Social Health Services), so the $130,999 median debt figure covers USC master's completers in that field, not the MFT program in isolation.
