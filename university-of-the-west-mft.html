What this program actually is

OrientationBuddhist psychology integrated with recovery-oriented, multicultural Western practice

UWest's headline claim is that it offers "the ONLY Buddhist-focused MFT curriculum in the state and the nation." Checked against the 2025–26 catalog, a narrower version of that holds. Three required courses carry Buddhism explicitly, and they sit at the three structural corners of the degree: PSYCH 505 Multicultural/Buddhist Community Mental Health is the single 3-unit Introduction course every student takes first, and it "covers core learning areas while orienting students to Buddhist wisdom in the context of Western MFT"; PSYCH 551 Theories II: Buddhist-Inspired Evidence-Based Practice is a required Core course and the second half of the theories sequence; and PSYCH 651 Buddhist Psychology & Addiction is the required Advanced substance-use course, treating addiction "through the lenses of resiliency, recovery, medical, and Buddhist psychology models." So 9 of 63 required units are named-Buddhist — although a student may substitute PSYCH 529 Drug & Alcohol Theory & Intervention for PSYCH 651 and bring that down to 6. The catalog also commits to more than course titles: graduates are to be "clinically and personally skilled in contemplative practice" and "versed in effective practices that borrow heavily from Buddhist psychology, such as Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction, Mindfulness-Based Cognitive Therapy, Acceptance and Commitment Therapy, Compassion Focused Therapy, and Dialectical Behavioral Therapy Skills Training," plus "the fundamentals of Emotionally Focused Therapy for couples" and "the applications of Interpersonal Neurobiology from the inside out." Two caveats a prospective student should hold: PSYCH 551's catalog description is a single generic sentence with no Buddhist content in it at all, and PSYCH 617 Application of Mindfulness in Western Therapy — the most obviously on-theme course in the catalog — is not in the MFT required list. Nor is PSYCH 503 Fundamentals of Buddhist Psychology, which belongs to the Buddhist Counseling Certificate.

The four specializations advertised on the program page do not appear in the catalog, and this is the single biggest discrepancy found. The website says students "customize their degree by choosing a specialization, completing 12 units within their selected focus area," and names Generalist, Family Systems & Intergenerational Studies, Mindfulness-Based Therapy, and Community Mental Health. The 2025–26 catalog's MFT program requirements table has no specialization line and no elective units at all: Introduction 3 + Foundation 18 + Core 18 + Advanced 18 + Fieldwork/Practica 6 = 63, every course named. The only thing in the catalog resembling a specialization is one list headed "Multicultural Generalist Specialization Courses" — PSYCH 537 Motivational Interviewing, 602 Advanced Couples Counseling, 604 Advanced Child & Adolescent Interventions, 614 Religion & Psychotherapy, 650 Directed Reading, five 3-unit courses — which sits outside the 63 required units with no stated unit requirement. The marketing page is also visibly built from a business-program template, carrying blocks headed "A Global Business Perspective" and promising "practical skills in leadership, finance, and entrepreneurship" inside a therapy degree. Treat the four-specialization menu as unverified and ask admissions to show it to you in writing before enrolling.

The institution: small, cheap, real. UWest was founded in 1990 by Hsing Yun of the Taiwanese Buddhist order Fo Guang Shan, opened as Hsi Lai University in a Hsi Lai Temple classroom with four professors and about thirty students, and bought its 10-acre Rosemead hilltop campus from Biola in 1996. It was granted WASC Senior College and University Commission accreditation on 16 February 2006; WSCUC currently lists it as "Accredited — Reflects the Commission's finding that the institution is in compliance with the Standards," most recent Commission action 30 October 2025, with an offsite review in Fall 2027 and a visit in Spring 2028. Graduate psychology tuition is $765 per unit for 2025–26, so 63 units is about $48,195, plus roughly $634 per semester in mandatory fees — genuinely inexpensive for a private California program, and the catalog notes UWest's MA in Psychology was ranked the ninth most affordable graduate psychology program in the US in 2015. But the scale is very small: federal data reports just 7 master's completions in the field (CIP 51.15) in the most recent year and 2 the year before, and the university publishes no cohort size, no licensure pass rate, no graduation rate and no placement data. There is no in-house training clinic; practicum is at outside agencies you help find.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California program teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

PSYCH 5053 units

Multicultural/Buddhist Community Mental Health (listed in the program tables as "Buddhist & Multicultural Community Mental Health")

"Introduces the MFT program, focusing on values exploration and acculturation/orientation to UWest, professional identity, and community mental health. Covers core learning areas while orienting students to Buddhist wisdom in the context of Western MFT."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the course that carries the Buddhist claim structurally: it is the entire 3-unit Introduction requirement, taken in the first term by every student, and it is the prerequisite for PSYCH 600 Group Psychotherapy and PSYCH 617. The Buddhist framing is placed at the front door rather than offered as an elective. Note the catalog itself uses two different titles for it.

PSYCH 6513 units

Buddhist Psychology & Addiction

"Examines the theories, etiology, assessment, diagnosis, and treatment of alcohol and substance use disorders and addiction through the lenses of resiliency, recovery, medical, and Buddhist psychology models, with special attention to co-occurring disorders." Prerequisite: 18 units of graduate work in Psychology.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingEvery California MFT program has to teach substance use for licensure; almost none teaches it through a Buddhist psychology frame. This is the clearest case of the Buddhist orientation reshaping a compliance requirement rather than sitting alongside it. Worth knowing that students may substitute the conventional PSYCH 529 Drug & Alcohol Theory & Intervention instead.

PSYCH 5513 units

Theories II: Buddhist-Inspired Evidence-Based Practice

"A follow-up course to PSYCH 531, designed to expand students' knowledge and experiences in counseling and helping skills." Prerequisite: PSYCH 531 Theories I. That two-line entry is the entire published catalog description.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingIncluded precisely because of the mismatch: this is the required Core course whose title carries the program's central promise, and its catalog description contains no Buddhist content whatsoever. If you are choosing UWest for the Buddhist curriculum, this is the course to ask for a syllabus for before you commit.

PSYCH 6233 units

Poverty, Dislocation, & Stigma

"Seminar on the impact of poverty, deprivation, dislocation, social stress, and stigma upon mental health and recovery, with a focus upon culturally congruent therapeutic orientations and interventions." Prerequisite: 18 units of graduate work in Psychology.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required 3-unit seminar built entirely around structural determinants of mental health — poverty, displacement, stigma — is unusual as a requirement rather than an elective, and it fits a program whose stated aim is community mental health with California's underserved populations. It pairs with PSYCH 607 Advanced Seminar in Culture & Psychotherapy in the final year.

PSYCH 505A0 units

Pre-Practicum

"This not-for-credit course meets one hour each week for a total of 15 hours. All second semester psychology students are required to enroll in this course. Provides first-year cohort continuity, support, and process during the second semester." Pass/No Pass only.catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA zero-unit weekly cohort process hour in the second semester, required of everyone, is a small but telling structural choice: it keeps a small cohort together through the term just before practicum eligibility opens. It is the only cohort-maintenance mechanism in the curriculum.

PSYCH 6173 units

Application of Mindfulness in Western Therapy

"Introduces the concept of mindfulness and its influence on Western psychotherapeutic research and practice and historical and contemporary literature on the efficacy and effectiveness of mindful-based therapeutic approaches on the process and outcome of treatment." Prerequisite: graduate standing (elsewhere listed as PSYCH 505).catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingListed here as a warning rather than a selling point: this is the course a prospective student would assume anchors a "Mindfulness-Based Therapy" specialization, and it is not in the 63-unit MFT requirement table at all. It appears in the Buddhist Chaplaincy and Buddhist Counseling Certificate course lists instead.

The whole degree, term by term

What you would actually take, in the order you would take it. Sequences change; check the current catalog before you count on any single term.

Cohort-based and fully prescribed — the 63 units are five named blocks with no elective allowance in the catalog's requirements table. A minimum grade of B is required for every Foundation, Core and Advanced course; a failed course may be repeated once only. The catalog publishes a full-time sample progression across six terms (three years), with optional summer intersessions used to keep supervised clinical hours continuous. Although 9 units counts as full-time for financial aid, "MFT students are expected to enroll in 12 units per term to complete the program following the cohort model." Up to 6 units may transfer in. A comprehensive exam, offered only in the spring semester, is required to graduate; the thesis (PSYCH 699A) is an approved alternative. Curriculum is aligned to California B&P Code §4980.37. course sequence ↗

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a program can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your program approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a program controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the license requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a program’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programs on this list.

The school does not say

Nothing on this school’s published pages states who secures your placement. That is not the same as a bad answer - but it is the first question to ask admissions, and the specificity of the reply tells you a great deal. Thirty of the seventy-eight are in this position.

Students are required to gain 6 units of fieldwork (practicum) at an approved practicum site. Upon completion of 18 units of coursework students may seek the chair's approval to pursue a practicum. All fieldwork and practica placements must be approved by the department chair and training director before training begins. the school’s own page →
StartsSecond year, fall term in the published full-time sequence. Eligibility opens after 18 units of coursework plus the department chair's approval, so it can begin earlier in principle; a zero-unit PSYCH 505A Pre-Practicum runs weekly through the second semester of year one.
Hours required6 units of fieldwork are required for graduation — PSYCH 700 Practicum (3) plus PSYCH 710 Advanced Practicum (3), which "must be taken in consecutive semesters." The catalog does not publish a minimum number of direct client contact hours, a relational-hour sub-minimum, or a supervision ratio; it defers entirely to "the pre-MFT licensure requirements, as guided by California's Board of Behavioral Sciences."

There is no in-house training clinic — every placement is at an outside agency, and "all fieldwork and practica placements must be approved by the department chair and training director before training begins." The gate is a written contract: "training sites must have a written contract with UWest indicating the commitment to abide by the supervisory conditions required for the pre-MFT licensure requirements, as guided by California's Board of Behavioral Sciences," which means an unaffiliated site you find yourself has to be brought into contract before you can start there. The classroom half is PSYCH 700, described as "clinical experience at an approved practicum site with a focus on multicultural psychotherapy theory and related interventions," with coursework on "group consultation, assessment, case conceptualization, intervention, self-of-the-therapist, and self-care," and the catalog states plainly that the "approved practicum placement must provide quality supervision & training" — the supervision itself comes from the site, not from UWest faculty. Continuity is managed by two extra registrations that do not count toward the degree: PSYCH 702 Intersession Practicum (1 unit) exists specifically to "fulfill BBS guidelines requiring that students seeing clients are enrolled in a supervised practicum with no break in excess of 90 days" over the summer, and PSYCH 712 Continuing Practicum (1–3 units, repeatable twice) covers hour collection beyond the required 6 units. The practical consequence for a prospective student is that the quality of your clinical training here depends almost entirely on the agency you land, and the total cost can exceed the 63-unit sticker if you need multiple terms of 702 and 712 to keep your hours continuous. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enroll, not after.

How its graduates did on the Board’s exam

Too few candidates to publish a rate

The Board recorded 26 first-time candidates from this school across the whole window, and this page does not print a percentage below 40. On a handful of candidates a single result moves the figure by tens of points, and a small program would be described by noise. It usually means the program is small, new, or both.

Recorded by the Board as University of the West. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A program that enrolls more career-changers and more students of color will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the program changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a program page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programs. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the program places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the program cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A program placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • The four specializations advertised on the program page (Generalist, Family Systems & Intergenerational Studies, Mindfulness-Based Therapy, Community Mental Health), each said to be 12 units, do not appear anywhere in the 2025–26 academic catalog. The catalog's MFT requirements table accounts for all 63 units in named required courses with no elective or specialization allowance, and the only specialization heading present is a single list of five "Multicultural Generalist Specialization Courses" with no stated unit requirement. This could not be reconciled from any published source.
  • No minimum direct client contact hours, relational-hour sub-minimum, or supervision ratio is published for practicum. The catalog defers to BBS requirements without itemizing them, so a prospective student cannot compare UWest's clinical floor to other programs.
  • No cohort size, acceptance rate, graduation rate, licensure exam pass rate or job placement data is published for the MFT program.
  • No total program cost is published. The $48,195 figure here is arithmetic — 63 units × the catalog's $765 per unit graduate psychology rate for 2025–26 — and excludes mandatory fees (about $634 per semester) and any non-degree-applicable practicum continuation units (PSYCH 702, PSYCH 712).
  • PSYCH 551 Theories II: Buddhist-Inspired Evidence-Based Practice has a two-line catalog description containing no Buddhist content, so what the course actually teaches could not be established from published sources.
  • PSYCH 503 Fundamentals of Buddhist Psychology and PSYCH 617 Application of Mindfulness in Western Therapy both exist in the catalog and both carry Buddhist content, but neither is in the MFT required curriculum — they belong to the Buddhist Chaplaincy and Buddhist Counseling Certificate programs. The catalog notes the Buddhist Counseling Certificate "does not meet the requirements to sit for licensure in Marriage and Family Therapy."
  • The catalog uses two different titles for the same required introductory course — "Multicultural/Buddhist Community Mental Health" in the course descriptions and sample progression, "Buddhist & Multicultural Community Mental Health" in the program requirements table.
  • Reddit was unreachable from this environment (HTTP 403), so no student comment text could be quoted. No MFT-specific student reviews were found on any platform; the only reviews located (Niche) are from undergraduates about campus facilities.
  • The program page advertises telehealth counseling skills but no telehealth course appears in the catalog curriculum.
