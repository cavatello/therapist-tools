What this program actually is

OrientationEclectic by design — a modality-by-modality survey (psychodynamic, CBT, family, couples, group, child/adolescent each as its own required course) opening from a Client-Centered skills base, with a single Christian integration course

Vanguard's M.S. in Clinical Psychology is organized as a survey of therapeutic modalities rather than around a single house theory, and the required list makes that unusually literal: separate three-unit courses in Psychodynamic Theory and Treatment (PSYG-616), Cognitive-Behavioral Theory and Treatment (PSYG-617), Family Psychotherapy (PSYG-605), Couples Psychotherapy (PSYG-716), Group Psychotherapy (PSYG-717) and Child and Adolescent Psychotherapy (PSYG-615). Skills training opens with Client-Centered therapy in PSYG-601 and there is no capstone course forcing you to pick one orientation — the exit requirement is a case presentation. If you want a program with a strong theoretical commitment, this is not it; if you want broad exposure with the four BBS-relevant relational modalities each taught as its own course, it is a clean fit. The degree is 61 semester units, rising to 66 if you add the LPCC coursework, and Vanguard's mechanism for that is unusual: rather than a separate track, three courses carry variable credit and you take the higher value (PSYG-711 at 3 instead of 2, PSYG-720 at 3 instead of 2, plus PSYG-660 Career Development at 3).

The Christian question here has a precise answer. Exactly one course, PSYG-712 Psychology, Theology and Spirituality, carries the faith content — three units of sixty-one — and it is backed by a stated program learning outcome, "Demonstrate and apply the integration of faith in the psychotherapeutic process." There is no Bible, theology or ministry requirement, no doctrinal statement in the admissions checklist, and the remaining sixty courses read as conventional clinical training. The identity is institutional and explicit — the mission statement is to equip students "to serve with excellence as Christian mental health professionals," and the department describes itself as grounded in "a Christian understanding of God, human nature, families, relationships, and God's redeeming compassion for humankind" — but as a curriculum component it is one required course.

Two structural things matter more than the branding. First, Vanguard runs its own clinic, Journeys at Vanguard, opened on the Costa Mesa campus in March 2022 on a sliding-scale, nobody-turned-away model, staffed by graduate students under licensed supervision — one of a small number of California MFT-track master's programs with an in-house community clinic. Second, and less flattering: practicum starts late. On every published track — two-year, three-year, four-year — the practicum course first appears in the summer of year two, and the graduation bar is 225 clinical hours with a minimum of 150 face-to-face client contact hours, the lowest published requirement of the Christian-institution programs in this set. Offsetting that, the personal-therapy requirement is one of the heaviest in California: 30 sessions, begun within your first semester and running concurrent with coursework.

See it and hear it

The courses that define it

Every California program teaches law and ethics, assessment and a survey of theories. These are the courses that are not on every list — the ones that tell you what this particular faculty believes therapy is. The quoted text is the catalog’s own; the note underneath each one is mine.

PSYG-6013 units

Clinical Foundations I

"Clinical and theoretical foundations of the field of clinical psychology are presented with an emphasis on Client-Centered therapy and the development of beginning level skills. Course includes supervised experience which will be used in assessing eligibility for trainee status."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThis is the program's gatekeeping course, and the last clause is the part to read twice — your performance in it determines whether you are cleared for trainee status at all. Vanguard also states that the supervised experience means practicing on Vanguard undergraduate volunteers in your first semester, which is a genuinely unusual arrangement: a live practice partner who is not a classmate and not yet a real client.

PSYG-6111-3 units

Clinical Foundations II

"This class offers continued training of basic clinical skills with an emphasis on facilitating the capacity for empathic listening, reflection, and skills necessary for building, monitoring, and maintaining a therapeutic alliance. Concurrent practicum placement is required."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA second, dedicated course on nothing but the alliance — empathic listening, reflection, maintaining the relationship — taken while you are already on placement. Most programs cover this in one skills course and move on; Vanguard splits it in two and puts the second half after you have real clients.

PSYG-7123 units

Psychology, Theology and Spirituality

"Explores the integration of psychology, theology, and spiritual formation and how it affects the mental health of the client. Consideration given to the role of the therapist and the therapeutic relationship with an emphasis on the Christian faith."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe whole of the faith requirement, in one course, three units of sixty-one, taken in the fall of year two. If you are weighing whether Vanguard's Christian identity will shape your training, this course plus the mission statement is the entire answer — nothing else in the required list is religious.

PSYG-7261.5 (taken twice, 3 total) units

Case Conceptualization

"In-depth processing of clinical casework with emphasis on case conceptualization, etiology, diagnosing both theoretically and according to the DSM, treatment planning and prognosis. Case management and use of supervision are discussed with emphasis on appropriate referrals. Concurrent field placement is required for this course."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingThe half-unit-per-term structure taken across your final fall and spring is how Vanguard keeps you in a supervised case-processing group for a full year of placement. Note the explicit content on 'use of supervision' — teaching students how to be supervised is rarely named as a learning objective in a California catalog.

PSYG-7243 units

Psychosocial Stressors and Mental Health

"Examination of the unique theoretical and clinical components related to working with minors, the elderly, and issues related to pregnancy, death and dying. Students are trained to work within the public mental health system and with the severely mentally ill. Placement within the legal system, housing and healthcare alternatives will be explored."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingA required course explicitly about the public mental-health system, severe mental illness, and the housing/legal/health infrastructure around it. Programs that train mainly for private practice do not require this. It signals that Vanguard expects a meaningful share of graduates to work in county and community settings.

PSYG-7653 units

Comprehensive Project

"This comprehensive class assists the student in the synthesis of knowledge gained over the span of the graduate program for the purpose of furthering professional identity and competency... Exit Project is completed and field placement is required concurrently with this course." The catalog specifies the exit project is "a case presentation."catalog ↗

Why it is worth noticingNo thesis and no mock licensing exam — the degree ends in a case presentation drawn from your own live caseload, which is why concurrent field placement is required. It is a lighter exit than a comprehensive exam but a more clinical one.

The whole degree, term by term

What you would actually take, in the order you would take it. Sequences change; check the current catalog before you count on any single term.

One 61-unit degree with two delivery formats that Vanguard treats as separate cohorts: on-campus courses run 16 weeks (a 3-unit course is one three-hour meeting a week), online courses run 8 weeks (a 3-unit course is one two-hour live meeting a week) except practicum-related courses, which stay 16 weeks in both. The online track is open only to California residents and carries three mandatory on-campus weekends — Orientation Weekend, the Current Topics course in the first summer, and Group Psychotherapy over an extended weekend in the final spring. "On campus and Online tracks are separate and students on each track are guaranteed priority registration in their format"; switching needs program permission and space. Both formats lead to the same degree and the same BBS statement, so both are CA MFT-eligible. Tracks of 2, 3 and 4 years exist (the 2-year is accelerated and needs department approval); the 3-year track below is the one Vanguard calls most popular. LPCC adds 5 units: PSYG-660 plus one extra unit each on PSYG-711 and PSYG-720. course sequence ↗

How practicum actually works

Californian clinical training runs in two legally distinct stages, and the difference decides what a program can and cannot do for you.

A trainee is a student. You see clients while still enrolled, at a site your program approves, supervised by someone the site provides. This is practicum, and it is the stage a program controls. An associate — registered with the Board after you graduate — is no longer a student, is usually paid, and is working toward the 3,000 hours the license requires. The Board renamed this stage from “intern” to “associate” in 2018, so any page still saying “MFT intern” is quoting something out of date.

Hours you bank as a trainee count toward the 3,000, up to a limit, which is why the size and quality of a program’s placement matters long after graduation: a student who leaves with 1,000 hours starts the associate years a third of the way through them, and one who leaves with the 225-hour minimum starts at the beginning.

Practicum is also where a degree most often stops being on schedule. Coursework runs on a calendar the school controls; placement does not. The questions that matter are therefore all about who owns the problem of finding you a site — and the answer differs enormously between programs on this list.

You apply, with the school’s help

There is an approved-site list and real support, and the seat is still yours to win. Most programs are here and most students manage it. Ask for last year’s numbers rather than the policy: how many students placed on the first round, and how many had to extend.

The program will support and assist you in securing a placement at one of our more than 60 partner sites. the school’s own page →

The school runs its own training clinic.  Journeys at Vanguard Owning a clinic and holding a seat in it are different things, so this sits beside the model above rather than replacing it.

StartsLate — the summer of year two on every published track, including the accelerated two-year one. Placement then runs continuously through the remaining fall and spring semesters, tied to PSYG-626, PSYG-726 and PSYG-727.
Hours required225 clinical hours minimum with at least 150 face-to-face client contact hours for the MFT-track degree. LPCC students need 280 face-to-face hours. No relational (couples/family) sub-minimum is published.
In-house clinicJourneys at Vanguard, an on-campus community counseling center in Costa Mesa, opened March 2022

Clinical contact starts long before practicum does. In your first semester PSYG-601 puts you in supervised practice sessions with Vanguard undergraduate volunteers, and the catalog says that experience "will be used in assessing eligibility for trainee status" — so semester one is functionally a screening for whether you are allowed to see clients at all. Actual placement begins in the summer of year two and is governed by a hard rule: "in compliance with state requirements, students must be concurrently enrolled in a practicum course (PSYG-626 Professional Skills in Clinical Practice, PSYG-726 Case Conceptualization or PSYG-727 Practicum) while gaining clinical hours," so you cannot bank hours in a gap term. Sites can be community counseling centers, college counseling centers or substance-abuse treatment programs, and one of them is the school's own — Journeys at Vanguard, opened in March 2022 on a sliding-scale model where clients pay what they can and, in the founding director's words, "no person is turned away"; graduate students staff it under licensed supervision. Placement is collaborative rather than assigned: Vanguard says "the program will support and assist you in securing a placement at one of our more than 60 partner sites," which means you apply and interview, with a curated list and staff help. The graduation bar of 225 hours / 150 face-to-face is modest, and Vanguard is candid that the payoff differs by license — MFT trainee hours "may also be applied to licensing requirements... in the state of California," while for LPCC "hours of experience gained as a student are not applied to licensing requirements," so an LPCC-bound student starts the clock at zero on graduation day. You must also be formally Advanced to Candidacy before the degree is confirmed, and that requires having begun placement and met the minimum client hours. source ↗

Will this degree travel

Not COAMFTE accredited

That is not a defect — 66 of the 78 institutions on the Board’s list are in the same position, including most of the Cal States, and their graduates license in California every year.

It becomes a problem if you leave. Several states will not accept a non-COAMFTE degree without remedial coursework, and a few will not accept it at all. If there is any chance you move, check the destination board’s rules before you enroll, not after.

How its graduates did on the Board’s exam

85%passed first time102 of 120 candidates
80%statewide, same window14,096 of 17,698 candidates

Counting every attempt rather than only first attempts, the figure is 78% across 157 sittings here, against 66% statewide. First attempts are the fairer comparison — counting resits measures how many tries people needed rather than how many passed, and it penalises a school whose graduates keep going.

On the separate California Law and Ethics exam, 88% of this school’s 145 first-time candidates passed, against 80% statewide.

Recorded by the Board as Vanguard University of Southern California. Its list is kept by institution and its names lag: a school that has since renamed appears under the name it had when the candidates sat.

What this number is, and what it is not

It describes candidates, not teaching. In 2022 the Council on Social Work Education removed licensing-exam pass rates from its accreditation standards, saying the data “may not be an equitable measure of program outcomes” — after the social-work exam board’s own analysis found first-time pass rates of 84% for white candidates and 45% for Black candidates. A program that enrolls more career-changers and more students of color will post a lower rate while teaching at least as well. The accrediting body with the most reason to want this metric examined it and stopped using it.

The denominator is not this year’s students. These are people who sat the exam during the window, often several years after graduating and sometimes after the program changed shape or owner.

It is published here because it is a real, official figure that is otherwise almost impossible to find — and it is published on this page only. There is no pass-rate column in the directory, nothing is sorted by it, and no school on this site is ranked against another.

What to ask before you apply

None of these are answered on a program page, and all five change what the degree is actually worth to you.

Where do students actually get placed, and who finds the placement?

The single question that separates programs. Ask for a list of current sites, and ask plainly whether the program places you or expects you to find your own. A rural or online student unable to find a site cannot graduate.

How many of your graduates passed the clinical exam last year?

The Board publishes this by school, so the program cannot be the only source. Ask anyway, and see whether the answer matches.

What is the total cost, including fees, in the year I would start?

Per-unit rates move. Multiply by the actual unit count and add campus fees before comparing anything.

How many relational hours does a typical student leave with?

The 500 couples-and-families hours are carved out of the 1,750 clinical ones, and they are the category associates most often finish short on. A program placing students in adult individual settings will not generate them.

Does this degree also make me eligible for the LPCC?

Sometimes yes, sometimes with extra units, sometimes only in one delivery format. Get it in writing rather than from a brochure.

What I could not establish

Everything above is sourced. These are the things I looked for and could not establish - take them to admissions, and note how quickly and precisely they answer.

  • No freely-licensed photograph of the Vanguard University campus exists on Wikimedia Commons — the only Vanguard files there are logos. Rather than hotlink a copyrighted university image, photo is null.
  • No independent student discussion of this program was found on Reddit, Student Doctor Network or GradCafe, so there are no genuine outside voices. Vanguard's own alumni testimonials were the only student-perspective material available and are not reproduced as if they were independent.
  • No relational (couples/family) client-hour sub-minimum is published — only the 225 total / 150 face-to-face figures.

  • Vanguard points readers to the BBS website for licensing-exam pass rates rather than publishing its own.
